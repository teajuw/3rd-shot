# One Camera Restages Pickleball, It Cannot Re-Film It

From a single broadcast camera you can build a **parametric replay**: a hand-modelled regulation court, four generic body meshes driven by recovered motion, and a ball whose flight is a fitted physics trajectory, viewable from any angle in a browser. You cannot build what the 15th National Games "Ultra-Perspective" system delivered, because that system re-renders light captured by 36 cameras, and published monocular 4D methods still sit near 16 dB PSNR at large view offsets even on synthetic single objects ([USplat4D](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12768v3)). Every layer of the parametric recipe has monocular-broadcast precedent in 2026 (TT4D for table tennis, Volley3D for volleyball), but none exists for pickleball, so each stage needs a pickleball fine-tune or adaptation. The recommended stack is court-keypoint calibration with net-height points solved once per match, a TrackNetV3-class multi-frame ball detector, per-arc physics fitting anchored at bounces and paddle contacts, GVHMR or SAM 3D Body for players with contact-aware optimisation on the known ground plane, a three.js viewer, and a DuckDB-backed tool-calling agent. Most of it is plain PyTorch, SciPy or ONNX and should run offline on the 64 GB M5 Pro, but almost no source reports measured Apple Silicon throughput, so "should run" is inference for nearly every component. On the two hypotheses: open decision models have no coordinate output and belong in a verify-the-crop role, and frontier LLMs box medium and large objects well but collapse on tiny ones and almost never say "not present". The largest unknowns are real-world 3D ball accuracy from the behind-the-baseline view (unmeasured anywhere on broadcast footage), far-side player pose quality, and local compute cost.

Throughout this report, "verified" means the research notes record the claim as read on the cited page; "search summary" means the notes took it from a search-result excerpt; "inference" means reasoning in the notes with no source behind it. Those labels are carried over as given.

## The achievable target is AO Animated, not the 36-camera National Games rig

**What the reference system actually was.** The National Games "超视角" system was built by China Media Group's 央视频 with Peng Cheng Laboratory and Peking University Shenzhen Graduate School. It used **36 custom cameras** ringing the venue, offered continuous 360-degree drag in the 央视频 app for basketball (Dongguan) and badminton (Shenzhen), and quoted a 0.1 s view-switch response ([Tencent News / 央视频, 2025-11-08](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20251108A052GL00); [CGTN, 2025-11-20](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2025-11-20/360-degree-live-streaming-tech-enhances-National-Games-experience-1IsFXdXo6Yg/p.html); [ScienceNet, 2025-11-24](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/11/555757.shtm)). No source states the reconstruction algorithm, codec, resolution or end-to-end latency, and every source is CMG, a partner lab or an outlet repeating them. The notes infer, without confirmation, that it is multi-view video plus geometry with small-baseline view synthesis rather than Gaussian splatting. Comparable real-pixel systems use similar or larger arrays: Intel True View used roughly 38 5K cameras ([SVG, 2020-11-16](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2020/11/16/svg-tech-insight-immersive-media-experiences-with-intel-true-view-delivers-new-reality-for-sports/)) and Canon's system used 125 at the 2019 Rugby World Cup ([SVG, 2019-10-25](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2019/10/25/live-from-the-rugby-world-cup-canon-free-viewpoint-system-offers-near-realtime-volumetric-capture/)).

**Why one camera cannot do that.** USplat4D (arXiv October 2025, ICLR 2026) reports novel-view PSNR of roughly **16 dB** for Shape of Motion and MoSca at 0 to 180 degree offsets on a synthetic six-object benchmark and improves on them by under 1 dB; the notes warn these table values came through a lossy extraction and should be checked against the original ([USplat4D](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12768v3)). A February 2026 paper restates that monocular 4D reconstruction is "fundamentally ill-posed" ([arXiv 2602.13806](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13806)). FreeOrbit4D (arXiv 2026-01-26, v2 2026-05-19, SIGGRAPH 2026) says diffusion-based camera redirection "often break[s] down under large-angle viewpoint changes" ([arXiv 2601.18993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18993)). A fixed elevated baseline camera makes this worse: zero parallax, far players seen only from the front and near players only from behind. The notes found no Gaussian-splatting paper that produces a monocular broadcast sports replay with real-player appearance and no quantitative test of any of these methods on broadcast sports; the 2026 papers in this family were assessed from abstracts only. These codebases are also CUDA-first (inference in the notes; VRAM and runtimes were not verified).

**What one camera can do.** The other family of broadcast products renders tracking data in a game engine. Tennis Australia's AO Animated drives cartoon avatars from 12 cameras, 29 skeletal points per player at 50 fps plus ball tracking, and ESPN carried it for the 2026 semifinals and finals ([SVG, 2025-01-24](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2025/01/24/tennis-australia-innovates-at-ao-with-ao-animated-as-it-renews-tech-deal-with-emg-gravity-media/); [SVG, 2026-01-29](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/01/29/espn-debuts-tennis-australias-ao-animated-altcasts-for-semis-and-finals/)). Sony's Beyond Sports does the same for ESPN's animated altcasts, and its CTO noted that raw ankle and toe points produce unnatural movement, so an "enhancement system" cleans motion per sport ([Hawk-Eye](https://hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4216051/defining-the-sports-experiences-of-tomorrow-datadriven-virtual-recreations)). That is direct support for prioritising smooth, physically grounded motion: even 12-camera skeletal data needs a clean-up layer before it looks natural.

The monocular research version of this design now exists at scale. **TT4D** (arXiv 2026-05-02, v2 2026-09-09) reconstructs 140+ hours of singles and doubles table tennis from broadcast video with calibration, 3D ball, spin, shot segmentation and human meshes ([arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01234)). **Volley3D** (arXiv 2026-10-28) does physics-based full-rally reconstruction from volleyball broadcasts ([arXiv 2610.38358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38358)). Vid2Player3D (SIGGRAPH 2023) did it for tennis players ([nv-tlabs/vid2player3d](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)). None attempts photoreal novel views. The honest framing: positions, timing, shot shapes and footwork patterns can be recognisably right; faces, exact limb poses, paddle angles and ball spin will be approximations. The one optional route to a "real player look" is a single-image animatable avatar (LHM, ICCV 2025, [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Qiu_LHM_Large_Animatable_Human_Reconstruction_Model_for_Single_Image_to_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)) driven by the recovered motion; the notes rate it a cosmetic upgrade with an invented back and face, and its hardware needs were not verified.

### Broadcast parsing: borrow tennis and table-tennis cues, since nothing exists for pickleball

No pickleball-specific rally segmentation, replay detection or scoreboard OCR work was found. For shot boundaries, NVIDIA's Cosmos paper found TransNetV2 and AutoShot comparable, with TransNetV2 at F1 0.967 on BBC ([Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03575)). OmniShotCut (arXiv April 2026, NeurIPS 2026 poster) adds transition-type labels that suit logo-wipe replays, but its code is unverified and its benchmark figures come from an aggregator ([arXiv 2604.24762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24762)). For rallies, TennisExpert (arXiv 2026-03-17) detects racquet impact sounds with an audio-only network, keeps clips that show the standard court view and a scoreboard, and reads the scoreboard with a prompt-guided MLLM; it reports no accuracy for either step ([TennisExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13397)). Table-tennis datasets cut rallies at scoreboard changes ([ViSTec](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15952)), as does TT4D.

The notes' proposed stack is inference, not validated on pickleball: detect shots, then use the court calibrator itself as the main-camera classifier (accept a shot only if the solved camera matches the match's main-camera pose with low reprojection error), filter replays by scoreboard presence and transition cues, find rally bounds from paddle-hit audio onsets plus ball presence, and read the scoreboard once per rally with Apple Vision OCR or a local VLM. TransNetV2's PyTorch weights and its MPS behaviour are from prior knowledge, not checked. A December 2025 table-tennis paper using YOLOv8 plus SAM for scoreboards was seen only in a search summary with no primary URL.

### Calibration: from four regressed corners to a full camera solved once

The dominant recipe remains learned keypoint and line heatmaps, a closed-form camera, then non-linear refinement, and all of the strong implementations are soccer-trained; the notes found no foundation model for court registration ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)). The directly relevant evidence is **CourtSI** (arXiv 2026-03-10): four court corners plus two net-height points, solved jointly for intrinsics and extrinsics, give about **2% focal-length error** against chessboard ground truth, beat WildCamera and Depth Anything V3, and were applied to pickleball in the CourtSI-Ext set ([CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)). Its keypoints are manual, and the notes did not confirm that the pickleball images and calibrations are downloadable. PnLCalib supplies the solver pattern (point plus point-to-line refinement; lens-distortion optimisation added to the repo on 2026-03-10) under GPL-2.0 code and CC BY 4.0 weights, but hard-codes a soccer field ([PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)). TennisCourtDetector shows what a racket-sport heatmap detector achieves: median error 2.83 px, falling to 1.83 px after line-intersection refinement ([TennisCourtDetector](https://github.com/yastrebksv/TennisCourtDetector)).

For smoothness, the literature says to stop estimating a free camera per frame. BroadTrack fixes the tripod position and tracks only pan, tilt and zoom; it reports 5.02 px mean reprojection error against 10.28 px for per-frame NBJW, whose camera centre wanders up to about 20 m within a sequence, and removing its single radial distortion term is the largest ablation loss ([BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)). CourtSI simply reuses one calibration for static shots. The notes' recommendation (inference) follows: pool keypoint and line observations over hundreds of frames, solve one (f, k1, R, t) per static shot, share the camera centre and k1 across the match, and fall back to a spline-parameterised pan/tilt/log-f bundle adjustment only for shots that fail a staticness test. Geometry foundation models (DA3, VGGT, MapAnything, MoGe-2) are auxiliaries at most; a static camera gives them no parallax, and CourtSI found DA3-propagated camera parameters drifted.

Pickleball keypoint data is thin. Community Roboflow sets hold a few hundred to about 1,135 images, and a forum thread reports YOLO keypoint models failing on occluded points ([Roboflow forum](https://discuss.roboflow.com/t/pickleball-court-keypoints/10685)); the Roboflow pages returned HTTP 403, so counts and licences are from snippets. The notes' bootstrapping idea (inference) is to click about 14 points plus net-post tops on one frame per match, solve the camera, and reproject the template to auto-label every main-camera frame.

**How this supersedes PickleNet and TennisNet.** The Nov 2025 prototypes regressed 4 corners per frame with a ResNet50, used an unsmoothed ground-plane homography, and projected the airborne ball through it. Each choice is replaced. Direct coordinate regression of 4 points gives no redundancy, no outlier rejection and no focal or distortion estimate, and the notes judge it the weakest available design (inference), though a November 2025 tennis paper shows the same design is still common ([arXiv 2511.04126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04126)). A homography is only valid for points on the court plane, so projecting an airborne ball through it places the ball at the wrong ground location by construction; a full projection matrix with net-height constraints plus a physics fit recovers height instead ([Magera et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09807v1)). Per-frame independent solves are replaced by one solve per match, which is smooth by construction.

### Ball 2D tracking: a multi-frame heatmap model, fine-tuned

The practical state of the art is still the heatmap-over-stacked-frames family. RacketVision (arXiv November 2025, v3 2026-01-28, AAAI 2026) benchmarked at 1080p: single-frame YOLO11 had 17.5 to 30.6 px mean distance error and 0.42 to 0.60 recall, while multi-sport **TrackNetV3 with a background-median input reached 1.5 to 3.4 px**, and the background median roughly halved error ([RacketVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)). Its code, dataset and weights were released in April 2026 under MIT ([OrcustD/RacketVision](https://github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision)). The original TrackNetV3 and WASB are also MIT with weights ([TrackNetV3](https://github.com/qaz812345/TrackNetV3); [WASB](https://github.com/nttcom/WASB-SBDT)). BlurBall (arXiv September 2025, v2 2026-02-15) shows that labelling the ball at the midpoint of its blur streak improves every detector and yields streak angle and length as extra observations ([BlurBall](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18387v2)). TOTNet (arXiv 2025-08-13) targets occlusion but still scores 0.33 accuracy on fully occluded tennis frames, and its text and table disagree on that figure ([TOTNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09650v1)). Whether BlurBall or TOTNet weights are downloadable, and whether TrackNetV4 has any official code, was not confirmed.

Nothing peer-reviewed covers pickleball. A University of Florida student fine-tune of TrackNetV2 on one PPA match reported 94.07% accuracy but overfit to that match ([Hudson Griffith](https://hudsondev.pages.dev/pickleball)), and one repo offers TrackNetV2 pickleball weights with no licence or metrics ([TrackNet-Pickleball](https://github.com/AndrewDettor/TrackNet-Pickleball)). The perforated ball's appearance is untested in the literature. The recommendation (inference) is to fine-tune RacketVision's TrackNetV3 or WASB tennis weights on a few thousand labelled frames from several venues, keep a second detector as an agreement filter as Kienzle et al. do within 20 px ([arXiv 2511.20250](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20250v1)), and adopt mid-streak labelling.

### 3D ball trajectory: fit physics per arc and anchor the ends

Two families exist. Per-arc physics optimisation fits initial position and velocity of an ODE by reprojection error: MonoTrack uses gravity plus quadratic drag with a free drag coefficient and penalties tying arc ends to the hitter and receiver, cutting synthetic 3D error from 14.9 cm to 8.0 cm ([MonoTrack](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.01899)). TT3D adds Magnus force and a friction bounce and solves with IPOPT via CasADi ([TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)). Volley3D fixes segment endpoints to reconstructed 3D hand positions and reports **0.27 m RMSE against 1.56 m for a MonoTrack-style fit**, while its 2D reprojection error is higher (about 17 px against 3.9 px); fitting pixels tightly did not give the best 3D ([Volley3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.38358)). Learned lifters trained on synthetic trajectories form the second family: Where Is The Ball (code "coming soon" as of 2026-11-11, fails on volleys near the net; [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05763v1)), Uplifting Table Tennis (WACV 2026; [arXiv 2511.20250](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20250v1)) and TT4D's lift-then-segment design. No released lifter weights apply to pickleball.

The notes recommend (inference) the hit-to-hit physics fit, because pickleball has many volleys with no bounce and the bounce-per-arc assumption of TT3D does not hold. Anchors are bounce points (ray intersected with the court plane at ball-radius height), 3D wrist or paddle position at contact, and shared endpoints between consecutive arcs. Render from the fitted arcs, not from per-frame lifted points. The notes also suggest net clearance as an inequality constraint and candidate-segmentation scoring by total fit residual; both are the researcher's suggestions, not from a cited paper. The October 2026 badminton study supports the net idea indirectly: constraining a trajectory to cross the net plane cut median speed error from 13.3% to 8.6% ([arXiv 2611.00031](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00031)).

Event segmentation feeds the fit and is fragile. MonoTrack's authors estimate about 20% of shots may be wrong at about 90% hit-detection accuracy. Useful cues are track kinks, wrist proximity (Volley3D picks the acting player with 85.3% / 83.3% accuracy) and audio: a Tübingen table-tennis pipeline localises bounce onsets to 0.09 ms and classifies the surface at F1 0.97, but in a controlled recording ([arXiv 2409.11760](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11760v2)). No pickleball hit or bounce detector with reported accuracy exists, and whether audio onsets survive broadcast compression and sync drift was not verified.

Aerodynamic priors are sparse. Free-flight measurements give drag coefficient about **0.33 for the outdoor 40-hole ball** and 0.45 for the indoor ball at 5 to 13 m/s ([Lindsey, TWU, 2025-05-12](https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/pickleball/pickleball_aerodynamics.php)), and 0.30 ± 0.02 from six trajectories in Steyn et al. ([arXiv 2501.00163](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00163v1)). The two sources differ several-fold on lift, and Steyn et al. contains an internal inconsistency on it. The equipment standard implies a coefficient of restitution of 0.62 to 0.66 on granite ([Equipment Standards Manual, mirror](https://www.pickleball.ph/uploads/1/2/5/9/125919777/-_official_equipment_standards_manual.pdf)). The notes calculate a terminal velocity of about 16 to 17 m/s, so drag exceeds gravity on hard drives and cannot be omitted; that is the researcher's arithmetic. Treat drag as a bounded fitted parameter, Magnus as one bounded scalar per arc or omit it at first, and fit restitution from observed bounces. Spin is inferred from curvature, never observed.

### Player motion: the known court removes most of the hard problem

The best published offline world-frame numbers are from DuoMo (Meta, arXiv 2026-03-03), whose own comparison table on static-camera RICH lists world MPJPE of 80.8 mm for DuoMo, 118.6 for GENMO and **126.3 for GVHMR, with foot sliding of 3.1, 6.7 and 3.0 mm** ([DuoMo](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03265)). DuoMo's code release and venue are unconfirmed and it does not address multi-person input. GVHMR has a documented static-camera flag (`-s`) ([zju3dv/GVHMR](https://github.com/zju3dv/GVHMR)), and community ports report MPS output matching CPU to about 1.1 mm (search summary; [atelier PR #199](https://github.com/fontanierh/atelier/pull/199)). Its licence was not verified and it outputs SMPL, whose licence is non-commercial and forbids redistribution ([SMPL licence](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)). GEM-X (NVIDIA, latest README news 2026-10-21) is the commercially cleaner temporal option: Apache-2.0 code, SOMA body model, calibrated-camera adapters and a static camera mode, with weaker self-reported numbers and no MPS mention ([NVlabs/GEM-X](https://github.com/NVlabs/GEM-X)). Apple's CoMotion is the only model found with an official Core ML artifact, for its detection stage ([apple/ml-comotion](https://github.com/apple/ml-comotion)).

The closest benchmark to this use case is CalTennis (arXiv 2026-06-18), whose abstract concludes that "3D joint angle recovery is now fairly accurate, but all models struggle with depth and foot contact" ([CalTennis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20542)). The notes infer that court calibration fixes exactly those two weaknesses: place each player by intersecting foot rays with the court plane and fix body scale per player. Only the abstract was read; per-method rankings are unknown.

For clean-up, the evidence favours optimisation over learned physics trackers. PhysHMR (arXiv 2025-10-02, SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) shows that passing GVHMR output through a PHC+ tracker cuts foot-height variance about threefold but **more than doubles foot sliding (5.65 to 12.71 mm)** and worsens joint error ([PhysHMR](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02566v1)). These trackers target Isaac Gym, which the notes state is Linux and NVIDIA only. Vid2Player3D solved its root trajectory with foot-contact detection and court calibration before any physics, and the December 2025 SAM 3D Body world-coordinate paper gives an optimisation-only recipe: lock shape and scale, smooth in a sliding window, apply a soft foot-ground contact model, then optimise the global root ([arXiv 2512.21573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21573); no code link, no metrics in the abstract). The notes' synthesised stack (inference): fix shape per player for the match, detect foot contacts, run a per-rally trajectory optimisation with reprojection, pose-prior, jerk, ground-contact and joint-limit terms, pin stance feet with IK, and infill occluded spans. MuJoCo on CPU is the dependable later option for physics; Newton and Warp are CPU-only on macOS ([Newton install guide](https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/1.0.0/guide/installation.html)), and training a tracking controller locally is not realistic.

Identity tracking is simple here (inference): at most four players, static camera, net as a hard separator, association in court coordinates. Paddles have no off-the-shelf monocular 6-DoF estimator; attach a rigid paddle to the hand with a fixed grip transform, and treat a keypoint-plus-PnP refinement (RacketVision's 2D racket keypoints as pretraining) as later work. Far-side hands are a few pixels, so hand pose there will be mostly prior.

### Viewer: bake to GLB and orbit in the browser

The notes recommend a three.js or React Three Fiber viewer playing glTF skinned animation, with viser (which ships an SMPL skinned-mesh example; [viser](https://viser.studio/main/examples/demos/smpl_skinned/)) and Rerun as development viewers. The three.js capabilities are from prior knowledge, and no off-the-shelf viewer for SMPL or MHR sequences was found. Use an Apache-2.0 body. **Meta MHR** is verified Apache 2.0 on its repo, ships FBX rigs and an SMPL conversion tool, and has no glTF exporter, so a one-time Blender conversion script is needed ([facebookresearch/MHR](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MHR)). NAVER's Anny is also Apache 2.0 with a Mixamo-compatible rig option ([arXiv 2511.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03589v2)). Model the court by hand from rulebook dimensions; exact geometry is needed for calibration and bounce physics anyway. The ball needs no physics engine at playback: bake the fitted trajectory into the GLB.

### Analytics and agent layer: tools over a semantic layer, never frames in context

Mature racket-sport datasets share a backbone of match, game, rally and stroke with a separate frame-indexed tracking layer ([ShuttleSet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04948)). Pickleball has one public relational schema, pklmart (tournament, match, game, rally, shot, shot_type_ref, player, team; [pklshop docs](https://nolansmyth.github.io/pklshop/index.html)), and one commercial JSON schema from PB Vision ([partner docs](https://pbv-public.github.io/partner-sdk-nodejs/)). The notes propose (their design) three strictly separated layers: frame-indexed primitives with per-sample uncertainty, atomic events (contact, bounce, net crossing, rally end) with confidences, and a semantic rally and shot layer that stores shot index and kinematic class separately so "third-shot drop" is a query. The full pklmart column dictionary and the PB Vision schema pages were not opened.

For the agent, published systems that work put the LLM in front of structured data through tools. SportSQL shows the schema-only text-to-SQL pattern ([SportSQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17157v1)). SportsMetrics is the canonical evidence that in-context aggregation over event logs is unreliable ([arXiv 2402.10979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10979)). COACH (arXiv 2025-12-01) beat Gemini 2.5 Pro on badminton rally QA with a small backbone and structured stroke grounding: 85.60 against 24.20 exact match on action classification ([COACH](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01853v1)). The 2026 data-agent benchmarks locate failures in statistics, not coding: StatABench's best frameworks scored 61.86/100 on open-ended tasks ([StatABench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22977)) and LongDS-Bench attributes 52% to 69% of failures to long-horizon state tracking ([LongDS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30434)). Most of these figures came from search summaries; only COACH and the Muthukumarana and Swartz pickleball paper were read in full. No published system does free-form QA over raw high-frequency tracking data, and which local models fit this Mac for the agent role was not researched.

The notes' design (inference) is DuckDB over Parquet, a data dictionary as system prompt, and tools for SQL, sandboxed Python, curated metric functions, statistical tests that always return n and an interval, and clip rendering so every claim can be checked against video. Uncertainty handling matters because monocular error is the same order as the margins being judged: TT3D notes a 40 ms bounce-timing error moves the bounce up to 40 cm at 10 m/s ([TT3D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10035)). In/out and kitchen-fault claims should therefore be aggregate and probabilistic, reach envelopes should be learned from each player's own successful contacts (no published pickleball reach values exist), and resampling should be by game or match because points are not independent ([Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)). That paper states the opportunity plainly: what pickleball needs is "advanced computer vision techniques that take film as input and produce workable data".

## Decision models verify crops; they do not find balls

**Hypothesis 1: multimodal decision models can do zero-shot computer vision such as ball detection.** The evidence contradicts this for localisation and supports a narrower role. Decision models return yes/no probabilities, choices or ratings in one forward pass. LiquidAI d1-3B's question types are noul, choice and score only, with no box or point output, and its card lists no grounding benchmark ([d1-3B card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-3B)). StartLux Decision listings likewise mention no box output; no primary card was reached, so its image handling is unverified from public sources ([Featherless listing](https://featherless.ai/models/startlux-models/StartLux-Decision-v1.1-9B-Instant)). Only hosted Jev 1.14 (announced 2026-10-14) has a Box type, and it is experimental, flagged by TypeSafe as not production-ready, has no published accuracy, and always returns a rectangle even when the target is absent; these details come from a blog, since official docs were not opened ([flaviocopes.com](https://flaviocopes.com/jev.md)). The Jev Decision Index has no visual-grounding area ([aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/jev-decision-index-ranks-30-open-weight-decision-models)). No independent evaluation of any decision model on grounding, small objects or sports imagery was found.

Speed rules out brute force. The d1-3B card reports 62 ms for a 384 px image on an M5 Pro, consistent with the user's local measurements. The user's 8x8 grid at about 7 s per photo works out, by the notes' arithmetic, to roughly 210 s of compute per second of 30 fps video. Verifying three to five motion candidates per frame is feasible offline but still far slower than a small trained detector. The calibration claims were established on text-style tasks; on blurred 10 px balls they are unproven, so any threshold must be set on a held-out random sample of human-labelled pickleball crops.

**Hypothesis 2: the latest LLMs draw bounding boxes extremely well.** This holds for clear, medium-to-large objects and fails for a 5 to 15 px blurred ball. Recent models exceed 90% on RefCOCO, yet on GroundingME (arXiv 2025-12-19) the best model reaches **45.1%**, the tiny-object subset scores range from 0.7 (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to 52.0 (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B), and on object-absent rejection nearly every model scores **at or near 0%**, meaning they invent a box ([GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1)). SOUBench (April 2026) puts the best of 15 models at 58.97% on small-object questions, 23.53 points below humans ([SOUBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22884v1)). One practitioner test partly supports the user: at PyTorch Conference Europe on 2026-04-08, Gemini 3 found distant soccer balls that a fine-tuned YOLOv11, Qwen3-8B and InternVL-3.5-8B missed, at up to 2 s per image; it was qualitative, on 44 images ([slides](https://hosted-files.sched.co/pytorchconferenceeu2026/33/M_Szymkowski_PyTorch_Conference.pdf)). No benchmark measures boxes on 5 to 15 px motion-blurred objects, and no VLM result exists on any racket-sport ball dataset.

Video-native models are also not reliable referees. On TennisTV the best commercial model scored 45.1% against 89.0% for humans (via search summary; [TennisTV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15602)), and on CourtSI-Bench the best VLM scored 64.6% against 73.6% human, with fine-tuning transferring only partly to the pickleball split ([CourtSI overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2603.09896)).

**The role these models should play.** The notes' synthesis, which no source validates end to end, assigns three jobs. First, candidate verification: generate ball candidates by frame differencing inside the court region, then ask a decision model a binary question on a tight upscaled crop, then enforce trajectory consistency. Second, sparse auto-labelling: use a hosted frontier VLM on tiles for hundreds to low thousands of frames, always paired with a presence question and a temporal filter, to bootstrap labels for the TrackNet-style detector. Third, narrow typed questions on short clips or keyframes around events the geometry has already found, such as forehand or backhand, volley or groundstroke, and scoreboard reading. A cautionary data point on auto-labelling: a June 2026 soccer hobby project distilled SAM 3 plus LocateAnything-3B labels into an RF-DETR student whose ball AP@50:95 was about **0.08** against 0.89 for goals ([dcrey7 Substack, 2026-06-15](https://dcrey7.substack.com/p/world-fut-coach)). Zero-shot teachers pass their ball blindness to single-frame students, which is why the labels should train a multi-frame heatmap model at full resolution. No paper compares a zero-shot-labelled student with a human-labelled TrackNet.

## SAM 3D Body earns a place; SAM 3D Objects mostly does not

Meta announced SAM 3D on 2025-11-19 as two models alongside SAM 3 ([Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/sam-3d/)).

**SAM 3D Body fits as a per-frame pose source.** It is a single-image, promptable full-body mesh recovery model covering body, feet and hands, outputting the Momentum Human Rig, with README accuracy of 54.8 mm MPJPE on 3DPW ([sam-3d-body](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-body)). It has no temporal model and no world trajectory, and no video version or dated 2026 update was found. SAM-Body4D (arXiv 2025-12-09) notes that per-frame use is temporally inconsistent and degrades under occlusion; its training-free wrapper is MIT but requires CUDA and a long dependency chain ([arXiv 2512.08406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08406); [repo](https://github.com/gaomingqi/sam-body4d)). Its value here is licensing and hands: code and checkpoints are under the SAM License (commercial-use terms known only from a third-party summary; the licence was not read), and MHR is Apache 2.0. On Apple Silicon, issue #93 (opened 2025-12-15) reports a crash on MPS in the MHR head with a workaround of keeping MHR on CPU; an MPS fork commit is not merged upstream, and no Mac speed is given ([issue #93](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-body/issues/93)). The notes expect whole-match inference on four players to be slow and say it must be benchmarked.

**SAM 3D Objects does not fit the core pipeline.** It reconstructs objects from one image and exports a Gaussian splat; its README states no hardware or macOS information, and the notes infer it is likely CUDA-bound ([sam-3d-objects](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects)). The court must be modelled by hand, broadcast crops make poor inputs, and a generated static mesh is the wrong route to a player avatar. It is at most a prop generator, and for that the verified local option is TRELLIS.2 through the community `trellis-mac` port (about 3.5 minutes per mesh on an M4 Pro per its author; dependencies carry commercial restrictions) ([Show HN](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/47828896)). One of the two research notes recorded no primary source for SAM 3D Objects at all.

**SAM 3 fits for players and masks, not for the ball.** SAM 3 officially requires CUDA ([facebookresearch/sam3](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3)), but an MLX port of SAM 3.1 (released 2026-03-27) reports about **190 to 230 ms per frame for 3 to 5 tracked objects on an M3 Max** ([mlx-community/sam3.1-bf16](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/sam3.1-bf16)). The notes estimate roughly an hour for four players through a 10-minute clip; that is from model-card numbers, not replicated, with no M5 measurement or memory figure. The two notes describe SAM 3's Mac status differently (one cites only an open MPS PR and Triton failures, the other the MLX port), so the MLX path is the one to test. For the ball, the only detailed evidence is the same soccer hobby project: SAM 3 found the ball in **0 of 117 and 0 of 108 frames** at 1080p. Its default 1008 px input shrinks a pickleball to a few pixels. Tiling, exemplar prompts and the "paddle" prompt are all untested. Useful roles are player masks as prompts for SAM 3D Body, exclusion of officials and spectators, and auto-labelling of players.

## Real 3D accuracy on broadcast footage is unmeasured, and so is nearly all Mac performance

**Accuracy risks.** No end-to-end 3D ground-truth evaluation on broadcast footage exists in the ball papers reviewed, so real-world accuracy of any monocular method is essentially unmeasured. TT3D's simulated error is worst in the back view, which is the pickleball broadcast geometry: 22.3 cm, or 29.8 cm with noise, on a much smaller table ([TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)). The notes' expectation of "several tens of centimetres" along-court without anchors, and of a few centimetres of vertical calibration error near the net, are triangulated inferences with no pickleball measurement behind them. Kitchen-line volley exchanges are the hardest case: short arcs, no ground anchor, heavy occlusion. Lob recovery through an out-of-frame apex is untested inference. No source quantifies pose accuracy against player pixel height, and far-side shins hidden behind the net band are not discussed anywhere. "Beyond the Broadcast" (arXiv 2025-07-26) holds, per a search excerpt, that direct monocular 3D ball estimation is infeasible and uses manually annotated keyframes ([arXiv 2507.20006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20006)); TT4D and Volley3D are the counter-evidence, on other sports.

## Six experiments, ordered to fail fast

The order below follows the dependency chain and puts the cheapest decisive tests first; it is this report's sequencing of the notes' recommendations.

**First, calibrate one match by hand.** Click about 14 court points plus net-post tops and the net-tape centre on one main-camera frame, solve (f, k1, R, t), and check that the reprojected net tape lies on the image tape and that player heights are plausible. Measure how much the main camera moves across rallies. This costs an afternoon, validates the height mapping that everything else depends on, and immediately yields auto-labels for a keypoint detector.

**Second, benchmark the ball detector zero-shot, then fine-tuned.** Run RacketVision's TrackNetV3 and WASB tennis weights on a few rallies on MPS, record recall and throughput, then label a random sample of frames from several matches and fine-tune. Keep the evaluation frames a random sample, separate from any hand-picked hard cases. This is where the decision-model verifier and a hosted VLM labeller can be tested as label accelerators, with thresholds set on that same random sample.

**Third, fit one rally in 3D.** Hand-mark hits and bounces for a single rally with a mix of drives, dinks and a kitchen exchange, fit drag-ODE arcs with bounce and wrist anchors, and view the result from the side and top. Bounce-height residuals, net clearance and implied speeds give a free plausibility check. This is the project's central technical risk and needs nothing but SciPy.

**Fourth, time the player models on a 30-second clip.** Run GVHMR in static mode and SAM 3D Body (MHR head on CPU) on all four players, record seconds per frame and memory, and inspect far-side players specifically. Place players by foot-ray intersection with the court and apply the simplest contact-aware smoothing. The notes call this local benchmark the necessary next step, since no public number exists.

**Fifth, assemble the mockup viewer.** Export that one rally to GLB with the hand-modelled court, four bodies and the ball, and orbit it in three.js. A single convincing rally from the opposite baseline is the earliest honest test of whether the result is enjoyable to watch.

**Sixth, automate events and parsing, then add the agent.** Only after the manual rally works, replace hand-marked events with audio onsets, track kinks and wrist proximity under a rules-based decoder, add shot detection and scoreboard reading, and stand up DuckDB with a handful of vetted metric functions before exposing it to an LLM.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project from a rendering problem to an estimation problem with a strong prior. The court is the asset that single-camera video uniquely offers: known dimensions turn calibration, player depth, ball bounces and foot contact from learned guesses into geometric constraints, and the 2026 literature (CourtSI, CalTennis, Volley3D, PhysHMR) converges on the finding that anchoring to known geometry beats both tighter pixel fitting and heavier learned physics. That makes the user's priority, smooth and physically grounded over per-frame exact, the technically correct one and not a compromise.

Two implications follow. Pickleball is open territory: there is no peer-reviewed tracker, 3D reconstruction, event detector or tracking dataset for the sport, and the one academic review of pickleball analytics asks for exactly this pipeline, so even a modest working system produces data nobody else has published. And the role of the new model classes is supporting, not central: decision models and frontier VLMs speed up labelling, verification and narrow classification, while the load-bearing parts are a small trained heatmap network, a least-squares solver and a rulebook. The first three experiments will show within days whether the behind-the-baseline depth ambiguity is tolerable, which is the question no existing source can answer.
