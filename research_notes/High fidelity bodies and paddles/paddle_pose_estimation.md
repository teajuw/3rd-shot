# Paddle / racket 6-DoF pose estimation from single-camera broadcast video (as of November 2026)

Scope note: the setting is one fixed calibrated broadcast camera, 1080p, 50 fps, pickleball paddle about 16 x 8 in (about 0.41 x 0.20 m), near paddles 40-80 px long, far paddles 15-30 px. Research was done on 2026-11-12 with web search and page fetches. Where a fact came only from a search-result snippet and the primary page was not opened, it is marked "(snippet only)". Where a pointer comes from background knowledge and was not re-fetched in this session, it is listed under Gaps or marked "(not re-verified)".

## 1. Published work on racket/paddle pose from video (tennis, badminton, table tennis, padel, pickleball, squash, bat, club), including RacketVision

### Takeaway
No published method was found that recovers full 6-DoF racket or paddle pose from a single broadcast camera by looking at the racket, and nothing at all was found for pickleball. The state of the art is (a) RacketVision's 2D five-keypoint racket detector (tennis, badminton, table tennis; MIT-licensed data; about 5-10 px mean keypoint error in 1080p, with weak side keypoints), and (b) TT4D's indirect physics inversion of racket orientation at impact from the ball, validated at 26.4 degrees mean orientation error.

### Cited Findings

RacketVision (AAAI 2026)
- RacketVision is a benchmark covering table tennis, tennis and badminton with three tasks: ball tracking, racket pose estimation, and ball trajectory forecasting; 1,672 clips, 435,179 frames, 12,755 seconds, drawn from 942 professional broadcast games on YouTube. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- It was published at AAAI 2026 (described as an oral in a search summary); arXiv v3 is dated 28 January 2026. — [AAAI proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37362); [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Racket annotation is a bounding box plus five 2D keypoints named top, bottom, handle, left, right. Annotations are 2D pixel-level only; no 3D pose, no PnP, no 6-DoF output. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3); [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zonetom67/RacketVision)
- 24,621 racket annotations in total (table tennis 6,648; tennis 7,395; badminton 10,578) versus 64,042 ball annotations; annotators labelled 20% of frames per clip, evenly sampled; clips are 5-10 s of live play; video is 1080p (frame rate not stated in the paper text). — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Baseline racket model is top-down and single-frame: RTMDet detector plus RTMPose keypoint head. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Reported racket pose results for the multi-sport model (column mapping inferred from a garbled table header by the fetch tool, so treat exact column names with caution): table tennis PCK@0.2 81.8, mean keypoint error 9.71 px; tennis PCK@0.2 89.6, 5.34 px; badminton PCK@0.2 88.5, 5.00 px. Single-sport models are worse (table tennis 75.6 / 10.6 px; tennis 83.7 / 5.87 px; badminton 82.1 / 5.45 px). — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Multi-sport training improved PCK@0.2 by about 6 points in every sport (6.2 table tennis, 5.9 tennis, 6.4 badminton), i.e. racket appearance features transfer across sports. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Per-keypoint PCK@0.2 shows the long axis is easy and the width is hard: table tennis top 97.6, bottom 97.3, handle 97.9, left 64.8, right 64.8; tennis top 98.6, bottom 98.9, handle 92.6, left 79.7, right 80.1; badminton top 99.4, bottom 99.7, handle 97.3, left 74.6, right 75.5. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- The paper attributes the weak side keypoints to being "highly sensitive to viewing angles", "often occluded by hand grip", and subject to "motion blur during rapid movements". — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Naively concatenating racket pose features to ball history made trajectory forecasting worse than ball-only; a cross-attention fusion was needed to beat the ball-only baseline. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Dataset licence tag on Hugging Face is MIT. The release contains mp4 clips, ball CSVs, per-frame racket JSON (category, bbox_xywh, keypoints), COCO-format detection and pose annotations, and trajectory pickles; model checkpoints are in a separate Hugging Face repo (linfeng302/RacketVision-Models); code is at github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision. — [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zonetom67/RacketVision)
- A March 2026 follow-up, CourtSI-Bench, reuses RacketVision data to benchmark spatial reasoning of vision-language models; it does not address racket pose. (snippet only) — [alphaXiv 2603.09896](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09896.md)

Table tennis reconstruction pipelines
- TT4D (arXiv 2605.01234, May 2026; listed as MM '26 in a search summary) reconstructs table tennis in 4D from monocular broadcast video: about 146 hours of gameplay from 45,946 broadcast games; human meshes from 4DHumans aligned to the world frame; requires at least 25 fps. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- TT4D does not detect or track the racket in the image. It estimates racket orientation and velocity at impact as an inverse problem: optimise racket velocity and orientation so that a simulated post-impact ball trajectory (with aerodynamic flight dynamics and spin) matches the observed 3D trajectory and bounce timing, framed as a two-point boundary-value problem. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- TT4D validated this against infrared-marker motion capture on 92 strokes: mean racket orientation error 26.4 +/- 4.4 degrees; velocity error 0.58 +/- 0.40 m/s at an average impact speed of 3.72 m/s. Largest errors are in vertical velocity and in rotation about the racket's open/closed axis; residual error is attributed to unknown racket bounce properties. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- TT4D reports racket-hit time segmentation F1 of 0.83 versus 0.92 for table bounces, mainly because the ball is poorly visible near the players. Dataset licence not stated; no code URL given; dataset release promised upon acceptance. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- LATTE-MV (CVPR 2025) segments table and player rackets with a YOLOv8 segmentation model on 640x360 broadcast video, but states "Note that we do not reconstruct racket poses"; racket masks are used only for hit detection (local minima of 2D distance between ball and racket-mask centroid) and handedness; the ball's 3D position at a hit is approximated by the racket hand position from HMR 2.0. — [arXiv 2503.20936v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20936v2)
- TT3D (CVPR 2025 workshop track listing) reconstructs table tennis ball trajectories and player pose from match video and states that inferring spin "without relying on human pose estimation or racket tracking" is an advantage because those are "often unreliable or unavailable in broadcast footage". (snippet only) — [arXiv 2504.10035](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)

Tennis
- Vid2Player3D (SIGGRAPH / ACM TOG 2023) learns simulated tennis from broadcast video and explicitly treats video-derived wrist and racket motion as unreliable: "it is difficult to estimate the wrist or the racket motion due to occlusion and motion blur". Its fix is a hybrid controller in which body motion follows the video-derived reference while the wrist is directly controlled by a learned high-level policy so the simulated racket hits the ball. (snippet only for the quote) — [NVIDIA project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d); [paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- A 2026 humanoid-tennis paper reconstructs SMPL motion from broadcast clips with GVHMR, notes that GVHMR "often produces inaccurate wrist estimates due to viewpoint occlusions", and refines racket-related wrist joints per player stroke style following Vid2Player3D. (snippet only; PDF too large to fetch) — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20087)
- CalTennis (June 2026) is a tennis benchmark for monocular-to-3D human pose; authors report 3D joint angles are recovered fairly accurately but all models struggle with depth and foot contact. It covers bodies, not rackets. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2606.20542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20542)

Robot-oriented racket pose (closer cameras, not broadcast)
- "Real-time 6D Racket Pose Estimation and Classification for Table Tennis Robots" uses two cameras with a Kalman filter and reports about 7.8 mm position and 7.2 degrees orientation error against KUKA robot ground truth. This is a two-camera, close-range lab setup. (snippet only, from a library catalogue record) — [catalogue record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c799-bcb1513154564e1cc411e065952ed9080a48f60ef99c7e57384410cd363648623)
- A Chen et al. paper in IEEE Trans. Instrumentation and Measurement estimates table tennis racket pose from a high-speed monocular camera using PnP refined by orthogonal iteration, representing the racket by its face normal, to infer spin imparted by a human. A 2013 CCDC paper (Wang and Shi) segments the racket by colour and applies PnP. (snippet only) — [index record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1756210.htm)

Bat, club, stick
- A Fujitsu paper (arXiv 2604.17567, April 2026) recovers metric 3D trajectories of athlete and implement (golf club, baseball bat, hockey stick, kendo sword) from 2D keypoints, but it is multi-camera: the known implement length is used for multi-camera self-calibration and scale. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2604.17567](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17567); [project page](https://fandulu.github.io/sport_stick_multi_cam_calib/)
- The only monocular golf-club keypoint item found was a US patent describing a line-segment-based correction of club keypoints from a 2D model on mobile video; not peer reviewed. (snippet only) — [US 12711647](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12711647)

Pickleball
- Public paddle data found is limited to community Roboflow Universe detection datasets ("PBPaddle", "Pickleball Paddle Detection"); these are bounding-box detection sets, not keypoint or pose sets. (snippet only; sizes and licences not checked) — [PBPaddle](https://universe.roboflow.com/pickle-cs3pg/pbpaddle); [Pickleball Paddle Detection](https://universe.roboflow.com/pickleball-paddle-trainer/pickleball-paddle-detection)

### Inferences
- RacketVision does not include pickleball, padel or squash. Its closest analogue to a pickleball paddle is the table tennis bat (solid face, short handle), which is also its worst category (9.7 px mean error; left/right PCK 64.8). Expect zero-shot transfer to pickleball to give usable long-axis points (top/bottom/handle) and unreliable width points; fine-tuning on a few hundred to a few thousand labelled pickleball frames is the realistic route, and the 6-point multi-sport gain suggests pre-training on RacketVision helps.
- RacketVision's errors are measured on rackets that are large in frame compared with the far-side pickleball paddle. A 5-10 px mean error is 6-25% of a 40-80 px near paddle, and 17-67% of a 15-30 px far paddle. On far players, keypoints alone cannot give a useful face orientation.
- RacketVision labels only 20% of frames and the paper does not report accuracy split by blur or swing phase, so the reported numbers likely over-represent sharp, slow frames relative to the contact window that matters most for rendering.
- TT4D's 26.4 degree error is the only published ground-truth-validated number for racket orientation from monocular broadcast video. It sets a realistic expectation: physics inversion gives a plausible, not precise, face angle.
- Every broadcast-video reconstruction system found (Vid2Player3D, LATTE-MV, TT3D, TT4D, the 2026 humanoid tennis paper) avoids measuring the racket in the image and instead infers or synthesises it. That is a strong signal that direct image-based 6-DoF racket pose at broadcast resolution is not yet a solved, off-the-shelf capability.

### Gaps
- No paper found on racket/paddle pose for pickleball, padel or squash.
- RacketVision model checkpoint licence, input resolution, and the exact geometric definition of each keypoint per racket type (only shown in a figure) were not confirmed.
- TT4D Appendix F (impact model equations, restitution and friction coefficients) was beyond the fetched portion of the page.
- The two-camera 6D racket paper and the Chen et al. monocular PnP paper were seen only as catalogue snippets; authors, year and exact numbers should be checked at the primary source before quoting.
- Badminton-specific racket 3D work and MonoTrack-style systems were not searched in depth.

## 2. Keypoint-plus-PnP for a known planar object

### Takeaway
PnP on a planar target is well understood: four or more coplanar points give a pose, but planar PnP always has a two-fold "flip" ambiguity that gets worse at low resolution and near-frontal views, and the available racket keypoint detectors are weakest exactly on the width keypoints that determine face tilt. At 15-30 px it is not a viable standalone method.

### Cited Findings
- The IPPE planar solver always returns two candidate poses with their reprojection errors; the two "roughly correspond to a flip of the object about a plane whose normal passes through the line-of-sight from the camera centre to the object's centre", and "no pnp algorithm will be able to resolve it, because it is a property of the problem". Non-coplanar extra points resolve it. — [IPPE repository README](https://www.github.com/tobycollins/IPPE)
- The planar ambiguity "worsens at low resolutions and long distances, or when the plane containing the points is almost parallel to the image plane". (snippet only) — [arXiv 2312.08488](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08488v3)
- A 2026 paper notes that a pose from only the four outer corners of a symmetric planar marker is ambiguous under three rotations, and interior markings are needed to resolve it. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2601.17556](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17556)
- RacketVision uses five keypoints (top, bottom, handle, left, right); left/right PCK@0.2 is 65-80% versus 93-99% for top/bottom/handle; causes named are view sensitivity, grip occlusion and motion blur. — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- Hawk-Eye's multi-camera SkeleTRACK tracks seven points on a tennis racket. — [Hawk-Eye](https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4243365/skeletrack-a-new-era-of-data-in-tennis)
- Prior table tennis work applied PnP with orthogonal-iteration refinement to a racket seen by a high-speed monocular camera, representing pose by the face normal. (snippet only) — [index record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1756210.htm)

### Inferences
- Geometry of the setting: a 0.41 m paddle spanning 40-80 px means roughly 5-10 mm per pixel on the near side and 14-27 mm per pixel on the far side. The face is about 0.20 m wide, so its projected width is at most 20-40 px near and 7-15 px far, less when tilted.
- Face tilt from a planar outline is encoded mainly in foreshortening (projected width is roughly true width times the cosine of the tilt). Near face-on, cosine is flat, so a 1-2 px width error produces tilt errors of tens of degrees; and the sign of the tilt (which edge is nearer) is the IPPE flip ambiguity, which perspective cannot resolve for an object this small relative to its distance. So keypoint PnP yields in practice: reliable 2D position, reliable in-plane long-axis direction, a noisy magnitude of out-of-plane tilt, and an unknown tilt sign.
- The handle makes the paddle outline asymmetric along its long axis, so handle-versus-tip is observable, but front face versus back face is not (and for rendering a symmetric paddle it does not matter unless the two faces differ visually).
- Motion blur: at 50 fps a paddle head moving 10-20 m/s travels 0.2-0.4 m between frames, comparable to the paddle's own length, and smears across a large fraction of that during the exposure. Keypoints during the fast part of the swing will be absent or biased; the long-axis streak direction and mask extent are more recoverable than corners. This is an estimate from typical swing speeds, not a sourced measurement.
- A depth prior removes most of the weakness: the wrist 3D position from the body pipeline fixes the paddle's translation to within a grip offset, so the image only needs to supply two or three rotation degrees of freedom. Fitting a known paddle model anchored at the hand to 2D keypoints or a mask is far better conditioned than free PnP.
- Recommended keypoint set if training a custom model: butt of handle, handle/face junction (throat), tip centre, left and right face-edge midpoints, optionally the four face "corners". More points along the well-localised long axis do not help tilt; edge points do, but they are the hard ones.

### Gaps
- No source was found that quantifies planar PnP orientation error as a function of object pixel size and keypoint noise; the numbers above are geometric reasoning.
- No published keypoint detector trained on blurred rackets with blur-aware labels (for example, start/end-of-exposure keypoints) was found.

## 3. General 6-DoF object pose and tracking methods (FoundationPose, MegaPose, SAM-6D, BundleSDF, Any6D, newer)

### Takeaway
The general novel-object pose family is a poor fit: the leading methods assume RGB-D, hand-scale objects filling a reasonable crop, and NVIDIA CUDA GPUs, and their own documentation lists strong blur as a failure case. RGB-only derivatives exist but lose accuracy and still run on NVIDIA hardware; none reports results on thin, tiny, fast sports implements.

### Cited Findings
- FoundationPose (CVPR 2024) is a unified model-based and model-free 6D pose estimator and tracker for novel objects whose published setup uses RGB-D input. — [arXiv 2312.08344](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08344v2)
- NVIDIA's model card states FoundationPose "fails to work with transparent objects, glassy materials and acutely blurry images". (snippet only) — [NVIDIA NGC model card](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/isaac/models/foundationpose/1.0.0_onnx/model-card/explainability)
- FoundationPose needs an object mask in the first frame and can lose track; SuperPose/GearTrack pair it with SAM 2 to initialise and recover. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2409.19986](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19986v2)
- NVIDIA's published FoundationPose deployment and latency figures cover NVIDIA GPUs and Jetson only (Isaac ROS, TAO, NGC). No Apple Silicon or MPS port was found. (snippet only) — [Isaac ROS Pose Estimation](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/repositories_and_packages/isaac_ros_pose_estimation/index.html)
- RGBTrack (June 2025) makes FoundationPose depth-free: a binary search over depth where, for each candidate depth, rotations are sampled, refined and scored with FoundationPose's networks and the rendered mask size is compared with the observed mask; tracking then feeds an all-zero depth map, with a 2D tracker (XMem) and Kalman filter for recovery. — [arXiv 2506.17119](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17119v1)
- RGBTrack accuracy versus RGB-D FoundationPose on YCBInEOAT (average recall, FoundationPose vs RGBTrack): bleach0 0.956 vs 0.471; sugar_box1 0.973 vs 0.722; tomato_soup_can 0.896 vs 0.714; cracker_box_reorient 0.984 vs 0.861; bleach_hard 0.926 vs 0.856. Speed about 22 fps at 640x480 on an RTX 3090 (FoundationPose about 45 fps). Depth-from-mask is less accurate under occlusion. Small objects and motion blur are not evaluated. — [arXiv 2506.17119](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17119v1)
- Other RGB-only trackers surfaced: MonoTracker (BMVC 2025), a model-free RGB-only 6D tracker for unknown objects; OmniPose6D, whose authors say monocular RGB-only model-free tracking "remains underexplored"; 6DOPE-GS (ICCV 2025), online 6D pose with Gaussian splatting. (snippet only) — [MonoTracker](https://bmvc2025.bmva.org/proceedings/827/); [OmniPose6D arXiv 2410.06694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06694v2); [6DOPE-GS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Jin_6DOPE-GS_Online_6D_Object_Pose_Estimation_using_Gaussian_Splatting_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Needs NVIDIA GPU: FoundationPose, RGBTrack and the Isaac/TAO deployments are documented only on CUDA hardware (RGBTrack's own paper mentions CUDA memory limits). Running them on a Mac mini would require an unsupported port of the renderer and networks; treat as not available on Apple Silicon unless proven otherwise.
- Needs depth: FoundationPose, BundleSDF and Any6D-style methods were designed around RGB-D input. Monocular broadcast video has none, and monocular depth networks will not resolve a 20 cm paddle that is 15-80 px long.
- Render-and-compare methods rely on a crop of the object with enough texture or silhouette detail to score pose hypotheses. A paddle face is a nearly textureless flat plate; at 15-80 px, with blur, the score landscape over out-of-plane rotation is nearly flat and has the same flip ambiguity as planar PnP. RGBTrack's recall loss on ordinary, large, sharp YCB objects (for example 0.956 to 0.471) is a warning about how much worse a tiny blurred plate would be.
- The useful idea to borrow is the principle, not the networks: a custom, lightweight render-and-compare (analysis-by-synthesis) of a known paddle model against masks or blurred appearance, with pose parameterised relative to the wrist, is cheap enough to run on CPU or MPS because the model is a single rectangle-plus-handle.

### Gaps
- MegaPose (arXiv 2212.06870), SAM-6D (arXiv 2311.15707), BundleSDF (arXiv 2303.14158), GigaPose, FoundPose and Any6D (CVPR 2025) were not individually fetched in this session, so no sourced claims are made about them here. From background knowledge (not re-verified): MegaPose and GigaPose are RGB-capable render-and-compare / template methods with CAD models; SAM-6D, BundleSDF and Any6D require depth; all ship CUDA-only reference code.
- No evaluation of any of these methods on thin, small, fast, motion-blurred objects, or on sports implements, was found.
- No confirmed Apple Silicon (MPS/MLX) port of any novel-object 6D pose method was found; absence of evidence, not proof of absence.
- Whether any late-2026 method specifically targets blur-aware 6D pose (pose over the exposure interval) was not established.

## 4. Hand-object reconstruction (HaMeR, WiLoR, HOLD and newer) and inferring paddle orientation from wrist/hand plus a grip model

### Takeaway
Hand-mesh models are documented to fail on small hands and to give poor wrist orientation relative to the body, and sports reconstruction papers explicitly distrust video-derived wrist rotation; a player's hand at broadcast scale is only a few pixels. The practical consensus in the literature is to attach the racket rigidly to the hand with an assumed grip and then correct the wrist using other evidence (ball contact, priors), not to read finger pose from pixels.

### Cited Findings
- WiLoR's authors and later commentary list small hands as a weakness: small hands in crowded scenes are a failure case and "detector might fail on small hands". (snippet only) — [WiLoR arXiv 2409.12259](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12259); [UAlberta talk slides](https://vision-and-learning-lab-ualberta.github.io/talk_slides/20250603_hamid_3D_Hand_Reconstruction.pdf)
- WiLoR's supplement says its training data covers mostly common hand poses and appearances, so it can fail on unusual configurations. (snippet only) — [WiLoR CVPR 2025 supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/supplemental/Potamias_WiLoR_End-to-end_3D_CVPR_2025_supplemental.pdf)
- Hand-only estimators such as HaMeR and WiLoR do well on fingers in isolation but give implausible results when hands must be placed relative to the body; simple attachment is inadequate (Hand4Whole++ summary). Stitching independently estimated hands onto a body sequence yields "unnatural wrist orientations" and temporal wrist inconsistency (DanceHMR preprint). (snippet only) — [Hand4Whole++ summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2603.14726); [DanceHMR](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.18102.md)
- HaMeR is described as more prone to failure under occlusion and truncation. (snippet only) — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10561846)
- Vid2Player3D: wrist and racket motion from broadcast video is inaccurate "due to occlusion and motion blur"; the wrist is therefore controlled by a learned policy rather than taken from video. (snippet only) — [Vid2Player3D](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d)
- The 2026 humanoid tennis paper reports GVHMR "often produces inaccurate wrist estimates due to viewpoint occlusions" and refines racket-related wrist joints by player stroke style. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20087)
- LATTE-MV treats the racket-hand position from HMR 2.0 as the proxy for the ball contact point because racket pose is unknown. — [arXiv 2503.20936v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20936v2)
- ComPose (arXiv 2605.23523, May 2026) tracks hand-held object pose from RGB video only, with no CAD model: first-frame mesh and pose from SAM3D-Object, per-frame masks from SAM 3 text prompts, object rotation from ICP on point clouds from a 3D foundation model (Flow3R), hand rotation from weighted Procrustes over 21 WiLoR joints anchored at the wrist, and a learned SLERP blend of the two rotations. — [arXiv 2605.23523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23523)
- ComPose reports relative rotation error of 1.5 degrees (DexYCB), 2.4 degrees (HO-3D v2), 2.4 degrees (OakInk-v1) per step, but absolute rotation errors of 19.5, 49.2 and 28.8 degrees respectively, with drift over long sequences. Its stated limitations: performance drops "under severe occlusion, motion blur, or degraded image quality", and with unseen grasp types. Trained on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell; no code release or code licence confirmed. — [arXiv 2605.23523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23523)
- Other 2026 hand-object work: AGILE (SIGGRAPH 2026) reconstructs simulation-ready hand-object sequences from monocular video with a generated mesh and temporal pose propagation, including thin objects such as scissors and pens; CHOIR (arXiv 2605.20992) recovers hand motion, object shape, 6D pose trajectory and contact from open-world monocular video. HOLD jointly reconstructs hands and unknown objects from one monocular video. Template-based HOMAN fits pose with reprojection and contact terms given a CAD model. (snippet only) — [AGILE arXiv 2602.04672](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04672v3); [CHOIR arXiv 2605.20992](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20992v2); [HOLD arXiv 2311.18448 (not re-verified)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18448)

### Inferences
- Scale problem: if a 0.41 m paddle is 40-80 px, a roughly 0.09 m hand is about 9-18 px on the near side and about 3-7 px on the far side. All the hand-object benchmarks cited (DexYCB, HO-3D, OakInk, HOI4D) are close-range tabletop or egocentric data where the hand spans hundreds of pixels. Hand-mesh and hand-object methods should be assumed unusable for finger pose at broadcast scale, and of marginal value even for wrist rotation.
- Even in their home domain, the best 2026 hand-held object tracker (ComPose) has 20-50 degree absolute rotation error; that is the same order as TT4D's physics-only 26 degrees, on much easier imagery.
- What a grip model can and cannot give: the paddle handle axis is well constrained by the forearm and hand (handle passes through the closed fist, roughly perpendicular to the forearm axis by an angle set by wrist deviation). The remaining unknown is mostly rotation about the handle axis (face open/closed), which depends on grip type (continental, eastern, western, two-handed) and forearm pronation/supination. Body pose models estimate elbow and wrist position much better than forearm roll, so forearm roll and grip must come from priors plus contact constraints.
- A workable formulation is therefore: paddle pose = wrist frame (from body pipeline) composed with a per-player, slowly varying grip transform, where the grip transform and wrist-roll corrections are solved from image evidence (keypoints/masks on sharp frames) and contact constraints. This mirrors Vid2Player3D and the 2026 humanoid tennis paper, which correct the wrist rather than trust it.
- Computation: HaMeR and WiLoR are ViT models with PyTorch reference code; they can in principle run on MPS, but this was not verified, and the result at 3-18 px hands is unlikely to justify the cost.

### Gaps
- No quantitative benchmark of HaMeR/WiLoR wrist-rotation error versus hand pixel size was found.
- No published grip model for pickleball (or any racket sport) that maps hand pose to paddle face orientation was found.
- Whether whole-body models with hands (SMPL-X estimators, SAM 3D Body or similar) give usable forearm roll at broadcast resolution was not established in this session; adjacent researchers covering body pose should be consulted.
- AGILE, CHOIR and HOLD details came from search summaries only.

## 5. Physics / kinematic inference: face orientation at contact from ball velocities; interpolation with the arm chain

### Takeaway
Inverting ball impact to get racket orientation and velocity is an established idea in table tennis robotics and now in broadcast reconstruction (TT4D), and it fits this pipeline because ball trajectory and hit times are already available; expect roughly 25-degree-class orientation accuracy from physics alone, with the open/closed face angle the least certain. No paper was found that does this for pickleball or that combines it with image evidence of the paddle.

### Cited Findings
- TT4D treats the stroke as an inverse control problem: optimise racket velocity and orientation at impact so a simulated post-impact trajectory matches the observed 3D ball trajectory and bounce timing, using reconstructed ball trajectory plus spin estimates, formulated as a two-point boundary-value problem (citing Liu et al. 2012) with a unique physically plausible solution when flight time is short and spin moderate. The appendix cites CasADi and IPOPT. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- TT4D's stated rationale: "Given the pre- and post-impact ball velocity and spin, the impulse delivered by the racket is fully determined"; it uses a linear impact formulation in the racket frame. (snippet only for this sentence) — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01234)
- TT4D accuracy against marker-based motion capture, 92 strokes: orientation 26.4 +/- 4.4 degrees mean error; velocity 0.58 +/- 0.40 m/s at 3.72 m/s mean impact speed; worst components are vertical velocity and the open/closed rotation; error attributed to unknown racket bounce properties. — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- HITTER (humanoid table tennis robot, arXiv 2508.21043) uses the simple forward model: racket plane perpendicular to its velocity at impact, a coefficient of restitution along the racket normal, and no tangential friction. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21043)
- Gossard et al., "Learning Racket-Ball Bounce Dynamics Across Diverse Rubbers" (arXiv 2604.11349), use an impulse-based contact model with parameters from Gaussian processes conditioned on incoming velocity and spin; restitution and tangential response vary with impact state and differ across rubbers. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2604.11349](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11349)
- The reverse direction is also published: a racket-mounted inertial sensor gives blade velocity just after impact and, combined with a rebound model, predicts ball speed and spin. (snippet only) — [Sport-IAT record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4050077)
- Vid2Player3D resolves the wrist/racket under-determination with physics simulation and a learned wrist policy, so that the simulated racket actually produces the observed ball outcomes. (snippet only) — [Vid2Player3D](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d)
- Monocular spin estimation from the ball trajectory alone is feasible in table tennis broadcast video (Kienzle et al., 92.0% spin-class accuracy; follow-up pipeline arXiv 2511.20250), which supplies the spin input the inversion needs. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2504.19863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19863); [arXiv 2511.20250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20250)

### Inferences
- Minimal frictionless model for pickleball: the impulse on the ball is along the face normal, so the face normal at contact is parallel to (v_out minus v_in) of the ball, exactly, regardless of paddle speed and restitution. This gives two of the three rotational degrees of freedom of the face at each hit from quantities the pipeline already has. A pickleball is a hard, low-friction plastic ball on a comparatively stiff face, so the frictionless approximation is likely better than for table tennis rubber, but modern textured paddles do impart spin, which tilts the true normal away from the velocity-change direction by a friction-dependent angle. This is reasoning, not a sourced pickleball result.
- The remaining degree of freedom (rotation of the paddle about its face normal, i.e. where the handle points in the face plane) is not observable from the ball at all, but is exactly what the body pose provides: the handle must pass through the hand, and the contact point on the face must be within the face extent from the wrist. Ball position at contact plus wrist position therefore fixes the in-plane direction. Together, contact physics plus wrist position give a full 6-DoF paddle pose at each hit time.
- The normal estimate is only as good as the ball velocity estimates immediately before and after contact. With a monocular ball track, the velocity component along the camera axis is the weakest, so the face-normal error will be anisotropic; TT4D's finding that the open/closed angle is worst is consistent with this.
- Paddle normal velocity at contact follows from the restitution relation along the normal (v_out_n = -e * v_in_n + (1 + e) * v_paddle_n for a heavy paddle), giving a swing-speed constraint that can be checked against wrist speed from the body pipeline.
- Between contacts: interpolate the grip-relative transform, not the world pose. Hold a per-player grip transform (handle axis in the hand frame plus a roll about the handle), let the arm kinematic chain carry the paddle, and solve a smooth roll trajectory that hits the physics-derived face normal at each contact and a "ready position" prior between shots. Use spherical interpolation with angular-velocity limits; where sharp-frame image evidence exists (keypoints or mask on slow frames such as ready position and dinks) add it as soft reprojection terms in the same optimisation.
- Non-hit paddle motion (ready position, backswing, follow-through) has no physical constraint; here plausibility rather than accuracy is the achievable goal, and a learned or hand-authored stroke prior conditioned on stroke type would be the equivalent of Vid2Player3D's learned wrist policy.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that inverts ball impact for tennis or pickleball specifically; the cited work is table tennis. Pickleball paddle-ball restitution and friction coefficients were not researched here.
- TT4D's exact impact equations and coefficient values (Appendix F) were not retrieved.
- No published method was found that fuses contact-physics constraints with image keypoints or masks of the racket in a single optimisation; this appears to be an open combination.
- The Liu et al. 2012 reference cited by TT4D was not located or read.

## 6. Segmentation and tracking of the paddle (SAM 2 / SAM 3) and fitting a rectangle/ellipse model to masks

### Takeaway
SAM 3 accepts short noun-phrase prompts and tracks all matching instances through video, and at least one 2026 pose paper already uses SAM 3 text prompts to get hand-held object masks; official code targets CUDA, with a community MLX port that documents image (not video) support on Apple Silicon. No evidence was found on how well it segments 15-80 px blurred paddles, so this must be tested, and a small custom segmentation model (as LATTE-MV did with YOLOv8) is the proven fallback.

### Cited Findings
- SAM 3 ("Segment Anything with Concepts", ICLR 2026) takes a short noun phrase and returns masks for every matching instance in images and video; text is deliberately restricted to simple noun phrases such as "red apple" or "striped cat"; image exemplars can be added for concepts that are rare or hard to describe. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2511.16719](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16719v2); [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/segment-anything-model-3)
- Meta reports about 30 ms per image with 100+ detected objects on an H200 GPU. (snippet only) — [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/segment-anything-model-3)
- The official SAM 3 code (as mirrored in a fork named "sam3-apple-silicon") lists requirements of Python 3.12+, PyTorch 2.7+, and a "CUDA-compatible GPU with CUDA 12.6 or higher"; the model has 848M parameters; checkpoints require Hugging Face access approval; licence is the SAM License. Despite its name, that fork's README documents no Apple Silicon path. — [GitHub fork README](https://github.com/MaximeLglr/sam3-apple-silicon)
- A community MLX port exists (mlx-community/sam3-image, source github.com/Deekshith-Dade/mlx_sam3): image segmentation with text and box prompts, Apple Silicon only, macOS 13+, Python 3.13+, about 3.5 GB weights, Apache-2.0 tag on the model card. The card does not mention video tracking and gives no speed or memory figures. — [Hugging Face mlx-community/sam3-image](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/sam3-image)
- ComPose (May 2026) uses SAM 3 with text prompts for the object class to obtain per-frame masks of hand-held objects as the front end of an RGB-only pose tracker. — [arXiv 2605.23523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23523)
- LATTE-MV trained a YOLOv8 segmentation model for table, table base and player rackets on 640x360 broadcast frames, and used racket-mask centroids for hit detection and handedness only. — [arXiv 2503.20936v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20936v2)
- SuperPose/GearTrack use SAM 2 to initialise and recover a 6D tracker (mask-free initialisation), showing SAM-family masks as a standard front end for pose. (snippet only) — [arXiv 2409.19986](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19986v2)
- RGBTrack recovers depth by matching rendered mask size to observed mask size, an example of fitting a known model to a mask to resolve scale/depth. — [arXiv 2506.17119](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17119v1)

### Inferences
- "pickleball paddle" is a simple noun phrase of the kind SAM 3 is designed for, and "paddle" or "racket" are worth trying as fallbacks, with image exemplars cropped from the same broadcast to help. There is no sourced evidence either way on recall for 15-30 px, motion-blurred instances; expect misses on far-side and mid-swing frames, and plan to restrict search to a crop around each player's hands (upsampled) using the known wrist positions.
- On the Mac mini: image-mode SAM 3 via the MLX port, run per frame on hand-centred crops, sidesteps the lack of a documented MLX video tracker; temporal association is trivial because each paddle belongs to a known player. The PyTorch reference code on MPS is undocumented and should be assumed unsupported until tested.
- Fitting a known shape to a mask: a paddle silhouette is approximately a rounded rectangle plus a handle. From a clean mask one can get centroid, principal axis (in-plane direction of the long axis), projected length and projected width. Length/width versus the known 16 x 8 in dimensions gives foreshortening along each axis, hence tilt magnitudes, with the same sign (flip) ambiguity as planar PnP and the same sensitivity near face-on. At 15-30 px the width is 7-15 px and one boundary pixel of error is a 7-15% width error, so tilt from masks is coarse on the far side.
- Better conditioned alternative: differentiable or brute-force silhouette fitting of the 3D paddle model anchored at the wrist (three rotation parameters plus a small grip offset), scored by mask IoU or chamfer distance, with temporal smoothness and the contact-physics constraints from section 5 selecting between flip solutions.
- Blur-aware fitting: a blurred paddle mask is approximately the union of the paddle silhouette swept over the exposure. Fitting a swept model (pose at exposure start and end) to the streak uses blur as signal: the streak gives angular velocity direction and the swing plane, which is otherwise hard to obtain. No off-the-shelf implementation for rackets was found; this would be custom work.
- An edge-on paddle projects to a thin line a few pixels wide; masks will fragment or vanish. Edge-on is itself informative (the face normal is perpendicular to the view ray) and should be modelled as a low-area observation rather than a detection failure.

### Gaps
- No benchmark of SAM 2 or SAM 3 on small, fast, blurred sports implements was found, and none on pickleball paddles specifically.
- Whether SAM 3 video tracking runs on Apple Silicon (MPS or MLX), and its throughput and memory on a 64 GB Mac mini, were not established. The MLX port's quality relative to the reference weights is unverified.
- SAM License terms for SAM 3 were not read in this session.
- No published work fitting a rectangle/ellipse paddle model to masks for pose was found; the mask-fitting approach above is engineering inference.

## 7. Commercial products: what they expose about paddle or racket tracking

### Takeaway
Only Hawk-Eye publicly claims racket tracking (seven racket points, with racket velocity, path and angle), and that is a multi-camera in-venue system. Consumer single-camera products (SwingVision, PB Vision) publish ball, shot and player metrics, not paddle orientation; PB Vision lists paddle angle only as a long-term feature idea.

### Cited Findings
- Hawk-Eye SkeleTRACK "tracks seven key points on a player's racket, capturing data on racket velocity, ball speed, and spin", alongside 29 body points; it debuted at the Laver Cup in September 2024; the article is dated 4 April 2025 and describes the system only as optical tracking, without stating the camera count. — [Hawk-Eye: SkeleTRACK, a new era of data in tennis](https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4243365/skeletrack-a-new-era-of-data-in-tennis)
- Another Hawk-Eye post describes racket metrics as path, angle and speed, and says the system can also monitor equipment such as "a ball, a bat or any other sports equipment". (snippet only) — [Hawk-Eye: The future of data tracking in sport](https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/news/4211958/the-future-of-data-tracking-in-sport)
- Hawk-Eye's baseball deployment in Japan uses 8 cameras in each of 12 NPB stadiums and lists pitch and bat tracking as a product area; Hawk-Eye is a Sony group company. (snippet only) — [Hawk-Eye NPB](https://www.hawkeyeinnovations.com/npb)
- SwingVision: the ITF approval report describes an iOS app using the rear camera at 60 fps with a series of machine learning models, with outputs listed as 3D ball location, shot type, spin type and ball speed; racket angle and swing speed are not listed. (snippet only) — [ITF PAT-25-037 approval report](https://www.itftennis.com/media/15294/pat-25-037-approval-report-swingvision.pdf)
- A review reports that SwingVision's swing-related measurements with an Apple Watch come from the wrist-worn device, i.e. inertial sensing rather than vision of the racket. (snippet only) — [iMore review](https://www.imore.com/apps/level-up-your-serve-with-ai-powered-tennis-coaching-iphone-app-swingvision)
- PB Vision: on its public feature-request board, the PB Vision team itself posted (3 June 2025) an idea to track "spin rate, paddle attack angle, and ball revolution", possibly needing a custom marked ball, calling it "a more advanced idea" that "could be really cool long term". No shipped status is shown. — [PB Vision feature board: spin rate / paddle angle](https://pbvision.featurebase.app/p/spin-rate-paddle-angle)
- A separate PB Vision feature-board item covers movement, posture and paddle tracking, including paddle "ready position" detection and paddle height during rallies, as proposed features. (snippet only) — [PB Vision feature board: movement, posture, and paddle tracking](https://pbvision.featurebase.app/p/on-balance-shot-indicator)
- Third-party descriptions of PB Vision's shipped analytics list shot speed, placement, shot types, serve speed and rally length, not paddle orientation. (snippet only; third-party) — [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/pb-vision-pickleball-ai/)

### Inferences
- The only commercial racket tracking with published specifics is multi-camera (Hawk-Eye installs are venue systems with many synchronised high-frame-rate cameras; the NPB figure of 8 cameras per stadium is indicative). It is not evidence that single-camera racket pose is feasible, and no Hawk-Eye pickleball racket/paddle product was found.
- Hawk-Eye choosing seven racket points (versus RacketVision's five) suggests a denser outline is what practitioners use when resolution allows; their virtual recreations therefore render a measured racket, which is the quality bar a monocular pipeline will not match.
- Consumer single-camera vendors expose ball and body derived stats and use wearables for swing metrics. As of the sources found, a monocular pipeline that outputs a plausible paddle pose through a rally would be ahead of what consumer pickleball products publicly expose.

### Gaps
- PlaySight and Sportradar: no information on racket or paddle tracking was found; they were not searched individually in depth.
- SwingVision and PB Vision internals are unpublished; whether either tracks the paddle internally (for example for shot-type classification) is unknown. The current (late 2026) status of PB Vision's paddle-tracking ideas was not confirmed; the board items are from 2025.
- Hawk-Eye's racket-tracking camera count, frame rate and accuracy for tennis are not published in the pages read.
- MLB Statcast bat tracking (a Hawk-Eye-based multi-camera system) was searched for but no source was retrieved in this session.

## Cross-cutting synthesis for the report writer

### Takeaway
There is no off-the-shelf monocular 6-DoF paddle pose solution. The evidence supports a hybrid: paddle position from the wrist (body pipeline), face normal at hits from ball-impact physics, in-plane and roll refinement from 2D paddle keypoints or masks on sharp frames (near players mainly), and kinematic interpolation with a grip model in between. All components can be built to run offline on Apple Silicon; the NVIDIA-only general 6D pose stacks are not needed.

### Cited Findings
- Direct image evidence available today: 2D five-keypoint racket model with 5-10 px error and weak width keypoints (RacketVision, MIT data). — [arXiv 2511.17045v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3); [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zonetom67/RacketVision)
- Physics evidence available today: impact inversion with 26.4 degree validated orientation error (TT4D). — [arXiv 2605.01234](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01234)
- Wrist/hand evidence is acknowledged as unreliable in broadcast video by Vid2Player3D and the 2026 humanoid tennis paper. — [Vid2Player3D](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d); [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20087)
- General 6D pose stacks: RGB-D oriented, NVIDIA GPU deployments, fail on strongly blurred images. — [arXiv 2312.08344](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08344v2); [NVIDIA NGC model card](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/isaac/models/foundationpose/1.0.0_onnx/model-card/explainability)

### Inferences
- Suggested accuracy expectations to state plainly: paddle position within a few centimetres of the true grip location (inherits wrist error); face normal at hits within roughly 15-30 degrees; orientation between hits is plausible rather than measured, better for near players (40-80 px) on slow frames, essentially prior-driven for far players (15-30 px).
- Component hardware summary: RTMDet/RTMPose-class keypoint models and YOLO-class segmenters are small and run on CPU/MPS/CoreML (general knowledge, not verified in this session); SAM 3 has a community MLX image port; physics inversion and kinematic optimisation are CPU-only (TT4D uses CasADi/IPOPT); FoundationPose-family methods need NVIDIA CUDA and depth.
- Labelling a pickleball-specific keypoint/mask set (a few hundred to a few thousand frames, weighted toward blur and far-side instances) is the single most leveraged investment, since no pickleball paddle pose data exists publicly.

### Gaps
- No end-to-end published system does what this project needs, so there is no reference accuracy for the combined approach; a small ground-truth capture (for example a paddle with markers or an IMU, or a second camera) would be needed to validate it.
