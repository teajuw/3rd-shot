# Camera calibration / court registration and broadcast video parsing for a pickleball digital twin (single broadcast camera)

Notes compiled 2026-11-11. Conventions used below:

- **[NEW]** marks anything published or released after mid-2025, with the exact date where I could confirm it.
- "Cited Findings" are facts I read on the linked page during this research session. "Inferences" are my own reasoning or prior knowledge that I did **not** re-verify this session; they are labelled as such.
- Pickleball court numbers used throughout: 20 ft x 44 ft (6.096 m x 13.411 m), non-volley zone 7 ft (2.134 m) each side of the net, net 36 in (0.914 m) at the sidelines/posts and 34 in (0.864 m) at centre. These come from the task brief; I did not re-fetch the USA Pickleball rulebook.

## 1. Best current methods for sports field/court registration and full single-view camera calibration; which have code/weights and can be adapted to pickleball?

### Takeaway
The dominant, still-current recipe is "learned keypoint (+ line) heatmaps -> closed-form camera -> non-linear refinement" (NBJW / PnLCalib for per-frame, BroadTrack for temporally tracked), all soccer-trained; no 2025-2026 "foundation model for court registration" was found. For racket sports the directly relevant 2026 evidence is CourtSI (March 2026): 4 court corners + 2 net-height points -> joint intrinsics/extrinsics PnP gives about 2% focal-length error and was applied to pickleball, which is exactly the upgrade path from the user's 4-corner ResNet50 homography.

### Cited Findings

**SoccerNet challenge lineage**
- The SoccerNet 2025 results report (arXiv 2508.19182, submitted 26 Aug 2025 **[NEW]**) has no stand-alone camera calibration task; calibration appears only as a component of Game State Reconstruction (GSR). It states that popular calibration approaches use landmark detection followed by calibration or homography estimation, naming NBJW, PnLCalib and BroadTrack as the commonly used pipelines. — [SoccerNet 2025 Challenges Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)
- 2025 GSR winner KIST-GSR (GS-HOTA 63.90, vs 29.01 baseline; 14 teams) maps players to the pitch with a "multi-frame keypoint detection model"; another team (GSR-13, Leipzig) refined homographies across frames with tracked keypoints + RANSAC and reported up to 4% gain over the NBJW baseline. — [SoccerNet 2025 Challenges Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)
- The SoccerNet 2024 report lists four tasks (ball action spotting, dense captioning, multi-view foul recognition, game state reconstruction), i.e. no separate calibration leaderboard that year; participants used the "No Bells, Just Whistles" calibration and a SegFormer-based one-step method. — [SoccerNet 2024 Challenges Results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10587)
- The last dedicated SoccerNet camera calibration challenge found was 2023 (task: retrieve intrinsic and extrinsic parameters from images). The winning approach applied the standard OpenCV camera calibration algorithm to subsets of detected points selected with heuristics; code for the 1st-place solution is at NikolasEnt/soccernet-calibration-sportlight (reported inactive). — [SoccerNet 2023 Challenges Results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.06006); [Enhancing Soccer Camera Calibration Through Keypoint Exploitation](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07401v1)

**PnLCalib (extended NBJW) — per-frame, keypoints + lines**
- Paper: arXiv 2404.08401; v5 dated 10 Mar 2026, accepted to Computer Vision and Image Understanding **[NEW version]**. Extended version of "No Bells, Just Whistles" (NBJW). — [arXiv 2404.08401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08401v5); [GitHub mguti97/PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)
- Method: HRNetv2 predicts keypoint heatmaps and line-extremity heatmaps; line intersections are added as extra keypoints; full perspective model P = K R [I | -t] with principal point fixed at image centre, zero skew, known aspect ratio; closed-form solution then non-linear refinement. If >= 6 keypoints, K is estimated first; otherwise K, R, t jointly. For planar points: DLT + RANSAC homography, Levenberg-Marquardt refinement, then decomposition to R, t. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- Non-planar points (goal posts / crossbar) are used for calibration when too few ground points are visible, but are excluded from the homography estimate. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- PnL refinement: K fixed, optimise pose only; cost = weighted sum (alpha = 0.6) of point-to-line distances (detected line endpoints vs projected model line) and keypoint reprojection error. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- Results: SN22-test-center JaC@5 80.6 / completeness 98.7 / final score 79.5 (TVCalib: 57.6 / 93.7 / 53.9); WC14-test JaC@5 85.9 (TVCalib final score about 39.5-39.9). WC14 homography: IoU_part 97.0%, IoU_whole 93.4%, projection error 0.60 m. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- Runtime 164-439 ms/frame depending on configuration (hardware not captured in my read). — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- Stated limitations: needs >= 4 visible keypoints; lens distortion not modelled in the paper; temporal consistency listed as future work; reprojection error is not a fully reliable quality proxy. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- Code/weights: code GPL-2.0-or-later, weights CC BY 4.0, released via GitHub Releases v1.0.0 (keypoint and line models, base and fine-tuned on WC14/TSWC/WorldPose). Repo update dated 2026-03-10 added lens-distortion optimisation (`utils/utils_calib_wp.py`) **[NEW]**. Soccer only; custom fields/other sports not described. CUDA not explicitly required for inference (training defaults to `cuda:0`). 112 stars. — [GitHub mguti97/PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)

**BroadTrack — temporally tracked broadcast camera (most relevant design for "smooth")**
- arXiv 2412.01721 (Dec 2024). Camera model: pinhole with single focal length f, square pixels, principal point at centre, one radial distortion coefficient k1 (L(r) = 1 + k1 r^2); pose = pan, tilt, roll + focal point C that moves along the optical axis with zoom; tripod constraint: pan/tilt axes meet at fixed point T. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- Each frame is updated from the previous frame by Levenberg-Marquardt over (1) field-marking reprojection error from semantic segmentation with Cauchy loss, (2) optical-flow reprojection (pyramidal Lucas-Kanade, players masked out), (3) tripod penalty. A Jaccard confidence between segmented markings and projected template triggers re-initialisation. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- Results on sn-gamestate: JaC@5 56.88, mean reprojection error 5.02 px, completeness 100% vs NBJW 37.14 / 10.28 px / 93.67% and TVCalib 19.88 / 12.4 px / 99.93%. Removing radial distortion is the largest ablation drop (JaC@5 42.09 vs 56.88). — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- Per-frame NBJW's estimated camera position can drift up to about 20 m within one sequence, while BroadTrack's stays near the tripod; residual high-frequency focal variation is attributed to the focal/depth "vertigo" trade-off at long range. No numeric jitter metric is given. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- Speed 16 fps at 1080p on two RTX 4090s; code at https://github.com/evs-broadcast/BroadTrack. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)

**Benchmark-protocol argument for full cameras over homographies**
- Magera et al., "A Universal Protocol to Benchmark Camera Calibration for Sports" (arXiv 2404.09807, Apr 2024) argues that homography-based field registration overlooks non-planar elements and lens distortion. — [arXiv 2404.09807](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09807v1)

**Racket-sport calibration (closest to pickleball)**
- CourtSI / "Stepping VLMs onto the Court" (arXiv 2603.09896, 10 Mar 2026 **[NEW]**): annotators mark four ground corner points of the court and two height points on the net per image; intrinsics and extrinsics are optimised jointly as a PnP problem; the net height points "help stabilize focal length estimation". Against chessboard-calibrated multi-view ground truth, focal-length error is about 2% (fx 2.2%, fy 2.4%), worst for tennis (about 4.2-4.3%); the method outperforms WildCamera and Depth Anything V3 on intrinsics. Sports: badminton, tennis, table tennis, plus **pickleball in CourtSI-Ext**. Static-camera scenes reuse one calibration. Code: github.com/Visionary-Laboratory/CourtSI. Keypoints are manual, not a learned detector. — [CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)
- TT3D (table tennis, arXiv 2504.10035, CVPRW 2025): four table corners from a segmentation mask (contours + probabilistic Hough) -> PnP with unknown focal length; pose and focal are refined alternately with a line search on f (initialised at 1500 px), assuming no distortion, centred principal point, fx = fy. Corner error 2.39 +/- 1.47 px. On synthetic data rotation error about 3 degrees, about 5% relative error in the table XY plane, and "significant" translation and focal errors which the authors say do not hurt reconstruction. 3D ball MAE: 8.9 cm side view, 14.5 cm oblique, 22.3 cm back view (noise-free 2D), 29.8 cm back view with noise. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)
- TennisCourtDetector (yastrebksv): TrackNet-like single-frame heatmap network, 14 court keypoints (+1 centre channel), 8,841 broadcast images (hard/clay/grass) at 1280x720, network I/O 640x360. Accuracy (within 7 px) 0.933 base -> 0.961 with line-intersection keypoint refinement + homography post-processing; median error 2.83 px -> 1.83 px. Pretrained weights on Google Drive; license not stated; 286 stars; only 3 commits. — [GitHub yastrebksv/TennisCourtDetector](https://github.com/yastrebksv/TennisCourtDetector)
- TennisExpert (arXiv 2603.13397, 17 Mar 2026 **[NEW]**) still uses a 14-keypoint heatmap model + homography for court registration (not a full camera); parser runs up to 40 FPS on one GPU. Code/dataset: github.com/LZYAndy/TennisExpert. — [TennisExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13397)
- "Automated Tennis Player and Ball Tracking with Court Keypoints Detection" (arXiv 2511.04126, Nov 2025 **[NEW]**) uses a ResNet50-based court keypoint regressor — i.e. the same design as the user's prototype remains common in 2025 hobby-grade pipelines. — [arXiv 2511.04126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04126)
- MonoTrack (badminton, CVSports@CVPR 2022, arXiv 2204.01899) is an end-to-end monocular 3D shuttle trajectory system combining court dimensions, physics and player poses; the abstract page gives no calibration detail and no code link. — [MonoTrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01899)
- A 5 Oct 2026 badminton paper **[NEW]** (arXiv 2611.00031) calibrates a full pinhole camera (K, R, C) from court-line intersections manually verified against a template, with calibration residuals 1.2-9 px; lens-distortion correction did not remove a net-plane residual bias. — [Monocular Badminton Speed Estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00031)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The best adaptation for pickleball is not to fine-tune a soccer model's weights wholesale but to reuse the *architecture and solver*: a heatmap keypoint network (HRNet / TrackNet-style, as in PnLCalib and TennisCourtDetector) retrained on pickleball keypoints, followed by PnLCalib-style point+line refinement with a pickleball 3D model. PnLCalib's code hard-codes a soccer 3D field model; swapping in a pickleball template requires editing the model definition and retraining heads (the repo does not document custom fields). GPL-2.0 code licence matters if the project is ever distributed.
- (Inference) A pickleball court gives a compact but adequate planar point set: 4 outer corners, 4 NVZ-line/sideline intersections, 2 NVZ-line/centreline T-points, 2 baseline/centreline points, 2 net-line/sideline points = at least 12-14 coplanar keypoints (the Ultralytics-forum model in section 6 uses 12), plus non-coplanar net-post tops and the net tape. That exceeds PnLCalib's ">= 6 keypoints" threshold for estimating K first.
- (Inference) Because the PPA/MLP main camera is a single elevated behind-the-baseline view with the whole court nearly always visible, the hard soccer problems (partial field visibility, left/right ambiguity, large pan/zoom) are mostly absent; the remaining hard problems are occlusion of near-baseline keypoints by players, low-contrast or multi-sport line clutter, and the weak perspective constraint on focal length (see section 2).
- (Prior knowledge, not re-verified this session) TVCalib (arXiv 2207.11709) is a differentiable segment-reprojection optimiser with public code; KaliCalib is a basketball court keypoint calibration method; NBJW has its own repo (mguti97/no-bells-just-whistles, confirmed as listed in search results only).

### Gaps
- I found **no 2025-2026 foundation-model approach specific to sports field/court registration** (e.g. a zero-shot, any-sport court registrar). Searches returned only soccer-specific keypoint methods and manual-annotation pipelines (CourtSI).
- I found **no peer-reviewed or arXiv paper on pickleball court registration or pickleball 3D reconstruction** other than the pickleball extension set in CourtSI.
- I did not confirm what the KIST-GSR "multi-frame keypoint detection model" is or whether its code is public.
- I could not confirm whether a dedicated SoccerNet camera calibration challenge ran in 2024, 2025 or 2026; the 2024 and 2025 reports show none. A SoccerNet 2026 results report was not found.
- PnLCalib and BroadTrack runtime on Apple Silicon is untested in any source I found; both are PyTorch + classical optimisation, so MPS/CPU inference is plausible but unverified.

## 2. Getting a full projection matrix from a planar court: non-coplanar points, focal length from one homography, lens distortion, and height accuracy

### Takeaway
A single ground homography gives only two constraints on intrinsics, so with square pixels and a centred principal point it yields focal length in closed form — but the estimate is weakly conditioned for a distant telephoto view, which is why 2025-2026 racket-sport pipelines add net-height points and jointly refine f with pose. Published evidence puts focal error at about 2% with corners + net points (CourtSI) and 3D ball error at roughly 9-30 cm for table tennis with planar-only calibration and worst results in the behind-the-court view (TT3D); one radial distortion term materially improves broadcast calibration (BroadTrack ablation).

### Cited Findings
- Each plane-to-image homography yields two constraints on the image of the absolute conic (orthogonality and equal norm of the imaged basis vectors); with only two constraints one can estimate the principal point or the focal length but not both. If the principal point is assumed at the image centre, the focal length can be solved in closed form from the homography entries. — [Berkeley planar homography notes](https://farid.berkeley.edu/teaching/spring2025/info290t/readings/planarHomography.pdf); [Gurdjos et al., "Another Way of Looking at Plane-Based Calibration"](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-47979-1_17.pdf)
- The factorisation of the homography is not unique for a fronto-parallel view of the plane (degenerate case). — [Berkeley planar homography notes](https://farid.berkeley.edu/teaching/spring2025/info290t/readings/planarHomography.pdf)
- PnLCalib's practical recipe: assume zero skew, known aspect ratio, principal point at centre; DLT + RANSAC homography -> LM refinement -> decomposition for R, t; non-planar points (goal posts) are used in the full-camera estimate but excluded from the homography. — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- TT3D's practical recipe for 4 coplanar points: PnP with unknown focal length, alternating pose refinement and a line search over f, started at f = 1500 px; calibration is run twice with different corner orderings and the lower-error, plausible-distance solution is kept. Translation and focal errors are "significant" on synthetic tests while rotation error is about 3 degrees and in-plane error about 5%. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)
- CourtSI: adding two net-height points to four court corners and jointly optimising intrinsics + extrinsics gives about 2.2-2.4% focal error versus chessboard ground truth (4.2-4.3% for tennis), with centimetre-level ball/player localisation reported and a 15 cm threshold used for distance questions. — [CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)
- BroadTrack notes a "vertigo effect": at long range, depth (camera distance) and focal length trade off, producing residual high-frequency focal variation even with tracking. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- Lens distortion: BroadTrack uses a single radial coefficient k1 and its ablation shows removing it drops JaC@5 from 56.88 to 42.09 — the largest single ablation. PnLCalib's paper neglects distortion but its repo added distortion optimisation on 2026-03-10. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1); [GitHub mguti97/PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)
- In the Oct 2026 badminton study **[NEW]**, a free monocular trajectory fit gave 13.3% median speed error against a stereo reference; constraining the trajectory to cross the net plane reduced it to 8.6% (oracle 3.5%). Calibration residuals were 1.2-9 px, and lens-distortion correction did not remove a net-plane residual bias. — [Monocular Badminton Speed Estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00031)
- TT3D's 3D ball error is worst for the back (behind-the-table) view: 22.3 cm MAE noise-free, 29.8 cm with 2D noise, vs 8.9 / 12.4 cm for the side view. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)
- Direct ball-size depth is unreliable in broadcast footage because the ball is often under about 10 px. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)

### Inferences
- (Standard geometry, prior knowledge) Closed form: with H = K [r1 r2 t] up to scale and K = diag(f, f, 1) after shifting the principal point to the origin, r1 . r2 = 0 gives f^2 = -(h11 h12 + h21 h22) / (h31 h32), and |r1| = |r2| gives a second estimate; then r1, r2 = K^-1 h1, K^-1 h2 normalised, r3 = r1 x r2, re-orthogonalise with SVD, t = K^-1 h3 / scale. The estimate degrades as h31, h32 approach 0 (weak perspective / long lens), which is exactly the broadcast regime: a small pixel error moves f a lot, and f and camera distance are nearly interchangeable for points on the plane.
- (Prior knowledge of OpenCV, not re-fetched) Practical OpenCV routes: (a) `cv2.calibrateCamera` on a single view with flags `CALIB_FIX_PRINCIPAL_POINT | CALIB_FIX_ASPECT_RATIO | CALIB_ZERO_TANGENT_DIST | CALIB_FIX_K2 | CALIB_FIX_K3` and `CALIB_USE_INTRINSIC_GUESS` — non-planar object points require an intrinsic guess; (b) a 1-D search over f with `cv2.solvePnP` (`SOLVEPNP_IPPE` for purely planar points, `SOLVEPNP_ITERATIVE`/`SQPNP` once net points are added) picking the f that minimises reprojection error, as TT3D does; (c) a custom `scipy.optimize.least_squares` over (f, k1, rvec, tvec) with robust loss. Docs: https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/d0c/group__calib3d.html (URL from memory).
- (Inference) Why the net matters for the *twin*, not just for f: with planar points only, errors in f/distance barely change ground-plane reprojection but directly scale the back-projected ray's height above the court. The net-post tops (0.914 m) and net-centre tape (0.864 m) sit at ball-relevant heights in the middle of the playing volume, so constraining them pins down the height mapping exactly where rallies happen. The net tape is also a long, high-contrast line that can be fitted as a line constraint (point-to-line residual, PnLCalib style) rather than as two points; note the tape sags (36 in at posts -> 34 in at centre), so model it as two straight segments or a shallow curve, not a single straight 3D line.
- (Inference; caveat) Pickleball net posts are outside the sidelines (I recall 22 ft post-to-post, i.e. 1 ft outside each sideline, from the USA Pickleball rulebook — not verified this session), and on pro courts portable net systems and sponsor boards may hide the post base. Use the net-tape/sideline-plane intersection (36 in above the sideline) only if the rule and the physical net agree; otherwise detect post tops directly.
- (Inference) Expected height accuracy: no source reports per-height error for pickleball. Triangulating from CourtSI (about 2% focal error with corners + net points) and TT3D (back-view 3D ball MAE 22-30 cm from 4 planar points on a much smaller target), a behind-the-baseline pickleball camera calibrated with about 12 court points + net should place a *static* point at net height to within a few centimetres vertically near the net, with error growing toward the far baseline and with height above the net (lobs). The dominant 3D ball error will then come from the monocular depth-along-ray ambiguity, not from calibration; physics-constrained trajectory fitting (bounce on z = 0, gravity, net-plane crossing as in the Oct 2026 badminton paper) is what resolves it.
- (Inference) Distortion: broadcast zoom lenses at the main-camera focal lengths typically have small radial distortion; a single k1 (BroadTrack's choice) estimated once per match from many frames is the right complexity. Estimating k1 per frame from about 14 points will overfit.
- (Inference) Because the whole court is visible and the camera is essentially static, pool keypoint/line observations over hundreds of frames to estimate one (f, k1, R, t) — averaging out detector noise is far more effective than any per-frame trick, and it sidesteps the f/distance vertigo jitter.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies height (z) error as a function of calibration error for a behind-the-baseline racket-sport camera; the numbers above are triangulated from adjacent work and should be validated empirically (e.g. check that reprojected net tape lies on the image tape, and that player head heights and ball bounce heights are plausible).
- I did not fetch Zhang (2000) or the OpenCV docs this session; the formulas and flags above are from prior knowledge.
- I did not confirm from a primary source the official net-post spacing or whether PPA/MLP broadcast nets meet the 36 in / 34 in specification exactly.
- No source evaluates rolling-shutter or broadcast-encoder effects for this use; the Oct 2026 badminton paper explicitly left rolling shutter uncorrected.

## 3. General-purpose geometry foundation models (DUSt3R/MASt3R, VGGT, MoGe/MoGe-2, Depth Anything V2/V3, UniDepth, MapAnything, successors): do they help?

### Takeaway
For a static broadcast camera looking at a court of known dimensions, the published evidence favours classic court-based PnP: in CourtSI (March 2026) court+net PnP beat both WildCamera and Depth Anything V3 on intrinsics, and DA3-propagated camera parameters drifted enough that the authors reverted to per-frame PnP. Foundation models are useful as priors and auxiliaries (focal initialisation, sanity checks, player/scene depth ordering, keypoint propagation), and several run on Apple Silicon via community MPS ports, but none replaces the court as the metric anchor.

### Cited Findings
- CourtSI: the court-keypoint PnP calibration "outperforms WildCamera and DepthAnythingV3" on focal-length error (about 2%); for dynamic-camera cases they tried propagating keypoints with Depth Anything V3 depth + relative pose but "found DepthAnythingV3's propagated camera parameters drifted", so each frame's camera is estimated independently. — [CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)
- In sports like tennis and soccer the camera-to-ball distance is too large for accurate direct depth estimation, motivating calibration + trajectory priors instead. — ["Where Is The Ball", CVPRW 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/CVSPORTS/papers/Ponglertnapakorn_Where_Is_The_Ball_3D_Ball_Trajectory_Estimation_From_2D_CVPRW_2025_paper.pdf)
- Depth Anything 3 (arXiv 2511.10647, Nov 2025 **[NEW]**) predicts spatially consistent geometry from an arbitrary number of views with or without known poses; the HF model card for DA3METRIC-LARGE lists metric depth, Apache-2.0, and claims DA3 significantly outperforms VGGT on multi-view depth and pose; the quick-start selects CUDA or CPU (no MPS mentioned). — [arXiv 2511.10647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10647v1); [HF DA3METRIC-LARGE mirror](https://huggingface.co/APaul1/DA3METRIC-LARGE)
- DA3 on Apple Silicon (community fork `awesome-depth-anything-3`, PyTorch 2.9.0, MPS, batch 1; chip not stated; numbers unverified by upstream): DA3-Small 46 ms, Base 93 ms, Large 265 ms (3.8 img/s; CPU 0.3 img/s; NVIDIA L4 10.3 img/s), Giant 618 ms. DA3NESTED-GIANT-LARGE outputs metres directly; DA3METRIC-LARGE needs `metric_depth = focal * net_output / 300`. News entries dated 25 and 30 Nov 2025 **[NEW]**. — [GitHub Aedelon/awesome-depth-anything-3](https://github.com/Aedelon/awesome-depth-anything-3)
- A separate third-party wrapper marks its DA3 MPS path as untested while noting Depth Anything V2 works on MPS. — [RyannDaGreat/CommonSource depthanything3.py](https://github.com/RyannDaGreat/CommonSource/blob/master/depthanything3.py)
- VGGT on Apple Silicon: community port `jmanhype/vggt-mps` (MIT, v2.0.0) runs VGGT via MPS on M1/M2/M3 with 8 GB+ RAM, float32 only (MPS does not support float16 autocast for this model), about 5 GB weights; it lists 6-DoF camera parameters as output but publishes no measured speed/memory benchmarks and no validation of camera outputs on MPS. — [GitHub jmanhype/vggt-mps](https://github.com/jmanhype/vggt-mps)
- MapAnything (arXiv 2509.13414, V1 release Sept 2025, 3DV 2026 **[NEW]**): feed-forward metric 3D reconstruction that accepts images plus *optional* intrinsics/ray directions, depth and camera poses (64 input configurations) and outputs metric scale factor, depth, point maps, intrinsics and poses. Code Apache-2.0; `facebook/map-anything` weights CC-BY-NC 4.0, `facebook/map-anything-apache` weights Apache-2.0. Sample code selects CUDA else CPU; MPS/Apple Silicon not mentioned. The repo also wraps external models including VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R, Pi3-X and DA3. — [GitHub facebookresearch/map-anything](https://github.com/facebookresearch/map-anything)
- MoGe-2 (arXiv 2507.02546, July 2025, NeurIPS 2025 **[NEW]**) recovers a metric-scale 3D point map from a single image, predicting metric scale via an MLP head on the CLS token; original MoGe can derive focal length/FOV from the point map. — [MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02546v1); [MoGe](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19115v1)
- UniDepthV2 predicts metric 3D from single images with a self-promptable camera module (dense camera representation). — [UniDepthV2 search summary / arXiv 2502.20110](https://hyper.ai/papers/2502.20110)
- GeoCalib (ECCV 2024) is a single-image calibration method: a DNN predicts a perspective field with confidences and camera parameters are fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt; it reports uncertainties and can use partially known parameters. — [GeoCalib](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2024/veicht2024eccv-geocalib)
- 2026 successors surfaced in search (not read in depth): "UniScale: Unified Scale-Aware 3D Reconstruction for Multi-View" (arXiv 2602.23224, Feb 2026 **[NEW]**) and "Beyond Relative Geometry: Metric-Aware Geometry Perception for Robotics" (MAGP, arXiv 2608.27497, Aug 2026 **[NEW]**), whose authors claim image-only depth/point scores surpassing DA3 by 17.41% / 17.00% — a single-paper claim, not an independent benchmark. — [UniScale](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23224); [MAGP](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27497.md)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Multi-view feed-forward models (DUSt3R/MASt3R, VGGT, DA3 any-view, MapAnything) get their pose/scale from parallax between views. A static or purely rotating/zooming tripod camera has no translational baseline, so these models cannot triangulate; they reduce to monocular priors, where metric scale for a stadium-scale scene shot with a long lens is the weakest regime (long focal length, low perspective, out-of-distribution scale). A court of exactly known size is a far stronger metric constraint than a learned scale head.
- (Inference) Useful roles for the foundation models in this project: (1) focal-length/FOV initialisation or sanity bound for the court solver (MoGe-2, UniDepthV2, GeoCalib, DA3) — helpful mainly for replays/alternate angles where few court points are visible; (2) MapAnything's ability to take known intrinsics/poses as input means the court-derived camera can be fed in to get metrically consistent dense depth of players/stands for the 3D replay background; (3) relative depth ordering for player occlusion reasoning. None should be in the main calibration loop.
- (Inference) Apple Silicon feasibility: DA3 (community fork, measured) and VGGT (community port, unmeasured) run on MPS in float32; MapAnything, MoGe-2, UniDepthV2 are ordinary PyTorch ViT models and likely run on MPS or CPU with `PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1`, but I found no evidence either way. 64 GB unified memory is ample for single-image/few-view inference at these model sizes. Since calibration needs these models on a handful of frames per match at most, even CPU speed would be acceptable.

### Gaps
- No source benchmarks MoGe-2, UniDepthV2, VGGT or MapAnything on broadcast sports footage for focal length or metric scale; the only direct sports comparison found is CourtSI's (WildCamera and DA3 lose to court PnP), and I did not extract its per-model numbers for those baselines.
- No official (upstream) MPS support statement found for DA3, VGGT or MapAnything; all Apple Silicon evidence is from community forks. No MLX ports were found for any of these geometry models.
- I did not verify MoGe-2 / UniDepthV2 focal-length benchmark numbers, nor the status of "VGGT-Omega" and "Pi3-X" (named as wrapped external models in the MapAnything repo) — treat those names as leads only.
- Search results for 2026 successors (UniScale, MAGP) were not read in full; their code/weights status is unknown.

## 4. Temporally smooth calibration for a mostly static or slowly panning broadcast camera

### Takeaway
The literature's answer is to stop estimating a free camera per frame: fix what is physically fixed (camera/tripod position, distortion, usually focal within a shot) and track only pan/tilt/zoom with a field-reprojection + optical-flow objective (BroadTrack), or simply reuse one calibration for static shots (CourtSI); Kalman filtering of per-frame PnP (TT3D) is the lightweight fallback. Per-frame independent methods demonstrably produce physically implausible camera motion (NBJW's camera centre wanders up to about 20 m).

### Cited Findings
- BroadTrack: tripod-constrained model (fixed pivot T, focal point sliding along optical axis with zoom), frame-to-frame LM update using field-marking reprojection (Cauchy loss), Lucas-Kanade optical flow on the pitch plane with players masked, and a tripod penalty; confidence-triggered re-initialisation needing only two point correspondences (estimating f, pan, tilt with roll = 0 and camera at the tripod centre). Tripod position is estimated by first running without the constraint and then optimising T from the resulting cameras. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- BroadTrack vs per-frame: mean reprojection error 5.02 px vs NBJW 10.28 px; fixing camera centre alone ("fixed C" variant) already yields JaC@5 50.97 vs NBJW 37.14; qualitative plots show smoother pan and focal curves; NBJW's focal point drifts up to about 20 m in a sequence. — [BroadTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)
- TT3D: per-frame calibration, with a Kalman filter applied to smooth raw estimates when the camera moves or zooms. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)
- CourtSI: static-camera scenes reuse a single calibration; for moving cameras, per-frame independent PnP was preferred over DA3-propagated parameters because the latter drifted. — [CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)
- SoccerNet GSR 2025: one team refined homographies across frames with tracked keypoints + RANSAC and gained up to 4% over NBJW; the winning team used a multi-frame keypoint detector. — [SoccerNet 2025 Challenges Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182)
- PnLCalib lists temporal consistency across frames as future work (i.e. it is per-frame). — [PnLCalib v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401v5)
- PTZ-Calib (arXiv 2502.09075, Feb 2025) is a dedicated robust PTZ camera calibration method (surfaced in search; not read). — [PTZ-Calib](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09075v1)

### Inferences
Recommended hierarchy for the PPA/MLP main camera, from simplest (and probably sufficient) to most general — all inference:
1. **Solve once per shot (static assumption).** After shot segmentation, test staticness per main-camera shot (e.g. median court-keypoint displacement or a global homography between first/last frames below about 0.5 px). If static, aggregate keypoint and line detections over all frames (median/robust mean per keypoint, or stack all observations) and solve a single (f, k1, R, t). Output is perfectly smooth by construction and detector noise averages down roughly as 1/sqrt(N).
2. **Solve once per match for the physically fixed parameters.** The main camera sits on a fixed platform for the whole match, so camera centre C (and k1 at a given zoom) is shared across every main-camera shot. Estimate C once by bundle adjustment over many shots/frames, then per shot solve only rotation (3 DoF) + focal (1 DoF). This removes the f/distance "vertigo" ambiguity: with C fixed, f is well determined by the court's image size.
3. **Clip-level bundle adjustment for slow pan/zoom.** Parameterise pan(t), tilt(t), f(t) as low-order B-splines or with a second-difference (acceleration) penalty, with shared C, roll and k1; minimise robust reprojection error of all per-frame keypoints/lines in one `scipy.optimize.least_squares` problem (sparse Jacobian). This is a smoother rather than a filter, so it uses future frames — appropriate for offline processing and better than a causal Kalman filter. A Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother over (pan, tilt, f) is the cheap equivalent.
4. **Inter-frame registration as an extra constraint.** For a rotating/zooming camera with fixed centre, consecutive frames are related by a homography H = K2 R K1^-1 (true for the whole scene, not only the court plane), so dense optical flow / feature matches on static background (stands, banners, court surface; players masked) constrain relative pan/tilt/zoom much more tightly than about 14 court keypoints do — the same idea as BroadTrack's optical-flow term.
5. **Smooth in a physically meaningful parameterisation.** Never smooth homography entries or raw rotation matrices; smooth pan/tilt/log-f. Keep C, roll, k1 constant.
- (Inference) Sub-pixel jitter in the camera produces visible jitter in the 3D twin mostly through ball/player heights; since the user prioritises plausibility over per-frame accuracy, option 1/2 (piecewise-constant cameras with constants shared across the match) is the default, with option 3 only for shots that fail the staticness test.
- (Inference) Hand-held/jib/replay angles should not be calibrated with the main-camera prior; either skip them (the task only needs main-camera rallies) or calibrate independently with the per-frame method.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates temporal calibration smoothness quantitatively (BroadTrack's jitter evidence is qualitative), and none addresses racket-sport broadcasts specifically.
- I found no evidence on how much the PPA/MLP main camera actually pans/zooms during rallies; this must be measured on the target footage (it determines whether option 1 suffices).
- BroadTrack's code (github.com/evs-broadcast/BroadTrack) is soccer-specific and was benchmarked on CUDA; licence, weights and Apple Silicon behaviour were not checked.

## 5. Parsing broadcast video into rallies: shot boundaries, main-camera classification, replay/graphics filtering, rally start/end, scoreboard OCR

### Takeaway
Shot-boundary detection is a solved-enough commodity (TransNetV2 remains competitive with AutoShot in a 2025 benchmark; OmniShotCut, April 2026, adds transition/relation labels), and recent racket-sport datasets segment rallies with cheap cues rather than heavy models: scoreboard-change detection (table tennis datasets), audio hit detection + view filtering + MLLM scoreboard reading (TennisExpert, 2026), or ball-trajectory/hit segmentation (TT3D, MonoTrack). No pickleball-specific parsing work was found.

### Cited Findings

**Shot-boundary detection**
- NVIDIA's Cosmos paper (arXiv 2501.03575, Jan 2025) built a ShotBench evaluation (RAI, BBC Planet Earth, ClipShots, SHOT) comparing PySceneDetect, Panda70M's splitter, TransNetV2 and AutoShot at a 0.4 confidence threshold; on BBC TransNetV2 scored F1 0.967 vs AutoShot 0.952; the authors say the two neural methods are comparable on existing datasets but TransNetV2 handled harder shot changes better. — [Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03575)
- AutoShot (CVPR 2023, arXiv 2304.06116) introduced the SHOT short-video dataset and claims +4.2% F1 over TransNetV2 on SHOT. — [AutoShot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.06116)
- OmniShotCut (arXiv 2604.24762, first posted late April 2026, revised May 2026; NeurIPS 2026 poster **[NEW]**): shot-query Transformer that jointly predicts shot ranges, intra-shot transition style (dissolve, wipe, fade, zoom, push) and inter-shot relation (transition, hard cut, sudden jump); trained on a fully synthetic transition pipeline; introduces OmniShotCutBench. An aggregator lists transition IoU 63.2 for OmniShotCut vs 25.2 for AutoShot on that benchmark (aggregator figures, not checked against the paper). Project page listed as Omni-Shot-Cut.github.io; no verified code repository found. — [arXiv 2604.24762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24762); [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/150480)

**Rally segmentation in racket-sport datasets**
- TennisExpert (arXiv 2603.13397, 17 Mar 2026 **[NEW]**): (1) a lightweight audio-only network detects racquet-ball impact sounds, trained with crowd/clapping/announcer background mixed in; impacts are clustered in time into candidate rally intervals; (2) candidate clips are kept only if they show the standard broadcast top-down court view and a visible scoreboard; (3) the scoreboard is cropped and read on each clip's centre frame with a prompt-guided MLLM using tournament-specific templates. 202 broadcast matches / 471.9 h -> 40,523 rally clips. Architecture of the audio model, the MLLM name and segmentation/OCR accuracy are not reported. Code/data: github.com/LZYAndy/TennisExpert. — [TennisExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13397)
- RacketVision (arXiv 2511.17045, Nov 2025; AAAI, published 14 Mar 2026 **[NEW]**): 1,672 clips / 435,179 frames across badminton, tennis and table tennis from 942 professional YouTube broadcasts, annotated with ball positions and racket boxes + 5 racket keypoints (not court keypoints). Code: github.com/OrcustD/RacketVision. — [RacketVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37362)
- Table-tennis broadcast datasets cut rally clips automatically by recognising scoreboard changes (ViSTec: 18 games -> about 4,000 rally clips). — [ViSTec](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15952)
- A December 2025 table tennis paper **[NEW]** detects rallies and estimates scores by combining YOLOv8 (player/table detection) with SAM for scoreboard-area segmentation (reported via search summary only; primary URL not captured). — [search summary via RacketVision topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/racketvision)
- TT3D segments at the trajectory level: ball detected with BlurBall; trajectory split into second-degree polynomial segments by dynamic programming (fit cost + per-segment penalty + motion-blur consistency); bounces at segment intersections; racket strikes vs table bounces distinguished by reversal along the table's long axis. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)
- Badminton pipelines segment by hit frames; HitNet from MonoTrack can detect hit frames when not labelled. — [BST](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21085v2)
- Event spotting in TennisExpert builds on F3Set with near/far-court bounce categories, 224x224 clips, GRU temporal head, test Edit Score 81.2. — [TennisExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13397)
- Survey of precise event spotting datasets/methods in sports (May 2025). — [Action Spotting and Precise Event Detection in Sports](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03991v1)

### Inferences
Proposed zero/low-training parsing stack for PPA/MLP YouTube broadcasts (all inference; none of this is validated on pickleball in any source I found):
1. **Shot boundaries:** TransNetV2 (PyTorch weights exist in the upstream repo — prior knowledge, not re-verified) on 48x27 frames is tiny and should run on MPS or CPU far faster than real time; fall back to PySceneDetect's content detector for a dependency-free baseline. Sports replays are typically bracketed by logo wipes/stingers, which hard-cut detectors miss or double-fire on; OmniShotCut's transition labels are aimed at this but its code availability is unconfirmed.
2. **Main-camera view classification — use the calibrator itself as the classifier.** Run the court keypoint model on a few frames per shot and accept the shot as "main camera" iff the solved camera lies within tolerance of the match's main-camera pose (camera centre, tilt, focal range) with low reprojection error. This is geometrically strict, needs no extra training, and rejects close-ups, crowd shots, low-angle replays and full-screen graphics for free. Zero-shot alternatives: CLIP/SigLIP image-text scoring ("elevated behind-the-baseline view of a full pickleball court") or nearest-neighbour on DINOv2/v3 embeddings against a handful of exemplar frames.
3. **Replay filtering:** replays shot from the main camera angle will pass the geometry test. Cues: (a) replays sit between logo-wipe transitions; (b) scoreboard overlay absent or a "REPLAY" bug present; (c) slow motion detectable from ball/player kinematics or duplicated frames; (d) the audio hit-sound cadence disappears or is slowed. Combining "scoreboard present" with the geometry test mirrors TennisExpert's filter (standard court view + visible scoreboard).
4. **Rally start/end:** within a main-camera shot, combine (a) audio paddle-hit onset detection — pickleball's paddle "pop" is loud and broadband, so TennisExpert's audio-first approach should transfer well, though it needs a small labelled set or a spectral-flux onset heuristic; (b) ball track presence/motion; (c) player pose (serve stance at baseline, all four players static before serve). End-of-rally = last hit/bounce + margin, or the cut away from the main camera. Scoreboard change confirms that a point (or side-out) occurred between consecutive candidates.
5. **Scoreboard OCR:** locate the overlay once per match (it is static in screen space: per-pixel temporal variance over main-camera frames isolates it), crop, and read it at one frame per rally with a local VLM or classical OCR (Apple's Vision framework OCR is on-device on macOS; PaddleOCR/EasyOCR run on CPU). Pickleball doubles scores are three numbers (serving score - receiving score - server number) in traditional side-out scoring, while MLP uses rally scoring; a prompt-guided VLM with a per-tour template (as TennisExpert does per tournament) handles layout differences. Use score-transition consistency (scores change by legal increments only) as an error filter.
- (Inference) Order of operations: shot detection -> per-shot geometry gate -> per-match camera solve -> rally detection inside gated shots -> scoreboard read per rally. Because the geometry gate needs the court detector anyway, the incremental cost of main-view classification is near zero.

### Gaps
- **No pickleball-specific rally segmentation, replay detection or scoreboard OCR work was found** (papers or maintained open-source tools). Everything above is transferred from tennis/table tennis/badminton.
- No accuracy numbers exist for TennisExpert's audio rally detector or its scoreboard reader, so the reliability of audio-first segmentation is undocumented even for tennis.
- I found no source on TransNetV2/AutoShot/OmniShotCut running on MPS or MLX specifically; search returned only generic PyTorch-MPS documentation.
- I found no 2025-2026 dedicated replay-detection or logo-transition-detection model for sports broadcasts (only old patents surfaced).
- OmniShotCut's code/weights availability is unverified, and its benchmark figures come from an aggregator.
- The December 2025 YOLOv8 + SAM table-tennis rally/score paper was seen only as a search-summary description; I could not capture its primary URL or verify details.

## 6. Court-keypoint datasets and pretrained models for tennis/badminton/pickleball; zero-shot transfer across court types

### Takeaway
Tennis has a solid public dataset and pretrained detector (TennisCourtDetector: 8,841 broadcast frames, 14 keypoints, about 2 px median error); pickleball has only small community Roboflow datasets (hundreds to about 1,100 images) and forum reports of YOLO-pose models struggling with occluded keypoints. No evidence was found that any court-keypoint model transfers zero-shot across court types — keypoint semantics and counts differ by sport — so a pickleball-specific fine-tune on PPA/MLP frames is needed, which is cheap because camera placement is highly standardised.

### Cited Findings
- TennisCourtDetector: 8,841 images at 1280x720 from YouTube tournament highlights (frames every 50 frames, labelled by a classical CV algorithm and manually filtered), 75/25 split, 14 keypoints; hard/clay/grass courts; pretrained weights via Google Drive; post-processing by line-intersection refinement and homography reprojection lifts 7-px accuracy from 0.933 to 0.961 and median error from 2.83 px to 1.83 px. License not stated. — [GitHub yastrebksv/TennisCourtDetector](https://github.com/yastrebksv/TennisCourtDetector)
- Roboflow Universe hosts a "pickleball court" keypoint detection project by "Ping Pong Paddle AI with Images" described in search results as a YOLOv11 keypoint model trained on 288 images with a hosted API, plus a YOLOv11s-pose variant (the page returned HTTP 403 to my fetch, so image counts/metrics are from the search snippet only). — [Roboflow Universe: pickleball court keypoints](https://universe.roboflow.com/ping-pong-paddle-ai-with-images/pickleball-court-p3chl-7tufp)
- A Roboflow forum thread describes a pickleball court keypoint dataset of about 1,135 annotated images whose Roboflow 3.0 and YOLOv11 keypoint models struggled; Roboflow staff attributed failures to occluded keypoints and suggested more occlusion examples and grayscale augmentation. — [Roboflow forum: Pickleball Court Keypoints](https://discuss.roboflow.com/t/pickleball-court-keypoints/10685)
- An Ultralytics forum user trained a YOLOv8-pose model with 12 keypoints per pickleball court (baseline to kitchen line on each half; index 0 = background baseline left ... 11 = foreground baseline left), with the index order mattering for flip augmentation. — [Ultralytics forum: Detecting court keypoints from a pickleball video](https://community.ultralytics.com/t/detecting-court-keypoints-from-a-pickleball-video/1703/3)
- Roboflow Universe also lists a "Pickleball Court" instance-segmentation model (Hughs Workspace) reporting mAP@50 99.5% (court as polygon, not keypoints), a "pickleballtrain/pickleball-court-keypoint" project, and several generic "court keypoint" projects (val-aospz, CC BY 4.0; DataMentors) with no descriptions. — [Roboflow: Pickleball Court segmentation](https://universe.roboflow.com/hughs-workspace-plw3g/pickleball-court-cfyv4/model/1); [Roboflow search: pickleball-court-keypoint](https://universe.roboflow.com/search?q=like%3Apickleballtrain%2Fpickleball-court-keypoint-sc9an+keypoint+detection); [val-aospz court-keypoint](https://universe.roboflow.com/val-aospz/court-keypoint); [DataMentors court-keypoints](https://universe.roboflow.com/datamentors/court-keypoints-uwjr0)
- CourtSI **[NEW, 10 Mar 2026]** includes a pickleball extension set (CourtSI-Ext) with cameras calibrated from manually annotated court corners + net points; code at github.com/Visionary-Laboratory/CourtSI. This is the only academic dataset found with calibrated pickleball imagery. — [CourtSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09896)
- RacketVision **[NEW]** (badminton/tennis/table tennis, 942 broadcasts) annotates balls and rackets, not court keypoints. — [RacketVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17045v3)
- TennisExpert **[NEW]** uses a 14-keypoint heatmap court model on 202 broadcast matches; code/dataset public. — [TennisExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13397)
- TT3D avoids keypoint regression entirely: table segmentation mask (0.92 mIoU, about 70 fps) -> contours + probabilistic Hough -> 4 corners at 2.39 +/- 1.47 px. — [TT3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10035v1)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Zero-shot transfer of a *keypoint* model across sports is ill-posed: output channels are tied to sport-specific semantics (tennis's 14 points include doubles alleys and service boxes; pickleball has NVZ lines and a centreline that stops at the NVZ). Tennis weights are useful only as initialisation for the backbone/heatmap decoder. Expect a tennis detector applied to pickleball to output confident but meaningless points.
- (Inference) What transfers better across court types is *class-agnostic line evidence*: line/edge segmentation or classical white-line extraction + Hough, followed by template fitting with the known pickleball model (PnLCalib-style point-to-line refinement). A hybrid is the robust design: a small fine-tuned keypoint net for initialisation + line-based refinement for sub-pixel accuracy (TennisCourtDetector's own post-processing shows the gain: 2.83 -> 1.83 px median).
- (Inference) Cheap dataset bootstrapping for PPA/MLP: because the main camera is static per match, manually click about 14 court points + net-post tops + net-tape centre on *one* frame per match/court, solve the camera, and reproject the template to auto-label every main-camera frame of that match (thousands of frames with occlusion variety for free). A few dozen matches give a TennisCourtDetector-scale dataset with minimal labour. This also directly addresses the Roboflow forum's occlusion failure mode, since labels for occluded points come from geometry.
- (Inference) The user's ResNet50 4-corner regressor is the weakest of the available designs: direct coordinate regression is less precise than heatmaps, 4 points give zero redundancy (no RANSAC, no outlier rejection, no focal/distortion estimation), and the near-baseline corners are the ones most often occluded or cropped. Moving to about 14 keypoints + net points + lines with heatmap outputs is the single biggest accuracy lever, ahead of any temporal smoothing.
- (Inference) Pro pickleball venues often have multi-sport line clutter or coloured NVZ/"kitchen" zones and sponsor logos on court; colour-zone boundaries can be an additional strong cue (zone segmentation edges) where painted lines are low-contrast.
- (Inference) Apple Silicon: HRNet/TrackNet/YOLO-pose scale models train and infer comfortably on MPS with 64 GB; Ultralytics supports `device="mps"` (prior knowledge, not re-verified).

### Gaps
- I could not open any Roboflow Universe dataset page directly (HTTP 403), so image counts, keypoint schemas, licences, and whether images are broadcast-angle or amateur/phone footage are unverified for all pickleball datasets listed. Community Roboflow pickleball data is likely dominated by amateur/phone angles (inference), which would limit usefulness for PPA/MLP broadcast.
- No public dataset of **PPA/MLP broadcast** frames with court keypoints was found.
- No study quantifies cross-court-type transfer (tennis -> pickleball or badminton -> pickleball) for keypoint detectors; the "does not transfer zero-shot" conclusion is reasoned, not measured.
- No public badminton court keypoint dataset/model was confirmed in this session (MonoTrack claims improved court recognition, but its abstract page lists no code).
- I did not inspect the CourtSI repository to confirm that the pickleball extension images and their calibration annotations are actually downloadable, or under what licence.
