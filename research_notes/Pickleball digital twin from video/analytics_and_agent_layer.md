## 1. Data models and formats for tracking and event data; a pickleball schema

### Takeaway
Every mature racket-sport dataset uses the same backbone: match > game/set > rally > stroke, with per-stroke hit time/frame, hitter, shot type, hitter and opponent positions, and landing location; continuous tracking (ball and pose) sits in a separate frame-indexed layer. Pickleball already has one public stroke-level relational schema (pklmart: tournament/match/game/rally/shot/shot_type_ref/player/team) and one commercial JSON schema (PB Vision "insights" and "stats"), so the twin's event layer should be made mappable to both.

### Cited Findings

Badminton (the closest precedent for a stroke-level schema)
- ShuttleSet (KDD 2023): 36,492 strokes, 3,685 rallies, 104 sets, 44 matches (2018 to 2021), 27 top men's and women's singles players, human-annotated. Per-stroke fields described in the paper: hit time and video frame of the hit, stroke status (serve / return / dead bird), backhand flag, "above the head" (around-the-head) flag, shot type from 18 classes, hitting location, and the locations of both players at each stroke. [V] — [ShuttleSet, arXiv 2306.04948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04948)
- ShuttleSet22 is the 2022 follow-up used for stroke-forecasting benchmarks (CoachAI Badminton Challenge). [V] — [Benchmarking Stroke Forecasting, arXiv 2306.15664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15664)
- The CoachAI code and data live in one repository. [M] — [CoachAI-Projects on GitHub](https://github.com/wywyWang/CoachAI-Projects)

Tennis
- Hawk-Eye ball and player tracking has been the input to tennis shot-value work (2019 US Open data in Kovalchik et al.); a Grand Slam distance study notes that Hawk-Eye's player-tracking accuracy is unpublished. [V] — [Space-Time VON CRAMM, arXiv 2005.12853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12853); [JSSM 23(1)](https://jssm.org/23-1-1.p_d_f)
- TennisTV (September 2025, [NEW], month from search summary) represents a rally as an ordered sequence of strokes, movements, directions and outcomes; about 1,298 rally videos and 2,500 human-verified questions. [V] — [TennisTV topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/tennistv)
- The Match Charting Project is a crowd-sourced, shot-by-shot text-coded tennis dataset (serve direction, shot type, direction, depth, error type per shot). [M] — [tennis_MatchChartingProject on GitHub](https://github.com/JeffSackmann/tennis_MatchChartingProject)

Soccer (useful for the tracking-plus-event split, not for the taxonomy)
- kloppy is the open-source Python standardizer that loads vendor tracking and event feeds (StatsBomb, SkillCorner, Metrica, Tracab and others) into one data model with a common coordinate system and separate `TrackingDataset` and `EventDataset` objects. [M] — [kloppy on GitHub](https://github.com/PySport/kloppy)

Pickleball
- pklmart (Alex Spancake) is a manually charted pro-pickleball relational dataset; the `pklshop` Python package exposes eight tables: tournament, match, game, rally, shot_type_ref, shot, player, team. Only the `team` columns are shown on the docs landing page (team_id, player_id, player_seq_nbr, team_nm, plus audit columns). [V] — [pklshop docs](https://nolansmyth.github.io/pklshop/index.html)
- A third-party analysis of pklmart describes: a shot table with an original and a standardized shot-type column plus location fields; a rally table that records the third-shot type, rally length, per-side speed-up and lob counts (`speedup_count_S`, `speedup_count_R`) and dink counts (`serve_dink_count`, `return_dink_count`); the data-entry flow also records which side hit the third, stacking, and rally ending type. [V] — [Caleb Hyun, "How do I win a Pickleball Point?"](https://calebhyun.github.io/pickleball-point-analysis); [pklmart data-entry tutorial](https://pickleballarchives.blogspot.com/2023/11/getting-started-with-pklmart-data-entry.html)
- A pklmart extract is on Kaggle ("games, rallies, shots, players"); a Reddit thread reported that one upload was missing `w_team_id` and `ending_type` in the rally table. [V] — [Kaggle: pklmart's Competitive Pickleball Extracts](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/cakesofspan/pklmarts-competitive-pickleball-extracts); [Reddit thread](https://libreddit.winscloud.net/r/Pickleball/comments/1k0tub9/here_is_a_bunch_of_competitive_pickleball_data)
- A dashboard built on pklmart-style shot data claims 304K shots across 10 skill tiers (updated 2026-05-01, [NEW]). I did not open the page; the figure is from a search summary. [V, weak] — [Pickleball Strategy Lab](https://pickleball-strategy-lab.vercel.app/)
- PB Vision partner API: webhook JSON with top-level `cv` ("low-level frame-by-frame data"), `insights` (shot-by-shot description of the game), `stats`, `vid`, `aiEngineVersion`, `error`. Public schema pages exist for insights and stats; insights can be fetched as compact or `?format=augmented`. Players carry an `avatar_id`; player indices are 0 to 3 in doubles, consistent within a video but not across videos. Video requirements: H.264, 30 or 60 FPS, max 30 minutes, must follow framing guidelines. [V] — [PB Vision Partner Documentation](https://pbv-public.github.io/partner-sdk-nodejs/); [insights schema](https://pbv-public.github.io/insights); [stats schema](https://pbv-public.github.io/stats?s=~stats~game)
- A third-party tool built on PB Vision lists the shot categories as: serve, drive, lob, smash, dink, drop, reset, volley, speed-up, return, ATP, erne, other. This is not from PB Vision's own docs. [V, third-party] — [rallyiq](https://rallyiq.pages.dev/)
- DUPR is a proprietary rating (nominally 2.0 to 8.0, Elo-like with margin of victory); it is a rating system, not a shot-level stat taxonomy. [V] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz, "Pickleball Analytics"](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)
- Court and equipment constants: court 44 ft by 20 ft; net 36 in at the sidelines and 34 in at the centre; non-volley zone ("kitchen") extends 7 ft from the net on each side; doubles games to 11, win by 2, only the serving team scores; the serve and the return must both bounce (two-bounce rule). [V] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)

### Inferences (proposed schema; my design, not from a source)
Three layers, kept strictly separate so the agent can tell measurement from interpretation:

1. **Primitives (frame-indexed, columnar, e.g. Parquet)**: `ball_frames(match_id, frame, t, x, y, z, sigma_x, sigma_y, sigma_z, visible, source)`; `pose_frames(match_id, frame, t, player_id, joint, x, y, z, conf)`; `calibration(match_id, segment_id, K, R, t, reproj_err)`. Court frame: origin at net centre on the ground, x across (plus or minus 10 ft), y along (plus or minus 22 ft), z up; SI units internally. Store per-sample uncertainty from day one; retrofitting it is hard.
2. **Atomic events (derived, each with `t`, `frame`, `confidence`, `detector_version`)**: `contact(hitter_id, ball_xyz, paddle_side fh/bh, contact_height, is_volley, hitter_feet_xy, in_nvz_at_contact)`; `bounce(xy, in_out, margin_m, margin_sigma)`; `net_cross(height_over_net, lateral_pos, speed)`; `rally_end(reason: winner | error_net | error_out | fault_nvz | fault_double_bounce | fault_serve | unknown)`.
3. **Semantic layer**: `rally(rally_id, game_id, server_id, serving_team, score_before, side_out_flag, n_shots, duration, winner_team, ending_type, stacking_flags)`; `shot(shot_id, rally_id, shot_idx, hitter_id, contact_event_id, bounce_event_id, shot_type, shot_type_std, speed_out, apex_z, net_clearance, landing_xy, landing_depth_zone, direction: cross/line/middle, target_player_id, pressure_in, pressure_out, outcome)`.
   - `shot_type` enum that is a superset of pklmart and the PB Vision-derived list: serve, return, third_drop, third_drive, third_lob, drop (later transition drops, i.e. 5th/7th), drive, dink, speed_up, counter, reset, volley_block, lob, overhead/smash, ATP, erne, tweener/other.
   - Because serve/return/third are positional (shot_idx 1/2/3) while drop/drive/dink/lob are kinematic, store both `shot_idx` and a kinematic class rather than one merged label; "third-shot drop" is then a query (`shot_idx = 3 AND kinematic = drop`). This mirrors pklmart recording third-shot type on the rally and PB Vision-style per-shot labels.
   - Kitchen violations: derive from `is_volley AND` (any foot keypoint inside the NVZ at contact, or momentum carrying the player in within some window after). Flag as *candidate* faults; foot-on-line calls at centimetre scale are below monocular pose precision (see section 5).
- Keep the pklmart column names where they exist (`rally`, `shot`, `shot_type_ref`, `w_team_id`, `ending_type`) so public pro data can be joined for priors and base rates.

- I could not retrieve the full pklmart column dictionary or the `shot_type_ref` values (Kaggle page returned only a title; pklshop landing page shows only `team`). Install `pklshop` and dump `shot_type_ref` directly.

## 2. Deriving semantic events from trajectories and poses

### Takeaway
Hit and bounce detection are done either with velocity-change heuristics on the ball track or with small GRU models over ball position plus court coordinates plus player pose; stroke classification works best from skeleton sequences fused with the ball trajectory (BST). Value models (expected shot value, shot-by-shot Markov models) and reachable-region models exist but were built on multi-camera Hawk-Eye-grade tracking, so with monocular input they need coarser states and explicit uncertainty.

### Cited Findings

Hit, bounce and shot segmentation
- Prior methods "either use heuristics on the change in velocity or GRU networks" to detect bounce and hit frames; inputs are court coordinates, ball image positions and player pose. MonoTrack (CVPRW 2022) combines court, pose and shuttle positions to segment badminton video into shots. Input fidelity: single broadcast camera. [V] — [MonoTrack, arXiv 2204.01899](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2204.01899)
- TT3D (table tennis, arXiv April 2025, v2 dated 2025-06-25) uses a GRU-based hit-and-bounce detector to segment broadcast video into shots, then fits a physics model per segment by minimizing reprojection error. It notes that a 40 ms bounce-timing error can move the bounce position by up to 40 cm at 10 m/s. [V] — [TT3D, arXiv 2504.10035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10035)
- "Where Is The Ball" (CVPRW 2025, tennis and others): argues that physics pipelines requiring the whole trajectory to be pre-segmented into projectiles are fragile because that segmentation "is highly error-prone"; proposes learned 2D-to-3D lifting evaluated on synthetic and mocap data. [V] — [Ponglertnapakorn et al., CVPRW 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/CVSPORTS/papers/Ponglertnapakorn_Where_Is_The_Ball_3D_Ball_Trajectory_Estimation_From_2D_CVPRW_2025_paper.pdf)
- [NEW] "Uplifting Table Tennis" (arXiv 2511.20250, November 2025): states that existing approaches are susceptible to errors from inaccurate key-event identification, explicit calibration and poor video quality, and that earlier broadcast work lacks robustness to detector failures and varying frame rates. [V] — [arXiv 2511.20250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20250)
- Automated hit-frame detection for badminton (2023). [V, title only] — [arXiv 2307.16000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16000)
- An open-source pickleball pipeline groups hits into rallies with a simple rule: a gap of more than 2.5 s between hits starts a new rally. It also claims hits, bounces, shot types, rule faults and stroke form from pose. Hobby project, not peer reviewed. [V] — [ArvanJ/Pickleball-Vision](https://github.com/ArvanJ/Pickleball-Vision)

Shot-type classification
- BST (Badminton Stroke-type Transformer, arXiv 2502.21085, February 2025): clips each swing from broadcast footage, extracts skeleton joints, adds the shuttle trajectory, and classifies stroke type; the authors argue ball trajectory is a promising signal for racket-sport action recognition. Input fidelity: single broadcast camera, 2D pose plus 2D shuttle track. [V] — [BST, arXiv 2502.21085](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21085v3)
- [NEW] RacketVision (arXiv 2511.17045, November 2025): a multi-racket-sport benchmark for unified ball and racket analysis. I did not read its contents. [V, title only] — [arXiv 2511.17045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17045)
- A Hugging Face project claims 11 pickleball shot classes (dink, third-shot drop, erne, and others) built from TOTNet (arXiv 2508.09650) for ball tracking and BST for classification. Unverified hobby project. [V, weak] — [racquet-sports-analyzer](https://huggingface.co/Bot-Derpy/racquet-sports-analyzer)

Value, pressure and expected-outcome models
- Kovalchik et al., "Space-Time VON CRAMM" (2020): generative model of full-resolution ball and player trajectories (infinite Bayesian Gaussian mixture), conditioned on observed positions, then predicts shot outcome from a functional encoding. Outputs: expected shot value (ESV), value added with shot taking (VAST), Shot IQ, and value added with court coverage (VACC), separating execution, selection and movement. Input fidelity: Hawk-Eye multi-camera tracking, 2019 US Open. [V] — [arXiv 2005.12853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12853)
- Floyd et al. (2019) assigned shot value with a Markov model whose states are coarsened ball and player locations at one time point; a CMU capstone extends it with more of the Hawk-Eye detail. [V] — [CMU capstone report](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2020/66-502-Emery.pdf)
- "Shot-by-shot stochastic modeling of individual tennis points" (JQAS 2020): expected points from each shot given hitter and both players' locations. [V] — [record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4059523)
- Wei et al. (Disney Research): forecasting next shot location in tennis from Hawk-Eye spatiotemporal data; related work estimated which incoming-shot location and speed factors favour a winner. [V] — [Disney Research page](https://la.disneyresearch.com/?p=11305)
- Muthukumarana and Swartz ([NEW]; exact date not shown, cites a source accessed 2025-11-29): a law-of-total-probability framework for comparing pickleball strategies, P(W_A) = sum over partition of P(W_A | A_j) P(A_j). Worked examples with *assumed* (not measured) probabilities: "mild creep" after the serve gives 0.47 vs 0.40 for not creeping; drop vs lob comparison where lobs come out comparable or better under several settings. The authors state the main obstacle is "the lack of data available to inform the component probabilities". [V, read in full] — [Pickleball Analytics: Research Directions for a Fledgling Sport](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)
- Steyn et al. (2025), "Executing a successful third shot drop in pickleball" (Int. J. Sports Science and Coaching): successful drops depend more on speed and angle than on spin. Emond, Sun and Swartz (2024), "Pickleball flight dynamics" (Int. J. Racket Sports Science 6(2)): includes wind. Both are cited in the Swartz paper; I did not open them. [V, secondary] — [via Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf); DOI given there: https://doi.org/10.1177/17479541251365200

Reach, time-to-ball and court coverage
- Football "pitch control" work defines controlled space as the area a player can reach before any other player under a motion model, and validates motion models by how often the predicted reachable area contains the true later position and how small that area is. [V] — [Validation and Optimisation of Player Motion Models, ICCS 2022](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2022/papers/133510022.pdf); [arXiv 2204.13992](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2204.13992)
- A data-driven alternative: approximate a player's reachable region at time horizon t by the convex polygon of all points that player historically reached within t. A table tennis paper adapts large-field reachability models to estimate the regions a player can effectively return from. [V, from search summary; I could not pin which of the listed links holds each claim] — [Springer ML journal article](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10994-018-5725-1.pdf); [Eurographics DL record](https://diglib.eg.org/signposting/describedby/6c301aae-0024-46f0-94a5-ecfb67f72019)

### Inferences
- Recommended detector stack for noisy monocular input: (a) candidate events from kinematic discontinuities in the fitted 3D ball track (velocity-direction reversal along y = contact; vertical velocity sign flip near z = ball radius = bounce); (b) confirm contacts with wrist/paddle-hand proximity from pose; (c) apply rule constraints as a decoder (hitters alternate teams; exactly one bounce allowed; serve and return must bounce). The rules make pickleball rallies a constrained sequence, so a Viterbi-style decode over candidate events will fix many single-frame misses.
- Fit piecewise ballistic arcs (gravity plus drag; a pickleball is light and draggy, so drag is not optional) between contacts and bounces and derive net clearance, apex, landing point and speed from the fitted arc rather than from raw per-frame 3D points. TT3D's 40 ms / 40 cm sensitivity figure is the reason: at 30 fps one frame is 33 ms.
- Derived metric library that position plus pose supports: contact height and contact point relative to the hitter's torso (reach used), is_volley, feet relative to the NVZ line, split-step timing, time from opponent contact to own contact (time pressure), distance travelled in that window, lateral and forward recovery position at opponent contact, team spacing and gap between partners, net clearance and apex, landing depth zone, ball speed at contact out, "attackability" (ball height at the receiver's contact relative to net height), and transition-zone dwell time.
- A simple pressure score that is robust to noise: time available divided by time needed (distance to interception point over a player-specific speed), plus contact-height-below-net flag. Avoid full ESV-style generative models until there are thousands of rallies; a logistic or gradient-boosted P(rally win | state at contact) on coarse features is the realistic first expected-point model.
- For reach: use the empirical convex-hull idea per player (all contact points relative to the player's position and time available), since it needs no biomechanical constants and self-calibrates to the system's own noise.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed pickleball-specific paper on hit detection, bounce detection or shot classification, and no public pickleball tracking dataset with 3D ball and pose. Roboflow Universe is said (by a hobby repo) to host ball-detection image sets; I did not verify them.
- No published in/out call accuracy figures for single-camera pickleball systems were found.
- Padel and squash event-derivation papers were not covered this session.
- The specific tennis papers on time-to-ball / reachable region were not individually opened; the two links in that bullet need confirmation.

## 3. Tactical pattern mining for racket sports

### Takeaway
The field treats a rally as a multivariate event sequence and mines it three ways: frequent multivariate sequential patterns (visual analytics systems from the Zhejiang group), stochastic/Markov shot-transition models with a value attached to states, and learned stroke forecasting (ShuttleNet and successors). Findings are typically of the form "tactic X (a 2 to 4 stroke pattern) is used N times with win rate p, and its use changes over the match", which is exactly the user's "when this pattern occurs, this outcome is most likely".

### Cited Findings
- Wu et al., "Visual Analytics of Multivariate Event Sequence Data in Racquet Sports" (IEEE VAST 2020): steerable multivariate sequential pattern mining with adjustable weights over event attributes and reconfigurable glyphs; applied to tennis and badminton datasets. [V] — [IEEE VIS 2020 page](https://virtual.ieeevis.org/year/2020/paper_f-vast-1010.html)
- TacticFlow (IEEE VIS 2021 / TVCG 2022): models a rally as a multivariate event sequence, mines tactics, and shows how tactic use shifts over a match with a glyph-based Sankey diagram; evaluated with two case studies and four domain experts in tennis and badminton. [V] — [IEEE VIS 2021 page](https://content.ieeevis.org/year/2021/paper_v-full-1135.html); [PubMed 34587062](https://www.pubmed.gov/34587062/)
- ShuttleNet (AAAI 2022): stroke forecasting as sequence prediction; given the first 4 observed strokes, predicts shot type and landing coordinates of subsequent strokes; two encoder-decoder extractors (rally progress, player style) with a position-aware fusion network. [V] — [arXiv 2112.01044](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.01044); [AAAI proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20341)
- CoachAI Badminton Challenge 2023 (IJCAI): winning entry was an "advanced ShuttleNet". [V] — [arXiv 2307.13715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13715)
- Markov shot-value models in tennis (Floyd et al. 2019) use coarsened ball and player location states; see section 2. [V] — [CMU capstone report](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2020/66-502-Emery.pdf)
- Tennis statistical modelling precedents listed by Swartz: Klaassen and Magnus (2001) found points are not i.i.d. (positive correlation; servers do worse on important points); Tea and Swartz (2023) used Bayesian hierarchical models for serve tendencies; Kovalchik and Albert (2022) a semiparametric mixture model of serve-return impact patterns; Epasinghege Dona, Gill and Swartz (2025) a model of rally length; Peiris et al. (2025) on unforced errors. [V, secondary] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf); [Kovalchik and Albert, arXiv 2202.00583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00583)
- Pickleball findings from manually charted data: Gandhi (2024, The Dink) reports win percentages for deep vs shallow returns, with deep returns preferred and more beneficial at higher skill levels. Gill and Swartz (2019) find doubles pickleball is a "strong link" sport (outcomes depend more on the stronger partner). [V, secondary] — [via Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf); [The Dink article](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/do-deep-returns-win-more-rallies-what-about-drives-vs-drops/)
- From prior knowledge, not re-verified this session [M]: TenniVis (Polk et al., TVCG 2014; score/serve-based match visualization from non-tracking data); iTTVis (2018), Tac-Simur (TVCG 2020; simulation of tactic changes on a Markov model of table tennis), Tac-Miner (TVCG 2021; three-stroke tactic mining), RallyComparator (2021/2022; comparing rallies), all table tennis from the same Zhejiang University group; iBall (CHI 2023; gaze-aware augmented basketball video, not racket sports) — [iBall, arXiv 2303.03476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03476). I have no checked URLs for the others; cite only after confirming.

### Inferences
- For doubles pickleball the natural "tactic" unit is a short window (2 to 4 shots) of tuples (shot_type, hitter role, direction, landing zone, target player), the same shape the VAST 2020 / TacticFlow miners use. A weighted-attribute miner lets the user ask for patterns "ignoring direction" or "ignoring who hit".
- A first-order Markov model over a small phase-state space (serve, return, third, transition, all-four-at-kitchen dinking, speed-up/firefight, lob/scramble, end) with absorbing win/lose states is estimable from tens of matches and gives interpretable outputs: P(win | state), expected shots to end, and what-if simulation in the Tac-Simur style.
- Stroke forecasting models (ShuttleNet class) need tens of thousands of strokes; ShuttleSet has about 36k. A pro pickleball doubles match has a few hundred rallies at most, so learned forecasting only becomes viable once roughly 50+ matches are reconstructed, or by pre-training on pklmart shot sequences.
- Klaassen and Magnus's non-i.i.d. result matters for the user's claims: rallies within a game are correlated, so resample by game or match (cluster bootstrap), not by rally.

### Gaps
- No pickleball-specific sequence-mining or rally-forecasting paper was found.
- I did not find (in this session) 2025 to 2026 successors to TacticFlow / RallyComparator; whether newer LLM-assisted visual-analytics systems for racket sports exist is unconfirmed.
- Specific numeric findings from the visual-analytics case studies were not extracted.

## 4. LLM agents over sports data (2024 to November 2026)

### Takeaway
The published systems that work put an LLM in front of structured data through tools (schema-only text-to-SQL, entity-resolution plus grounded retrieval, role-specific agents with a verifier) rather than putting raw data or video in context; general video LLMs do badly on rally understanding. I found no published system that does free-form QA directly over raw high-frequency tracking data, and general data-agent benchmarks from 2026 show the dominant failures are statistical (wrong method, wrong interpretation), not coding.

### Cited Findings

Sports-specific agents and benchmarks
- [NEW] SportSQL (arXiv 2508.17157, August 2025): natural-language questions over live soccer statistics; the LLM sees the schema (not the data), writes SQL, it is executed, and a second code-generating LLM writes self-contained Matplotlib code for charts. Releases DSQABench of questions paired with executable SQL. [V] — [SportSQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17157v1)
- SoccerRAG (arXiv 2406.01273, June 2024): LLM extracts properties from the question, then a SQL agent queries a database built from SoccerNet (captions, event annotations, player info; not positional tracking). [V] — [SoccerRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01273v1)
- [NEW] "A Multi-agent Service for Football Data Querying" (Springer chapter, appeared roughly September 2026 per the search summary): four crewAI agents: entity resolution, grounded retrieval from PostgreSQL match data, visual summary generation, response writing. [V] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-28160-9_12)
- SoccerAgent / "Multi-Agent System for Comprehensive Soccer Understanding" (arXiv 2505.03735, May 2025): multi-agent soccer QA; I could not confirm its tool design from the snippet. [V, weak] — [arXiv 2505.03735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03735)
- [NEW] COACH (arXiv 2512.01853, 2025-12-01), badminton, read in full: Orchestrator / Grounder / Critic agents on a shared Flan-T5-XL backbone with a TC-CLIP video encoder and Q-Former, plus retriever and media tools; two fixed SOPs (analytical rally QA, video summarization). Trained on ShuttleSet-derived captions; evaluated on COACH-Data (22 matches, about 19,000 strokes). Reported vs Gemini 2.5 Pro: action classification EM 85.60 vs 24.20; action count EM 79.20 vs 37.60; temporal localization EM 63.97 vs 7.04; knowledge QA ROUGE-L 27.40 vs 29.76 (Gemini better). Critic ablation: classification falls 85.60 to 82.20 without it, but action count and Hit@1 are marginally higher without the Critic. Stated limits: single sport, fixed SOPs limit flexibility on novel tasks, no human evaluation. [V] — [COACH](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01853v1)

- [NEW] LLM-SoccerArena (arXiv 2607.24573, July 2026): live benchmark of LLM forecasts for the 2026 World Cup with recorded tool traces (forecasting, not tracking QA). [V] — [arXiv 2607.24573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24573)

Documented failure modes
- SportsMetrics (ACL 2024): LLMs must track points, assists and similar stats across thousands of play-by-play records, attributing each action to the right player and team; includes adversarial variants (new rules, longer games, scrambled narratives). This is the canonical evidence that asking an LLM to aggregate statistics by reading event logs in context is unreliable. The search summary did not include its numeric results. [V] — [SportsMetrics, arXiv 2402.10979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10979); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.17)
- [NEW] StatABench (arXiv 2606.22977, June 2026): 404 closed-form questions over 18 topics plus 30 open-ended modelling tasks; most cross-model differences come from statistical failures (inappropriate tool selection, result interpretation) rather than engineering failures; best agent frameworks scored 61.86/100 on open-ended tasks. [V] — [StatABench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22977)
- [NEW] P-Bench / Fisher-R1 (August 2026 preprint): 425 hypothesis-testing tasks; agents can execute code correctly and still reach wrong conclusions, and common benchmarks miss this because they check execution or final answers, not method appropriateness. Secondary source; not replicated. [V, secondary] — [news summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/fisher-r1-targets-more-reliable-hypothesis-testing-by-llm-ag-391221ef)
- [NEW] LongDS-Bench (arXiv 2605.30434, May 2026): multi-turn data analysis; best of five models about 48% average accuracy; long-horizon state-tracking errors were 52% to 69% of failures. [V] — [LongDS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30434)
- [NEW] JMIR 2026 pilot of an LLM agent across clinical data-analysis stages warns that agents can produce plausible outputs with methodological errors a non-expert reviewer may miss. [V] — [JMIR 2026](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e99597)
- From prior knowledge [M]: SPORTU (text plus video sports understanding benchmark) — [arXiv 2410.08474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08474); SportQA (70k+ multiple-choice sports questions, three difficulty levels) — [arXiv 2402.15862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15862). Both test sports knowledge and video understanding, not structured-data QA.

### Inferences (architecture recommendation)
- Use a semantic layer plus tool calls, never raw frames in context. Evidence chain: SportsMetrics (in-context aggregation fails), SportSQL (schema-only prompting works for SQL), BADGE (compact tabular input beats other encodings), TennisTV (video LLMs fail on rallies), COACH (a small local backbone with structured stroke grounding beats a frontier general VLM by a wide margin on rally QA).
- Concrete offline design for a 64 GB Apple-silicon machine: DuckDB over Parquet for the three layers in section 1; a data dictionary (table and column descriptions, units, coordinate frame, enum values, known noise levels) as the system prompt; tools: `run_sql`, `run_python` (pandas/polars/scipy in a sandbox), `get_metric(name, filters)` for a curated library of vetted metric functions, `stat_test(...)` wrappers that always return effect size, n, and an interval, `render_clip(rally_id, shot_idx)` so every claim can be checked against video, and `plot`. I did not research which local models fit this hardware; that is a separate question. COACH's use of Flan-T5-XL shows the backbone need not be large if the structure is good, but that system was trained per task.
- Guardrails that address the documented failures: (1) numbers in the answer must come from tool output (post-check that every figure appears in a tool result); (2) every proportion is reported with n and an interval, and the stats wrappers refuse or caveat below a minimum n; (3) a critic/verifier pass that re-derives the key number by an independent query (COACH's critic helped classification but not counting, so do not assume it fixes everything); (4) keep sessions short or persist intermediate tables by name, since LongDS-Bench attributes most failures to long-horizon state tracking; (5) pre-register metric definitions in the semantic layer so "third-shot drop success" means one thing; (6) log multiple-comparison count when the agent scans many patterns.
- Prefer curated metric functions over free-form code for anything involving the noisy 3D primitives (reach, in/out margin), because the uncertainty handling in section 5 is easy to get wrong ad hoc.

### Gaps
- No paper found where an LLM agent queries raw 10 to 60 Hz tracking data with tool calls and is evaluated for it; this appears to be open territory (stated as absence of evidence from about four searches, not proof).
- No pickleball coaching LLM or agent paper was found. Commercial pickleball tools may have chat features; I did not verify any.
- I found no study specifically measuring small-sample overclaiming by LLMs on sports data; the 2026 statistics benchmarks are the nearest evidence.
- SoccerAgent's architecture, DeepSport's tools, and SportSQL's accuracy numbers were not read in full.

## 5. Statistical care: propagating monocular 3D uncertainty; sample sizes

### Takeaway
Published monocular ball reconstructions report errors on the order of 8 to 15 cm on synthetic data and much worse on real broadcast footage, with event-timing errors dominating; that is the same order as the margin that decides "in vs out" or "within vs outside reach", so such claims must be made as probabilities over a per-shot error distribution and aggregated with intervals. I found no published work on uncertainty-aware racket-sport analytics from monocular data or on minimum sample sizes for tactical patterns.

### Cited Findings
- MonoTrack (2022, badminton): on synthetic tests, 3D reconstruction error about 14.9 cm, reduced to 8.0 cm with priors; on real footage the end-to-end pipeline showed up to four times the error of bootstrapped estimates, with a maximum of 37.1 px (about 5% of image resolution). [V, from search summary] — [MonoTrack, arXiv 2204.01899](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.01899)
- TT3D: a 40 ms bounce-time uncertainty maps to up to 40 cm of bounce position at 10 m/s. [V] — [arXiv 2504.10035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10035)
- Kienzle et al. (CVPRW 2025, table tennis broadcast): 2D reprojection error 0.19% of image diagonal; motion blur and trajectories cut short by the opponent's hit are named error sources. [V] — [arXiv 2504.19863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19863)
- LATTE-MV (CVPR 2025): reconstructs table tennis from monocular video and uses uncertainty estimation for downstream anticipation; numbers not seen. [V, snippet] — [LATTE-MV](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Etaat_LATTE-MV_Learning_to_Anticipate_Table_Tennis_Hits_from_Monocular_Videos_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- Hawk-Eye player-tracking accuracy is unpublished even for the multi-camera system. [V] — [JSSM 23(1)](https://jssm.org/23-1-1.p_d_f)
- Tennis points are not i.i.d. (Klaassen and Magnus 2001). [V, secondary] — [via Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)
- Swartz's strategy framework explicitly recommends sensitivity analysis over the assumed component probabilities (e.g. changing P(win | creep, deep return) from 0.20 to 0.10 moves the result only from 0.47 to 0.46). [V] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)

### Inferences (how to do it; my recommendations)
- **Carry a covariance per ball sample and per joint.** Monocular error is anisotropic: small across the image plane, large along the camera ray. For an end-on broadcast camera that means ball depth (along-court y) and height trade off, and far-side players' joints are worse than near-side. Store it in camera-ray-aligned form and rotate into court axes.
- **Estimate the noise empirically, per match**, using physical ground truth that is free: bounces must occur at z = ball radius; the ball must clear the net when the rally continues; contacts must be within a paddle-plus-arm of the hitter's wrist; players' feet are on z = 0; limb lengths are constant. Residuals against these constraints give a per-match, per-court-half sigma.
- **"Outside normal reach" as a probabilistic statement.** Define reach demand d = distance from the player's reference point (e.g. pelvis or hitting shoulder at opponent contact, or at own contact) to the ball's interception point, optionally divided by time available. Build each player's envelope from *successful* contacts (e.g. the 95th percentile of d as a function of direction and time available; the convex-hull-of-historic-reaches idea in section 2). For each miss, sample ball and pose from their error distributions (Monte Carlo, a few hundred draws) to get P(d > envelope). Report "k of n misses had P(outside) > 0.8" with a cluster-bootstrap interval, and compare with the same statistic for made shots as the control. Because both the envelope and the misses are measured with the same system, shared bias largely cancels; the residual risk is differential bias (misses happen at stretch, where pose estimation is worst, and the ball track near a miss often terminates early, the problem Kienzle et al. flag).
- **Compare near-side with near-side.** Stratify or include court side as a covariate, since teams switch ends; otherwise a "player A reaches farther" finding can be a camera-geometry artefact.
- **Proportions and patterns.** Use Wilson or Beta-binomial intervals; rule of thumb from the binomial: at p near 0.5, a 95% interval of plus or minus 10 points needs about 100 occurrences, plus or minus 5 points about 400. A pattern seen 12 times cannot support "most likely" claims. Use hierarchical partial pooling (player within league baseline, seeded from pklmart base rates) for per-player rates, and control the false discovery rate when the agent scans many patterns.
- **Resample by game or match**, not by rally, because of within-match dependence.
- **Label propagation.** Shot-type classifier confusion should be carried too: store class probabilities and let metric functions either threshold or weight by them.
- **In/out and kitchen-fault claims** should carry a margin and sigma and be reported only in aggregate ("x% of his third-shot drops land within 1 m of the NVZ line, plus or minus y") rather than as individual line calls.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy figures for monocular 3D ball reconstruction in pickleball, or for 3D pose of far-side players in broadcast racket-sport footage, were found in this session (other researchers on the reconstruction side may have them).
- No literature on minimum sample sizes for racket-sport tactical patterns was found; the numbers above are standard binomial arithmetic, not sourced findings.
- No uncertainty-aware racket-sport analytics paper (error propagation from tracking to tactical claims) was found.

## 6. Existing pickleball analytics: commercial tools and academic work

### Takeaway
Commercial pickleball analytics is built on a single fixed camera (phone on a tripod or a club's mounted camera) seeing the full court, producing shot-by-shot labels, placement, speed and line calls; none of the vendors I checked publishes accuracy figures. Academic pickleball analytics is close to nonexistent: one review paper says so explicitly and calls for exactly the computer-vision-to-data pipeline the user is building.

### Cited Findings
- PB Vision: ML/CV detection of rallies, shots, errors, player movement, shot accuracy and shot types from uploaded or recorded video; requires a stationary camera at least four feet off the ground with the whole court (all four corners) in frame; clubs integrate via standardized camera framing (PodPlay). API exposes frame-level `cv`, shot-by-shot `insights`, and `stats`; uploads limited to 30 minutes, H.264, 30/60 fps. Third-party-reported pricing: 20 and 50 USD per month tiers (may be stale). - SwingVision (tennis and pickleball, single iPhone): real-time line calls with replay challenges (including from Apple Watch), highlights by shot type, ball speed, shot placement, win percentage, rally length; real-time stats and line calling need iOS 18; no Android. - All commercial tools assume a camera the user controls (fixed, full court, known framing). - What the commercial products do not appear to offer (based on feature lists, i.e. absence of mention, not confirmed absence): 3D player pose, reach or biomechanical metrics, uncertainty on any statistic, cross-match pattern mining, free-form querying.
- PB Vision's public insights/stats schemas are the best available reference for what a consumer-grade taxonomy contains and are worth mirroring in naming.

## 7. Biomechanics and equipment reference data for a reach-envelope model

### Takeaway
Hard reference numbers found: paddle length at most 17 in (43.18 cm) and length plus width at most 24 in; tennis volley reaction time roughly 205 to 226 ms with stroke time of roughly 380 to 800 ms. I found no published lunge-reach distance for pickleball or tennis in metres, so the reach envelope should be learned from each player's own successful contacts rather than seeded from literature.

### Cited Findings
- Paddle rule (USA Pickleball rule 2.E.3 as reproduced by secondary sites): combined length and width, including edge guard and butt cap, must not exceed 24 in (60.96 cm); length must not exceed 17 in (43.18 cm); no thickness or weight limit. Secondary sites disagree on any separate width cap (one says 8.25 in, another 8 in), which is likely a misreading; the official rule sets only the 17 in and 24 in limits. Not checked against the official rulebook PDF. [V, secondary] — [playpickleball.com, 2025 rules section 2](https://playpickleball.com/2025-usa-pickleball-rules-section-2-court-and-equipment); [pickleball.cafe rules](https://pickleball.cafe/rules/court-and-equipment)
- Typical paddle about 16 in long and 8 in wide, 7 to 10 oz; ball under one ounce. [V] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)
- Court geometry: 44 ft by 20 ft; NVZ 7 ft each side; net 36 in at posts, 34 in at centre. [V] — [Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)
- Tennis volley reaction time (Chow et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 1999; seven skilled players): mean time from ball release to initial racquet movement 226 ms forehand and 205 ms backhand; stroke time from racquet movement to impact ranged from 381 ms (fast trials) to 803 ms (slow trials). [V, from search summary] — [PubMed 10378913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10378913)
- Bragg and Andriacchi (2001): a player was 52% more likely to reach a wide ball when using a quick lateral reaction step; distances not given in the snippet. [V, from search summary] — [ISBS proceedings](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/3761/3483)
- Steyn et al. (2025): third-shot drop success depends on speed and launch angle more than spin (useful for a physics-based drop-quality metric). [V, secondary] — [via Muthukumarana and Swartz](http://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/pb_analytics.pdf)

### Inferences
- Geometry check that follows from the cited court numbers: two players at the kitchen line are 14 ft (4.27 m) apart. A 15 m/s speed-up covers that in about 0.28 s, which is at or below the 205 to 226 ms reaction time plus any paddle movement, so in hand battles "reachable" is dominated by reaction time and paddle position at opponent contact, not by foot movement. The envelope model therefore needs two regimes: (a) reaction-limited (short time available: static reach from current paddle/hand position), (b) movement-limited (longer time: static reach plus distance coverable after a reaction delay).
- A parametric seed for the envelope, to be replaced by data: reach(t) = r_static + v_max * max(0, t - t_react), with r_static approximately shoulder-to-wrist length (taken from that player's own reconstructed skeleton) plus up to 0.43 m of paddle (the 17 in cap; typical is nearer 0.41 m), t_react about 0.2 s. Fit v_max and an acceleration term per player from the tracking data. The skeleton-derived arm length is itself noisy; use the per-player median over the match.
- Because the paddle is not tracked, the effective contact point is up to about 0.4 m from the wrist; when computing "ball distance from player" use distance to the hitting-side shoulder or wrist and treat paddle length as part of the envelope, not as measurement error.

### Gaps
- No published pickleball-specific values for lunge distance, lateral reach, movement speed or reaction time were found.
- No tennis/badminton lunge-reach distance in metres was found in this session.
- Paddle and court specifications were taken from secondary sources and the Swartz paper, not the official USA Pickleball rulebook PDF; confirm against the current official rulebook (also check for 2026 rule changes, which I did not research).
