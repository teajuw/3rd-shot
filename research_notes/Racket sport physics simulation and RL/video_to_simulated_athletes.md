# Video to physically simulated athletes (racket sports focus), as of November 2026

Notes on method: facts below come from primary papers, project pages and repos fetched on 2026-11-13. Where I only saw a search-result summary or another paper's description of a system, the bullet says so. Vid2Player3D details were read from the paper PDF text; the paper's GPU name is quoted as printed.

## 1. Vid2Player3D (tennis from broadcast video, SIGGRAPH 2023)

### Takeaway
Vid2Player3D is still the only fully documented end-to-end "single broadcast camera to physics-simulated racket-sport players" system: about 4.5 hours of estimated motion from 13 US Open broadcasts, a physics tracker to clean it, a motion VAE, and a high-level RL planner that also directly controls the wrist, trained in Isaac Gym in roughly 2.5 to 3 days on one GPU. Code is public under a non-commercial NVIDIA licence, but trained models are not released, and the paper is from 2023 (its pose-estimation front end, HybrIK + GLAMR-style root optimisation, is superseded by GVHMR/PromptHMR/SAM 3D Body class methods).

### Cited Findings

Identity
- "Learning Physically Simulated Tennis Skills from Broadcast Videos", ACM Trans. Graph. 42(4), August 2023, DOI 10.1145/3592408. Authors: Haotian Zhang (Stanford), Ye Yuan, Viktor Makoviychuk, Yunrong Guo (NVIDIA), Sanja Fidler (NVIDIA / U Toronto / Vector), Xue Bin Peng (NVIDIA / SFU), Kayvon Fatahalian (Stanford). — [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/)
- SIGGRAPH 2023 best paper honourable mention. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)

Data and reconstruction
- Source: 13 US Open match videos (1080p, 30 fps, 2017-2021) from the Tennis Channel. Resulting motion dataset: 80 min Federer, 96 min Djokovic, 103 min Nadal. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Only the near-side player is reconstructed: "Pose and depth estimates for the player on the far side of the court are less reliable." — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Tools: YOLOv4 player tracking, ViTPose 2D keypoints, HybrIK for SMPL shape and pose. Camera matrix from court lines via Perspective-n-Point. Global root position is not taken from the pose estimator: the mid-ankle 2D point is projected onto the court plane with the inverse camera projection, then refined by a GLAMR-like optimisation minimising 2D keypoint reprojection error. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Manual annotation: racket-ball contact frames and player identity. No stroke-type labels. Motion phase was learned semi-supervised with phase provided for only 20% of shots. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)

Four-stage pipeline
- Stage 1: kinematic motion estimation from video (dataset M_kin). Stage 2: a low-level imitation policy tracks M_kin in simulation to produce a physically corrected dataset M_corr. Stage 3: a motion VAE (MVAE, after Ling et al. character-motion-vaes) is fitted to M_corr, conditioned on global root position and extended to predict a cyclic motion phase (pi = ball contact, 0/2pi = recovery). Stage 4: a high-level planning policy outputs an MVAE latent code plus joint corrections for the swing arm; the resulting kinematic target is tracked by the low-level policy. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- The low-level tracker is built on EmbodiedPose and is trained in two stages, first on AMASS then on the tennis data (configs `amass_im`, then `djokovic_im`). The high-level policy uses a three-stage curriculum (`federer_train_stage_1/2/3`). The repo also builds on ASE and character-motion-vaes. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- The low-level policy may apply residual forces to the root (Yuan and Kitani 2020). Removing them cuts foot sliding from 1.46 cm to 0.87 cm but drops hit rate 0.89 to 0.78 and bounce-in rate 0.81 to 0.69. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Each training episode practises four consecutive shots, which the authors found better than one shot per episode. The reward after contact is on predicted bounce position versus target, zeroed if the spin direction (topspin vs backspin) does not match the commanded one. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)

Simulator, character, compute
- Simulator: NVIDIA Isaac Gym Preview 4, Python 3.7/3.8; demos run 8,192 parallel environments. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- Character built from SMPL: 24 rigid bodies, 72 DOF. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Compute, quoted: low-level policy "approximately one billion samples, which requires around 12 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX A100 GPU. Training the MVAE requires four hours and the high-level policy is trained over five billion samples, which requires about two days." — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)

Results
- Skills: serves, topspin forehand, topspin backhand, backhand slice; one- and two-handed backhands; left- and right-handed play; hitting to commanded target positions. — [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/)
- Over 10K test sessions of 15 consecutive balls (reported as three values per metric, median for Federer stated as hit rate 0.92, bounce-in 0.85): Fed-full hit 0.85/0.92/1.00, bounce-in 0.77/0.85/0.92, bounce position error 1.49/1.74/1.93 m; Djokovic and Nadal controllers similar (bounce-in 0.73-0.85 and 0.69-0.85). — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Two independently trained controllers were dropped into one simulation and produced a 38-shot, 41-second rally (Federer vs Djokovic controllers). — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Data-size sensitivity: with 12.5% of Federer's data (about ten minutes of motion) hit and bounce-in rates fall significantly "because the learned motion embedding is not dense enough". — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)

Code, data, licence
- Released: low-level imitation training code, MVAE training code, high-level policy training code, Isaac Gym environments, AMASS conversion script. Last dated updates November 2023; 9 commits, about 347 stars. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- Not released: trained checkpoints. README: "the trained models are currently not available due to license issues of the video footage we used." Demos therefore cannot be run without retraining. The video annotation pipeline is only referenced through third-party tools. A Google Drive folder with tennis data is linked but its contents are not described on the page. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- Licence: "NVIDIA License", clause 3.3: "The Work and any derivative works thereof only may be used or intended for use non-commercially." — [LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d/main/LICENSE.txt)

Limitations stated by the authors
- Controllers capture "coarse attributes" of player style; fine details such as wrist pronation and racket-head position in the backswing are not reproduced, and the authors suggest higher-quality input is needed. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- No strategy: "Our focus in this project was to successfully control characters to hit tennis balls in tennis rallies, not to learn strategies for winning tennis points." Controllers were trained against random incoming balls, not an opponent. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Reliance on residual root forces for best task performance. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Later critique from AdaPT (Aug 2026): Vid2Player3D is "simulation-based" with a decoupled planner-tracker and no adaptive mechanisms; an AdaPT reimplementation of it scored 81.5-83.2% rally success in sim and only 8-28% hit rate on a real Unitree G1. — [AdaPT, arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)

### Inferences
- Total wall-clock for one player is about 12 h + 4 h + 48 h = roughly 2.7 days on one data-centre GPU, not counting the video annotation stage. That is not feasible on Apple silicon as published: Isaac Gym is CUDA/Linux only and has been deprecated in favour of Isaac Lab; a Mac-side reproduction would need a port to MuJoCo MJX / mjlab-style tooling or a rented NVIDIA GPU.
- The reader's existing outputs (per-player SMPL-like motion on the court, contact times, ball path) correspond closely to Vid2Player3D's M_kin plus manual annotations. The stages they lack are exactly stages 2-4.
- Vid2Player3D needed about 80-100 minutes of motion per player and degraded badly at about 10 minutes. A single pickleball match gives far less per player unless pooled across players or supplemented with a general motion prior (AMASS pretraining, as the low-level tracker already does).
- The paper prints "NVIDIA RTX A100"; this is almost certainly an A100 (there is no "RTX A100" product), but I report it as printed.

### Gaps
- The arXiv ID for the paper was not confirmed (the ID I first tried was a different paper). Use the DOI or project page.
- Contents and licence of the linked Google Drive tennis motion data were not inspected.
- The supplementary material (network sizes, exact reward weights, number of environments used for training) was not read.
- No evidence found of repo activity after November 2023.

## 2. Follow-ups and relatives through November 2026

### Takeaway
Since 2024 the centre of gravity has moved from simulated graphics characters to real humanoid robots (mostly Unitree G1), and the dominant recipe is now: short human references (video via GVHMR + GMR retargeting, or mocap) plus a general motion tracker plus a task-level RL policy with a hand-written ball predictor. The closest direct successor to Vid2Player3D is AdaPT (Aug 2026), which again uses Nadal/Federer/Djokovic broadcast video and an MVAE; the cheapest is TaskNPoint (June 2026), one video demo per stroke and under an hour on one RTX 4090.

### Cited Findings

Simulated characters (graphics lineage)
- Strategy and Skill Learning for Physics-based Table Tennis Animation (Wang, Hodgins, Won; CMU / SNU; arXiv 2407.16210, July 2024; described in search results as SIGGRAPH 2024). Input: motion capture, categorised into five skill subsets; imitation policies per skill are blended by a mixer policy; a strategy-level controller picks a skill index and target landing location via behaviour cloning with iterative refinement. Simulator: Isaac Gym. Includes agent-vs-agent matches and a VR human-vs-agent mode (Unity front end, user's paddle physically simulated). Code and data link: jiashunwang.github.io/PhysicsPingPong. — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16210)
- Same paper uses broadcast video only for strategy, not motion: a "video strategy" is built from expert demonstrations extracted "from existing broadcast videos (20 minutes in total)" and fitted with a CVAE by behaviour cloning. — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16210)
- SMPLOlympics (Luo, Wang, Liu, Zhang, Tessler, Wang, Yuan, Cao, Lin, Wang, Hodgins, Kitani; CMU / NVIDIA; arXiv 2407.00187, June 2024). Isaac Gym, policy 30 Hz, sim 60 Hz. Sports: high jump, long jump, hurdling, golf, javelin, tennis (single and 1v1), table tennis (single and 1v1), fencing, boxing, soccer (penalty, 1v1, 2v2), basketball. Video demonstrations: TRAM reconstructs SMPL motion from internet video, PHC physically refines it; used for golf, tennis, table tennis and soccer penalty kicks. Track and field used AMASS instead because pose estimation failed on fast-moving players and cameras. — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- SMPLOlympics baseline results are weak for racket sports: tennis average hits per episode 2.76 (PPO), 3.95 (AMP), 2.48 (PULSE), 2.62 (PULSE+AMP); table tennis 1.01, 1.10, 0.74, 1.83. The authors call the reward designs preliminary. Compute: single RTX 3090, about 1-3 days per model, about 1e9 samples. Code via project page smplolympics.github.io. — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- A sibling site, HumanoidOlympics, compares PPO, AMP and PULSE controllers and uses PULSE+AMP for sports with video-derived demonstrations. — [humanoidolympics.github.io](https://humanoidolympics.github.io)

Humanoid robots, tennis
- LATENT (Tsinghua, PKU, Galbot, Shanghai Qi Zhi, Shanghai AI Lab; arXiv 2603.12686, March 2026). Input: 5 hours of optical mocap from five amateur players in a roughly 3 m x 5 m area, fragments of primitives (forehand, backhand, lateral shuffle, crossover step), no editing or annotation; retargeted with LocoMuJoCo. Simulator: MuJoCo JAX (MuJoCo Playground), PPO, policy 50 Hz, physics 2000 Hz for ball contacts. Method: general tracker (Any2Track based) with right-wrist control removed, distilled by DAgger into a latent action space with a learnable state-conditioned prior; a high-level policy outputs residual latent actions bounded by a "Latent Action Barrier" plus direct wrist corrections. Robot: Unitree G1 29-DoF. — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- LATENT results: sim success 96.52% forehand, 82.10% backhand (10,000 trials each), versus PULSE 71.85%, ASE 63.47%, AMP 41.32% on forehand; plain PPO and MotionVAE did not converge. Real: 20 consecutive rallies with a human; real success 90.9% forehand, 77.8% backhand using a relaxed "lands in opponent court" criterion. Compute: 8 GPUs in parallel, model and hours not stated. — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- LATENT code: the paper links github.com/GalaxyGeneralRobotics/LATENT — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686); contradicted by AdaPT (Aug 2026), which says LATENT's code was not public and used PULSE as a proxy baseline — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087). Status of the repo was not checked directly.
- AdaPT, "Towards Professional Tennis Styles for Humanoid Robots with Adaptive Motion Planning and Tracking" (Noitom Robotics, Shanghai AI Lab, SJTU, Dobot; arXiv 2608.20087, Aug 2026). Input: broadcast video of Nadal (267 min, 5,664 clips), Federer (179 min, 5,264 clips), Djokovic (189 min, 5,288 clips), about 2 s per clip, plus 219 min of mocap from one professional. Reconstruction: GVHMR to SMPL, GMR retargeting to G1. Annotations: player, stroke (forehand/backhand/serve), spin type, contact time, serve ball-release time. Simulator: mjlab, PPO, 4,096 envs, 4 x RTX 4090; total hours not given. Method: decoupled planner + speed-adaptive tracker; rally planner is an MVAE "following Vid2Player3D" with auxiliary stroke and spin losses; serve uses a residual tracker built on AdaMimic. — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)
- AdaPT results: sim rally success 91.5% (Nadal), 96.3% (Federer), 92.3% (Djokovic); sim serve success about 99.7-99.9%. Real G1 rally hit rates forehand/backhand 44/56%, 64/48%, 48/48%; real serve success 66.7%, 73.3%, 86.7% over 15 trials each. Code stated to be on the project site humanoidtennis.github.io/AdaPT. — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)
- TaskNPoint (Werner, Demler, Perona, Ames; Caltech; arXiv 2606.26215, June 2026). One human video demonstration per skill; a human "coach" marks the interaction window (for a backhand, about 20 cm of racket travel around contact) and the goal; randomised target sampling in RL generalises the single demo to new target locations. Simulator: mjlab at 200 Hz, policy 50 Hz, PPO, 4,096 envs on a single RTX 4090, "less than 1 GPU hour". Tasks on a Unitree G1: tennis forehands and backhands against thrown balls, kicking incoming soccer balls, box pick and place. Overall success on 34 motions 91%, mean target position error 4.9 cm (simulation). — [arXiv 2606.26215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26215)
- TaskNPoint video front end: ViTPose 2D joints, SAM2 for identity association, DROID-SLAM + ZoeDepth for camera, PromptHMR for per-frame SMPL-X of all people; depth axes of individual monocular cameras are down-weighted in a spatial fusion step. — [arXiv 2606.26215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26215)
- TaskNPoint's own comparison table (their estimates, assuming 30,000 RL iterations at 1 s each per policy): VideoMimic 4 stages / 33 GPU-h / 20.5 min data per task; HDMI 1 / 8 / 1.0 min; OmniRetarget 1 / 8 / 0.1 min; LATENT 3 / 25 / 300 min (>200 demos); HITTER 1 / 8 / 0.3 min (2 demos); HumanX 3 / >50 / 500 min; TaskNPoint 1 / 1 / 0.5 min. — [arXiv 2606.26215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26215)
- CyboRacket (arXiv 2603.14605, March 2026): described in a search summary as a perception-to-action framework for humanoid racket sports, evaluated on vision-based humanoid tennis. Not read. — [arXiv 2603.14605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14605)

Humanoid robots, table tennis
- HITTER (Su, Zhang, Rahmanian, Gao, Liao, Regan, Sreenath, Sastry; UC Berkeley; arXiv 2508.21043, Aug 2025). Human references: two swings (forehand, backhand) recorded on video, reconstructed with GVHMR, retargeted with GMR, upsampled 30 to 50 Hz; one clip is 94 frames (1.88 s). Simulator: Isaac Lab, PPO. Model-based planner predicts ball flight and picks a racket target on a fixed virtual hit plane; RL whole-body controller executes. Perception: nine OptiTrack cameras at 360 Hz. Results on Unitree G1: 24 of 26 balls returned (92.3%), up to 106 consecutive shots against a human, humanoid-vs-humanoid exchanges. Compute not stated; no code release mentioned. — [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21043)
- SMASH (HKU, Kinetix AI; arXiv 2604.01158, April 2026). Input: 400 mocap whole-body strike clips of 1.08 s each (0.54 s either side of contact), retargeted with GMR; a conditional autoregressive Motion-VAE generates about 5k extra clips, filtered by tracker rollout. Reference motion chosen by nearest neighbour on desired strike target. Perception: head-mounted ZED X stereo at 60 Hz (YOLO + HSV + triangulation on Jetson Orin), adaptive EKF with bounce handling. Results: sim success 86.38%; hardware with ego camera 19/20 hits, 12/20 returns; 50-minute run of 642 launches with 93.7% contact and 59.7% return. Simulator not named in text read; compute not reported; no code release mentioned. — [arXiv 2604.01158](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01158)
- PACE / "Towards Versatile Humanoid Table Tennis: Unified Reinforcement Learning with Prediction Augmentation" (Purdue; arXiv 2509.21690; described as ICRA 2026): end-to-end RL deployed zero-shot on a Booster T1, sim hit rate at least 96% across serve ranges, training code open-sourced, per a search summary of the project page. Not read in full. — [Project page](https://engineering.purdue.edu/cdesign/wp/pace/)
- "Physics Models for Sim-to-Real Transfer in Professional-Level Robot Table Tennis" (arXiv 2606.28805, June 2026): title only; relevant to ball/contact modelling. — [arXiv 2606.28805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28805)

Humanoid robots, badminton
- "Learning Human-Like Badminton Skills for Humanoid Robots" (Chen, Dong, Ji, Sun, et al., corresponding Peng Lu; arXiv 2602.08370, v2 Sept 2026). "Imitation-to-Interaction": four progressive stages (teacher tracking of mocap, distillation, motion stabilisation with adversarial priors, interaction-driven refinement). Input: expert badminton mocap recording body markers and the 6-DoF racket pose. Simulator: Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab, physics 200 Hz, policy 50 Hz, 4,096 envs. Skills: forehand and backhand lifts, drop shots. Robot: EngineAI PM01; real-world success 90% forehand lifts, 70% backhand lifts over 10 trials each, with ball and robot tracked by optical mocap and an EKF. Compute hours not found in the text read. — [arXiv 2602.08370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08370)

### Inferences
- Three reusable recipes exist for the reader, in increasing cost: (a) TaskNPoint/HITTER style, one or two reference swings per stroke plus a goal-conditioned tracker with a model-based ball predictor (about 1-8 GPU-hours on one RTX 4090); (b) LATENT/SMASH style, hours of fragments plus a latent skill space plus a task policy (multi-GPU, about a day by TaskNPoint's estimate); (c) Vid2Player3D/AdaPT style, 1.5-4.5 hours of per-player broadcast motion, MVAE planner and tracker (days on one data-centre GPU, or 4 x RTX 4090).
- For "digital twin of a specific match" rather than "a controller that can play", recipe (a) plus plain per-clip motion tracking (the Vid2Player3D stage 2 step alone) is the smallest step from the reader's current reconstruction: it yields physically corrected replays without needing a motion embedding or a strategy policy.
- Toolchain trend: Isaac Gym (2023-2024) to Isaac Lab (2025) to MuJoCo-based mjlab / MuJoCo Playground (2026). The MuJoCo-based stacks are the ones with any realistic path to running on a Mac (MuJoCo itself runs natively; MJX/mjlab GPU training paths target NVIDIA, so this needs checking by whoever covers compute).
- Robot papers use the G1's morphology, not SMPL; their reward and curriculum designs transfer, but their retargeting steps (GMR, LocoMuJoCo) are unnecessary for an SMPL/MHR-shaped simulated character.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed against primary text: PACE, CyboRacket, the Nov 2025 badminton paper, arXiv 2606.28805, and the basketball/soccer/volleyball papers listed as citations. No volleyball-specific or squash/padel-specific physics-character work surfaced in searches; that is absence of search hits, not proof of absence.
- PhysicsPingPong mocap amount, number of subjects, and training compute were not located in the text I scanned.
- SMASH simulator and all compute figures; HITTER compute; AdaPT total training hours; LATENT GPU model and hours: not stated in the portions read. The GPU-hour numbers for LATENT and HITTER above are TaskNPoint's assumption-based estimates, not the original authors' figures.
- Whether SMPLOlympics / HumanoidOlympics code is actually runnable today (repo noted elsewhere as "still under construction" for SMPLSim) was not verified.

## 3. Racket/paddle attachment, wrist, ball contact and spin

### Takeaway
Every system surveyed rigidly fixes the racket to the wrist or replaces the hand with it; none simulates a grip. Wrist motion at contact is the one thing most systems refuse to take from the reference data and instead let the task policy learn directly. Spin is modelled with a Magnus force only in Vid2Player3D; the 2025-2026 robot systems ignore spin in their ball predictors.

### Cited Findings
- Vid2Player3D racket: "a combination of two solid cylinders and the grip is simplified by directly attaching the end of the racket handle to the wrist joint"; different grips (eastern for Federer's forehand, semi-western for Djokovic and Nadal) are modelled by changing the fixed racket orientation per player. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D wrist ("hybrid control"): full-body motion comes from the MVAE while "the wrist motion is directly controlled by the high-level policy". The correction is three Euler angles: two for the wrist joint (twist excluded) and a twist angle for another swing-arm joint. Without hybrid control, hit rate stays reasonable but bounce-in rate "drops nearly by half" (medians 0.46 vs 0.85). — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D two-handed backhand: the free hand is placed on the racket by inverse kinematics from wrist to shoulder as a kinematic constraint, then tracked physically; head/eye tracking of the ball is also added kinematically rather than learned. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D ball: rigid sphere with real radius and mass, restitution 0.9, friction 0.8; external drag F_d = C_d A v^2 / 2 with C_d = 0.55 and Magnus force F_M = C_L A v^2 / 2 with C_L = 1/(2 + v/v_spin), A = pi rho R^2. Spin enters the reward as a binary topspin/backspin match. Analysis of one million simulated shots shows balls with higher velocity and spin are harder to hit. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- SMPLOlympics: the humanoid's right hand is replaced by the implement. Tennis racket circular, 15 cm radius, 35 cm from the wrist; table tennis paddle circular, 8 cm radius, 12 cm from the wrist. Little detail on contact or aerodynamics. — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- PhysicsPingPong: rigid-body simulation of ball, player and table; the ball can spin, but "air resistance is modeled using only damping based on velocity, rather than incorporating the Magnus effect", which the authors list as a limitation. Mixer blending weights for shoulder, elbow and wrist are lowest (most constrained to one skill) at paddle contact. — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16210)
- LATENT: racket mounted on the right end-effector with a 3D-printed adapter, hand replaced. Wrist excluded from the tracker's controlled joints and randomly perturbed during tracker training; the high-level policy outputs direct wrist corrections. Removing wrist correction lowers forehand success from 96.52% to 82.36%. Ball: randomised mass, restitution, tangential damping, quadratic drag; spin not mentioned. — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- AdaPT: 23-inch junior racket on a wrist mount with a 25 degree offset. Spin type is an annotation label and auxiliary MVAE target, but the ball trajectory predictor "ignores spin" and models only gravity and aerodynamic drag. Serve reward weights racket and wrist at the deepest backswing keyframe 50x the standard tracking reward. Ball dynamics calibrated from 20 real-court mocap ball trajectories. — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)
- HITTER: racket on the right wrist via 3D-printed connector; ball model has quadratic drag and a bounce restitution matrix; spin and Magnus force assumed negligible; the robot uses a flat push, so heavy-spin strokes are not handled. — [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21043)
- SMASH: flight uses quadratic drag and gravity, table bounce uses separate horizontal and vertical restitution; spin not modelled (listed limitation). — [arXiv 2604.01158](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01158)
- Badminton (2602.08370): PM01 arms have only 5 DoF and lack full wrist dexterity; the policy compensates with torso yaw and base orientation. The mocap recorded the 6-DoF racket pose directly. — [arXiv 2602.08370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08370)
- TaskNPoint: no simulated object-contact feedback in training (listed limitation); real ball tracked with OptiTrack and a Kalman filter, bounce handled with an estimated coefficient of restitution; most hardware failures are due to imprecise ball trajectory estimates. — [arXiv 2606.26215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26215)

### Inferences
- The consistent design across five independent groups (Vid2Player3D, LATENT, AdaPT, SMPLOlympics, HITTER) is: racket welded to the wrist with a fixed per-player grip offset, and wrist angles around contact treated as a free action or heavily perturbed, because wrist motion is the least reliable channel in both video and mocap. The reader's "approximate paddle" is therefore adequate as a starting point; the paddle face at contact should be solved by the controller, constrained by the observed ball outcome, rather than taken from pose estimates.
- A pickleball digital twin has an advantage the tennis work lacked: the reader already has a 3D ball path with hits and bounces. The outgoing ball velocity after each hit can serve as a direct per-shot target (in place of Vid2Player3D's "bounce position" target), which constrains paddle face and swing speed at contact.
- For pickleball, spin and aerodynamics need their own model. A perforated plastic ball has much higher drag relative to mass than a tennis ball, and the published coefficients here (C_d = 0.55, restitution 0.9, friction 0.8) are tennis-specific and should not be reused.

### Gaps
- No surveyed paper models string-bed or paddle-face compliance, dwell time, or spin generation from tangential paddle friction in detail; whether Isaac Gym / MuJoCo rigid contact gives realistic spin was not evaluated in what I read.
- No published pickleball ball aerodynamic or bounce parameters were found in this pass (probably covered by another researcher).

## 4. Handling noisy video reconstruction as imitation data

### Takeaway
The standard answer is "track it in physics first": a pretrained general motion tracker (usually pretrained on AMASS or similar clean mocap) imitates the noisy clip in simulation, which removes jitter, foot skating and ground penetration; then a generative prior over the corrected motion smooths further; and the wrist is handed to the task policy. Root trajectory is anchored to the court plane from feet and camera calibration rather than trusted from the pose network.

### Cited Findings
- Vid2Player3D measured motion quality at each stage for Federer (jitter in 10^3 m/s^3, foot sliding in cm): raw kinematic M_kin 6.08 / 7.41; physics-corrected M_corr 3.14 / 1.46; MVAE output M_vae 0.96 / 4.70; full system 0.51 / 1.46; ablation without physics correction 1.19 / 2.82. Physics correction removes foot sliding; the MVAE removes jitter but reintroduces foot sliding, which the final physics tracking removes again. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D: "even with physics correction, occlusions and motion blur result in M_corr containing notable errors in the character's estimated wrist motion (and corresponding racket motion). Even small errors in swing motion can prevent the high-level policy from finding motion solutions that accurately hit the ball." — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D conditions the MVAE on global root position; without it the autoregressive motion drifts globally and the character misses subsequent shots. Without phase prediction, racket-ball distance at contact cannot be minimised. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- Vid2Player3D low-level tracker is pretrained on AMASS before fine-tuning on tennis motion. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- Vid2Player3D suggests combining "a small number of high-quality demonstrations (via high-resolution video capture or mocap)" with large-scale low-quality broadcast motion as future work. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- SMPLOlympics: TRAM for reconstruction then PHC (a universal physics tracker) to refine; abandoned video for track and field because pose estimation failed on fast-moving players and moving cameras. — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- AdaPT: GVHMR wrist estimates are "often wrong under occlusion", so racket-related wrist joints are refined per player's stroke style; randomised wrist perturbations are added during retargeting of rally data to diversify swing directions; a general tracker trained on the retargeted data makes motions physically plausible. Foot sliding and jitter handling is not otherwise described. — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)
- LATENT treats even optical mocap as imperfect: wrist motion during swings is imprecise; the fix is to drop wrist control from the tracker, perturb it randomly, and let the task policy correct it. Removing the Latent Action Barrier lowers success and increases jitter. — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- TaskNPoint's position: only the short interaction window matters, so the reference is used for style and RL fills in the trajectory; "randomized target sampling during training lets a single demonstration generalize zero-shot to unseen goal locations". — [arXiv 2606.26215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26215)
- HITTER uses just two video-derived swings (GVHMR + GMR) as style references and lets RL with a task reward produce the actual strikes. — [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21043)
- SMASH filters generated motions through a tracker rollout and strike-phase check before adding them to the library. — [arXiv 2604.01158](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01158)

### Inferences
- The reader's smoothing and court placement play the role of Vid2Player3D's root optimisation; the missing piece is a physics tracker. Running a pretrained universal tracker (PHC / PULSE family for SMPL-shaped bodies, per SMPLOlympics) over each player's clip is the established way to turn smoothed kinematics into dynamically consistent motion, and it is what gives the largest foot-sliding reduction (7.41 cm to 1.46 cm in Vid2Player3D).
- MHR-to-SMPL conversion is likely needed to reuse those trackers, since every system here assumes SMPL/SMPL-X or a retargeted robot.
- Residual root forces are a pragmatic crutch worth copying initially: they let a tracker follow imperfect video motion at a measured cost of about 0.6 cm extra foot sliding.
- Far-side players are a known weak point (Vid2Player3D skipped them entirely). In pickleball doubles two of four players are far-side, though the court is much shorter than a tennis court, so the degradation should be smaller; this is my inference, not a tested claim.

### Gaps
- No paper reports how physics tracking behaves on reconstructions from SAM 3D Body / MHR specifically.
- No quantitative study found of how much smoothing before tracking helps or hurts (over-smoothed swings lose racket-head speed); AdaPT's speed-adaptive tracker is the nearest related idea.

## 5. Doubles, multi-player rallies, and pickleball

### Takeaway
I found no published physics-simulation or RL work on pickleball, and no simulated-character doubles for any racket sport learned from video. Multi-player work is limited to 1v1 rallies between independently trained controllers, 1v1 strategy learning in table tennis, 2v2 soccer in SMPLOlympics, and one late-2026 humanoid table-tennis doubles paper known to me only through a secondary write-up.

### Cited Findings
- Vid2Player3D 1v1: two separately trained single-player controllers placed in one simulation produced a 38-shot rally; no opponent modelling or self-play. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf)
- PhysicsPingPong: genuine two-agent strategy learning (competitive and cooperative), with iterative behaviour cloning on winning or cooperative interaction data, plus human-vs-agent in VR. Singles only. — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16210)
- SMPLOlympics: tennis and table tennis are single-player and 1v1; 2v2 exists only for soccer, and the authors say 2v2 soccer and basketball fall short of prior state of the art. — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- HITTER showed humanoid-vs-humanoid table tennis exchanges without explicit multi-agent training. LATENT's authors note professional-level play "may need multi-agent training". — [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21043); [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- PingpongDuo (reported as arXiv 2610.20579, 2026): described by a secondary site as the first system for humanoid table tennis doubles, with footwork from human doubles recordings and average returns per rally rising from 3.60 (rule-based planner) to 4.87 (trained planner with learned risk), in simulation. Secondary source only; arXiv ID and numbers unverified. — [i-scoop write-up](https://www.i-scoop.eu/pingpongduo-humanoid-table-tennis-doubles/)
- Pickleball: two targeted searches (pickleball + physics simulation / RL / humanoid / digital twin; pickleball robot RL 2026) returned no pickleball-specific research; results were table tennis, tennis and badminton humanoid papers. — [search result example: PACE project page](https://engineering.purdue.edu/cdesign/wp/pace/)

### Inferences
- A four-player pickleball twin driven by per-player trackers would be novel as far as this search shows. For replay (digital twin) purposes doubles adds little difficulty: each player tracks their own reconstructed clip and only one player contacts the ball per shot. Doubles only becomes a research problem when players must decide who takes the ball and where to stand, which nothing surveyed solves for racket sports in simulation.
- Vid2Player3D's result that independently trained controllers can rally suggests a staged path: per-player return-to-target controllers first, joint behaviour later.

### Gaps
- PingpongDuo primary paper not read.
- Searches were English-language web searches; unpublished industry work (pickleball analytics or training-machine companies) would not appear.

## 6. Applications and products

### Takeaway
I found no evidence that Vid2Player3D or its simulated-character relatives have become a shipped coaching, broadcast or game product. The visible downstream use is robot training: the same pipeline shape now drives real humanoid tennis, table tennis and badminton demonstrations, some from companies (Galbot, Noitom Robotics, Kinetix AI, EngineAI hardware).

### Cited Findings
- A search for products built on Vid2Player3D / simulated tennis / PhysicsPingPong (coaching, broadcast, game AI, commercial) returned only academic pages. The predecessor Vid2Player (2021, controllable video sprites of tennis players) is described on its pages as research with suggested uses in entertainment, visualisation and training, not a product. — [Vid2Player project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player/index.html)
- Commercial reuse of Vid2Player3D is blocked by licence terms (non-commercial use only, except NVIDIA) and trained models were withheld over video footage licensing. — [LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d/main/LICENSE.txt); [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)
- Robot training is the active application: LATENT (Galbot and universities) on Unitree G1 sustaining multi-shot rallies with humans; press coverage notes the work was not yet peer reviewed and "first" claims come from Galbot's marketing. — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12686); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-03-humanoid-robot-tennis-skills-imperfect.html)
- AdaPT is led by Noitom Robotics (a motion-capture company's robotics arm) and uses broadcast video of named professionals to give a robot "professional tennis styles". — [arXiv 2608.20087](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20087)
- Human-in-the-loop play exists as a research demo: PhysicsPingPong lets a VR user play cooperative or competitive table tennis against the simulated agent, after fine-tuning the skill controller on that user's play data. — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16210)
- Broadcast footage rights are a recurring practical constraint: Vid2Player3D's footage was "provided courtesy of the Tennis Channel" and still prevented model release. — [Paper PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_main.pdf); [GitHub README](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d)

### Inferences
- If the reader intends anything beyond personal research, the footage-rights problem that stopped NVIDIA releasing models applies equally to pickleball broadcast video, and Vid2Player3D code cannot be used commercially.
- The practical value demonstrated so far is (1) physically plausible cleaned motion, (2) controllable "what if" shots to commanded targets, and (3) sparring agents in VR; none has public evidence of productisation.

### Gaps
- No systematic search of sports-tech company products (for example pickleball analytics apps, broadcast graphics vendors, game studios) was done; absence of hits from one query is weak evidence.
- Whether NVIDIA or others use these controllers internally in products is unknown.
