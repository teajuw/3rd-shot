# Self-play, RL and imitation from video for racket-sport agents (as of November 2026)

Scope note: every item under "Cited Findings" was read from the linked source during this research pass (paper HTML, supplement PDF, or search-result summary of the page). Where a number comes from a search summary rather than the paper body, it is marked "(search summary)". Things I believe from background knowledge but did not verify this pass are confined to "Inferences" or "Gaps" and labelled as such.

## 1. Hierarchical designs: low-level motion skills plus a high-level strategy policy

### Takeaway
Every published sports agent that plays whole points uses the same split: a low-level controller, learned by imitating human motion, that turns a compact command (a latent code, or a skill ID) into joint targets at 30-50 Hz, and a high-level policy that sees the ball and the opponent and emits that command plus a target, often only once per incoming ball. The low level is trained by motion imitation (tracking or adversarial); the high level by task-reward RL, evolution strategies, heuristics/bandits, or behaviour cloning of winning rallies.

### Cited Findings

**Vid2Player3D (Zhang et al., SIGGRAPH / ACM TOG 2023) - tennis from broadcast video**
- System "combines a low-level imitation policy and a high-level motion planning policy to steer the character in a motion embedding learned from broadcast videos"; a "hybrid control policy" overrides erroneous parts of the embedding with corrections from the high-level policy, because broadcast-derived motion is low quality. Needs only simple rewards and no stroke-type annotations. Produces serves, forehands, backhands, topspin and slice, one- and two-handed backhands, left/right-handed play, and two simulated players in extended rallies with simulated racket and ball dynamics — [Project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/vid2player3d/); [Stanford page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/)
- Motion data: 13 US Open matches (4 Federer, 4 Djokovic, 5 Nadal, 2017-2021) — [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf)
- Low-level policy: MLP [1024, 1024, 512], PPO, simulation at 120 Hz, 262,144 samples per update, episode length 300. Pre-trained on AMASS with about 1 billion samples, then used to physically correct the video-derived kinematic motion; then fine-tuned per player on the corrected dataset with about 0.25 billion samples; a further ~1 billion samples to anneal away residual forces (reduced 1% about every 10 million samples; "critical to slowly decrease") — [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf)
- Motion embedding: conditional VAE fitted to the corrected motion; 1 conditioning frame, 1 predicted frame, sequences of length 10, trained in three modes (supervised, scheduled sampling, autoregressive) for 50/50/400 epochs; only 20% of data labelled with motion phase — [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf)
- High-level policy: same network as the low level; trained with 30,720 parallel environments in a three-stage curriculum (simulation frequency 120, 360, 360 Hz; 327,680 to 983,040 samples per update). Observes the predicted incoming ball trajectory from a precomputed ball-trajectory lookup table (launch height, velocity, spin sampled at 0.1 m, 0.1 m/s, 0.5 RPS), which is also used to estimate the outgoing bounce position for the reward — [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf)
- Code (low-level policy, motion embedding, high-level policy, Isaac Gym environment) is public, but trained models are withheld "due to license issues of the video footage" — [GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/vid2player3d) (search summary)

**DeepMind humanoid football (Liu et al., arXiv 2021 / Science Robotics 2022) - 2v2 team play**
- Three stages. (1) Low-level motor module (NPMP): imitates about 1 h 45 min of football mocap cut into 4-8 s clips; per-clip trackers distilled into one decoder that maps proprioception plus a 60-D latent "motor intention" to 56 joint-position targets. (2) Mid-level drills (follow, dribble, shoot, kick-to-target): experts trained by population-based RL, each distilled into a "drill prior". (3) Full 2v2: multi-agent RL regularised toward a mixture of the four drill priors plus shaped rewards; the player policy observes proprioception, ball, goal posts and partial views of other players and outputs the 60-D latent every 30 ms into the frozen decoder — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.12196)

**Google DeepMind robot table tennis (D'Ambrosio et al., 2024) - paddle on a robot arm, real opponents**
- 17 low-level controllers (4 serve, 13 rally; 11 forehand, 6 backhand). Each observes 8 timesteps of ball position/velocity (6), robot joint positions (8) and a style flag (2) and outputs 8 joint-velocity commands at 50 Hz; ~10k-parameter dilated CNN, ~3 ms on CPU — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- High-level controller runs once per opponent hit (~20 ms total): a forehand/backhand style policy, a spin classifier, per-skill "skill descriptors" (KD-trees keyed by incoming ball state storing each skill's landing rate, hit velocity, landing position; built from ~28k simulated ball states and updated with 91-257 real throws per skill), five hand-coded heuristics that shortlist candidates (e.g. fastest hitter, opponent's weaker side), and per-skill bandit preferences ("H-values") updated online from land/no-land outcomes that persist across games against the same opponent — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- Trained entirely in MuJoCo with Blackbox Gradient Sensing (an evolution-strategies method), chosen over PPO and SAC because it gave smoother actions; initial ball states sampled from real data. Total compute not reported — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)

**Physics-based table tennis character (Wang, Hodgins, Won, SIGGRAPH 2024)**
- Five skills (forehand drive/push, backhand drive/push, forehand smash). Per-skill imitation policy π(a | s, z) trained adversarially (ASE-style) outputs PD joint targets; a ball-control policy per skill maps (agent state, ball state, target landing location) to a latent; a mixer policy blends a universal policy and the skill policies with learned weights to switch skills smoothly — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- Strategy policy observes (agent state, opponent state, ball state) and outputs (skill index, target landing location); it is updated when the ball travels from opponent to agent, i.e. once per shot — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- Strategy is trained by iterative behaviour cloning with a CVAE: play many rallies, keep the winning ones (competition) or the ones the opponent returned (cooperation), re-fit. Opponents are fixed heuristics, not self-play: a random strategy and a "video strategy" built from about 20 minutes of broadcast video. Simulator Isaac Gym; Unity for a VR human opponent — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)

**LATENT (Zhang et al., March 2026) - humanoid robot tennis**
- About five hours of optical mocap from five amateur players in a 3 m x 5 m area, covering primitive skills (forehand, backhand, lateral shuffle, crossover step), not full matches, with no editing or annotation — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- Low level: motion tracker (teacher) distilled into a latent-conditioned student through a conditional variational information bottleneck with a learnable state-conditioned prior. High level: observes robot state, root pose and ball state; outputs latent actions as bounded residuals around the prior mean ("Latent Action Barrier") plus direct right-wrist corrections. Both run at 50 Hz; PPO in MuJoCo JAX on 8 GPUs — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- Simulated success rates over 10,000 trials: forehand 96.52%, backhand 82.10%, forecourt 86.35%, backcourt 89.80%. Plain PPO and MotionVAE baselines did not converge; AMP, ASE and PULSE scored lower — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)

**PingpongDuo (Jiang et al., October 2026) - humanoid table tennis doubles**
- Hierarchy: a team-level planner jointly selects positioning targets for both robots; individual footwork and striking skills guided by human motion references; a racket-arm residual policy refines the strike. Skills are "trained from states produced by each other" so transitions are fast — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20579)
- Team planner: constrained PPO; inputs are the strike command, both robots' base positions and the previously accepted target pair; outputs a lateral base target per robot. A learned risk estimator (MLP predicting hand/foot/base separation risk) filters proposals, with the current striker's target preferentially preserved. Footwork policy takes proprioception, a movement command and a motion-matched reference clip; striking policy takes proprioception, a whole-body reference and desired racket position/velocity/time-to-contact. Isaac Lab, 200 Hz physics, 50 Hz control — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20579)

### Inferences
- The command interface between levels falls into two families: (a) continuous latent of a motion prior, issued every control step (Vid2Player3D, DeepMind football, LATENT); (b) discrete skill plus a ball landing target, issued once per incoming ball (DeepMind table tennis, Wang et al. table tennis, PingpongDuo's strike command). Family (b) lines up directly with per-rally data that already records each hit, hitter position and bounce location: the reader's hit/bounce log is literally a dataset of high-level actions.
- All systems that learned from video-quality motion needed a physics-correction step or a wrist/arm correction head on top of the motion prior (Vid2Player3D's hybrid control, LATENT's wrist correction, PingpongDuo's arm residual). Paddle-face precision is the part that monocular reconstruction gets wrong, so expect to need the same.
- From background knowledge, not re-verified this pass: in Vid2Player3D the high-level policy outputs the motion-VAE latent plus corrections to the racket-arm wrist joints, and the paper reports about three hours of usable player motion and a 2-3 point-per-rally style evaluation. The main PDF exceeded the fetch size limit, so treat those specifics as unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Vid2Player3D main-paper numbers (hours of usable motion after filtering, hit rate, bounce-in rate, GPU count and wall-clock days) were not retrievable: the main PDF is over the 10 MB fetch limit and there is no arXiv HTML version. Only the supplement was read.
- DeepMind table tennis and PingpongDuo do not report training compute.
- No paper found with a four-body, physically simulated doubles racket game (two per side sharing a court with free choice of who hits). PingpongDuo is two robots on one side in strict alternation, with no learned opponent.

## 2. Self-play and multi-agent RL for competitive and team play

### Takeaway
Full population-based self-play with physically simulated bodies has been done at scale once (DeepMind 2v2 football, weeks on thousands of CPUs). Racket-sport work has largely avoided true self-play: agents are trained against ball launchers, fixed heuristic opponents, or a frozen copy, and "two-player rallies" are usually two copies of a single-player return policy.

### Cited Findings
- DeepMind football: population of 16 agents per experiment (three experiments); matches pair two agents sampled uniformly with replacement, each team is clones of one policy; fitness by Nash averaging; PBT evolves reward weights, discounts, learning rates and prior-regularisation weights. Drill experts: 5x10^9 environment steps per population. Full agent: 8x10^10 environment steps, about six weeks wall-clock, in two generations (second generation 4x10^10 steps regularised toward the best first-generation agent). Hardware: one 16-core TPU-v2 learner, 128 inference servers, 4,096 CPU actors. Performance was still improving at six weeks — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.12196)
- DeepMind football failure modes and caveats: early training shows uncoordinated ball-chasing and negative pass-value correlation; evaluation leans on 13 fixed evaluator agents and the authors note multi-agent strength is non-transitive, so no single number captures it — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.12196)
- SMPLOlympics (2024): tennis and table tennis are single-player and 1v1 only; 2v2 exists only for soccer and basketball, where the authors say their team-play reward "does not yield interesting behavior". Competitive self-play (alternating training with one side frozen) is used only for fencing and boxing with qualitative results; no tennis or table tennis self-play numbers. Each model trains in 1-3 days on a single RTX 3090, ~10^9 samples, Isaac Gym, policy at 30 Hz — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- SMPLOlympics failure modes: PPO without a motion prior gives unnatural tennis swings; AMP gets the most tennis hits (3.95 average) but 5.30 m landing error because "prolonged rallies increase discriminator rewards, leading AMP to ignore task rewards"; task-specific demonstrations are needed or the discriminator reward overpowers the task. Best table tennis baseline averages only 1.83 consecutive hits — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- Wang et al. table tennis: a plain RL strategy baseline "converges to fewer skills and a small table region, and overfits to specific opponents"; a single universal skill policy mode-collapses (uses forehand when asked for backhand, never smashes). Their iterative-BC strategy wins 0.687 of rallies against the random opponent versus 0.641 for RL, but in head-to-head matches against the RL strategy it wins only one of three reported pairings — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- LATENT: "self-play" is two instances of the same return policy on opposite sides in simulation, reaching up to 25 consecutive rallies; the authors list real multi-agent/adversarial training as future work — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- PingpongDuo uses no multi-agent RL or self-play: one planner outputs both robots' targets, skills are trained separately and mirrored. Learned positioning raised mean consecutive returns from 3.79 to 4.87 over rule-based targets; arm refinement raised return success from 44.2% to 55.6%; rule-based and heuristic control-barrier planners let the two robots collide in simulation — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20579)
- DeepMind table tennis reached its level without self-play: 7 rally and 2 serve train-in-sim / deploy / collect cycles over about 3 months with 50+ human opponents grew the ball-state dataset from ~2.6k to 14.2k rally states and 3.4k serve states — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- Recent humanoid boxing work: a 2026 hierarchical framework notes self-play "introduces severe stability problems" from non-stationary opponents and uses an AMP warm-up to solve the competitive cold start before fictitious self-play; the Spherical Latent Motion Prior paper uses alternating self-play (one learner, one frozen opponent) to stop both agents drifting at once — [arXiv 2601.22517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22517); [arXiv 2603.01294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01294) (both search summary)
- A 2022 badminton RL-environment abstract notes that doubles would need two agents controlling two players on the same side, which standard single-agent environments do not provide — [arXiv 2211.12234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.12234v1.pdf) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Compute spans roughly four orders of magnitude: about 10^9 samples and 1-3 days on one consumer GPU for a single-player return skill with a motion prior (SMPLOlympics, and the same order for Vid2Player3D's per-stage budgets), versus 8x10^10 steps and six weeks on 4,096 CPU actors for emergent 2v2 team play. GPU-parallel simulators have cut the hardware need since 2021, but no racket-sport paper has reported a population/league run, so the cost for doubles is unmeasured.
- The recurring failure modes are: (1) cold start, where neither side can return the ball so no point-level reward signal exists (fixed by first training against a ball launcher or with an imitation warm-up); (2) mode collapse onto one stroke or one court region; (3) overfitting to the current opponent (non-transitivity), fixed by populations or opponent pools; (4) style reward overpowering task reward under adversarial imitation; (5) teammate interference and collisions in shared space.
- For a twin whose goal is fidelity to real players rather than maximal strength, self-play is optional: the published "plays like the pros" systems (Vid2Player3D, SportsNGEN, RallyNet) get tactics from data, not from self-play.

### Gaps
- No published league or fictitious-self-play result for simulated tennis, badminton, table tennis or pickleball with full bodies was found. The football-style numbers are the only compute reference for team self-play and date from 2021 (pre-GPU-simulation; likely pessimistic today).
- No source quantifies how non-stationarity scales from 1v1 to 2v2 in a net sport.

## 3. Imitation from video or tracked match data

### Takeaway
Published systems have learned usable player-specific behaviour from modest data: about 13 broadcast matches for full-body tennis skills, about 43k strokes (75 matches) for badminton shot-and-movement imitation, about 9-10k Hawk-Eye shots for a three-player tennis placement model, and 20 minutes of video for a heuristic table tennis opponent. Offline imitation at the shot level (hierarchical BC, RallyNet) currently beats plain BC and offline IRL; adversarial imitation (AMP/ASE) is the standard for body motion style.

### Cited Findings
- Vid2Player3D: 13 US Open matches across three players; pipeline is video pose estimation, physics-based correction with an AMASS-pretrained tracker (~1 billion samples), conditional-VAE motion embedding, per-player fine-tuning (~0.25 billion samples). No stroke labels needed; only 20% of clips need motion-phase labels — [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf)
- Predecessor Vid2Player (2020, kinematic video sprites): Wimbledon footage including three matches among Federer, Nadal and Djokovic, with about 57.7 and 56.9 minutes of clips for Federer and Nadal respectively — [arXiv 2008.04524](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.04524v2) (search summary)
- RallyNet (Wang et al., arXiv 2024): hierarchical offline imitation of badminton players. Dataset 75 singles matches, 31 players, 180 sets, 4,325 rallies, 43,191 strokes. State: score, shuttle position, incoming shot type, both players' positions, opponent's moving vector. Action: landing position, shot type (12 classes), own position after the return; shot type by softmax, positions by mixtures of bivariate normals. A context selector generates the player's intent and the two players interact in a latent space under geometric Brownian motion — [arXiv 2403.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12406v2)
- RallyNet results (rollout from the initial state only; landing DTW / shot CTC / movement DTW): RallyNet 0.593 / 18.87 / 0.342; hierarchical BC 0.694 / 32.60 / 0.403; plain BC 0.960 / 63.60 / 0.483. Baselines also include IQ-Learn (offline IRL), ShuttleNet and DyMF. The paper stresses that one agent's errors compound across the rally in turn-based sports — [arXiv 2403.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12406v2)
- SportsNGEN (Hawk-Eye Innovations / Cambridge, 2024): a generic model is trained on professional tracking data at 25 Hz, then fine-tuned on matches containing a target player with a new learned identity vector; unseen players fall back to a "generic player" vector. Training takes about 2 days on one A100. The paper does not state match or shot counts — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- Fernando et al. (2019) semi-supervised GAN with per-player memory for tennis next-shot location: 8,780 Hawk-Eye shots from the 2012 Australian Open (Djokovic 3,410; Nadal 3,488; Federer 1,882); per-player memory cut mean location error from 1.09 m (global model) to 0.93 m — [arXiv 1901.05123](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.05123)
- Wang et al. table tennis: a "video strategy" opponent built from about 20 minutes of broadcast video; human-play data from VR sessions used to fine-tune the skill controller before human-agent matches — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- SMPLOlympics: video demonstrations are obtained with TRAM pose estimation refined by the PHC physics tracker and fed to AMP; with PHC refinement, table tennis hits improved from 0.91 to 1.83 — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- LATENT: five hours of unedited amateur mocap fragments suffice for robot tennis; the authors argue fragments are far easier to collect than full match sequences — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- PingpongDuo: about 200 footwork clips segmented from human doubles recordings — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20579)
- DeepMind football: 1 h 45 min of mocap for the low-level motor module — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.12196)

### Inferences
- Rough data ladder: tens of minutes of a player supports a crude shot-distribution opponent; about 2-5k shots per player supports a player-specific placement model; tens of thousands of strokes across about 30 players supports learned shot+movement policies; about a dozen broadcast matches (or 2-5 hours of mocap) supports a full-body motion prior for one sport.
- The proven recipe for a new player from new video is "generic model plus per-player fine-tune or identity embedding" (SportsNGEN identity vector, Vid2Player3D per-player fine-tune, ShuttleNet/RallyNet player embeddings, Fernando per-player memory). New match videos therefore add value incrementally without retraining from scratch.
- The reader's per-rally data (3D body, 3D ball, hits and bounces) is a superset of what RallyNet/ShuttleNet used (2D positions, shot type, landing) and comparable to what SportsNGEN used (centre-of-mass tracks), plus the body motion Vid2Player3D needed.

### Gaps
- No offline-RL (CQL-style, decision-transformer) result on logged racket-sport rallies with reported numbers was found; RallyNet cites Decision Transformer only as related work.
- No published learning curve of "accuracy versus hours of footage per player" apart from an unquantified plot in SportsNGEN.
- No published imitation learning from doubles match data in any racket sport was found.

## 4. Strategy-only simulation without bodies

### Takeaway
Shot-level and tracking-level generative models already answer tactical questions (rally outcome, best shot choice, counterfactuals, match win probability) at a cost of days on one GPU or less, with no body physics. Full-body physics is needed only when the question depends on whether a body can physically reach and execute a shot, or when the output must be animation.

### Cited Findings
- SportsNGEN: transformer decoder (4 layers, 2048-D, 8 heads) over object tokens (learned identity, position, velocity, distance to ball, elapsed time; players 2D, ball 3D) plus context tokens (surface, tournament, serve type). It predicts each object's next offset as a classification over a 61x61 (players) or 61x61x61 (ball) grid with nucleus sampling. With a shot classifier and rally start/end logic it simulates entire matches. About 2 days on one A100 — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- SportsNGEN counterfactuals: branching a real rally at a shot, cross-court corner shots gave the player about 58% win probability versus below 50% for the middle shot actually played; another experiment forces shot types and aggregates win percentage over 100 simulations per option — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- SportsNGEN limitations: poor out-of-distribution handling, unseen players default to generic, and (per the summary) no coupling between the current shot and previous shots so strategic play is limited — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- Match Point AI (2024): shot-level tennis match simulator with bot strategies derived from the Match Charting Project (2017-2023 shot-by-shot data); Monte Carlo Tree Search optimises shot direction and finds placement strategies resembling real tennis; authors call the experiments early — [arXiv 2408.05960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05960)
- ShuttleNet (AAAI 2022): given the first τ strokes (shot type, landing coordinates, hitter), autoregressively predicts the next strokes' type and landing area; 75 matches / 43,191 strokes; code public — [arXiv 2112.01044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01044)
- ShuttleSet22 / CoachAI Badminton Challenge 2023 (IJCAI): stroke forecasting track with ShuttleNet as baseline; 11 of 16 submitted models beat it; the winning team reported landing-spot prediction was hard to improve while shot type had room to improve — [arXiv 2306.15664](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.15664); [arXiv 2307.13715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13715) (search summary)
- Curriculum Dueling DDQN on a custom tennis strategy simulator (Dec 2025): single agent; notes reward design is the key challenge — [arXiv 2512.22186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22186v1) (search summary)
- Pickleball-specific: doubles games modelled as absorbing Markov chains with 4n^2+10 states for a game to n, parameterised only by each team's probability of winning a rally on serve; answers whether the first-serving team has a structural advantage — [arXiv 2303.02942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02942) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Three price points: (1) point-level Markov chain from a handful of rally-win probabilities, minutes on a laptop, answers scoring-format and win-probability questions; (2) shot-level model (ShuttleNet/RallyNet/Match Point AI style) from tens of thousands of labelled shots, answers "what does this player do next and how often does it win"; (3) tracking-level generator (SportsNGEN) from continuous player and ball tracks, answers spatial counterfactuals. All run comfortably on a single machine, including a 64 GB Mac for inference and probably for training at levels 1-2.
- Full-body physics adds value over these when (a) the counterfactual moves outside the data distribution (SportsNGEN's stated weakness), since physics constrains what is reachable; (b) the output must be watchable 3D motion; (c) body-level questions matter (reach, recovery time, kitchen-line footwork).
- A shot-level model also serves as the high-level policy for a later physical version, since its outputs (shot type, landing target, recovery position) match the command interface in section 1.

### Gaps
- No shot-by-shot generative or forecasting model for pickleball was found in the academic literature; the only formal pickleball model found is the point-level Markov chain.
- SportsNGEN's dataset size is undisclosed, so the data needed for a tracking-level generator is unknown.

## 5. Evaluation: showing an agent plays like a specific player or reproduces match statistics

### Takeaway
Papers use four kinds of evidence: distribution matching on physical quantities (shot speeds, contact heights) and aggregate match statistics; sequence distances between generated and real rallies; outcome-level checks (win-rate difference, win-probability calibration, rally-length distribution); and for embodied agents, task metrics plus matches against humans. No paper found reports a blinded human "which is the real player" test.

### Cited Findings
- SportsNGEN: for 3 male professionals, simulates 6 best-of-3 matches per pairing across hard, clay and grass; compares median, IQR and Wasserstein distance on toss contact height, first/second serve speed, return speed and groundstroke speed; absolute differences on first-serve %, double-fault %, first/second-serve win %, ace %, serve points won %; rally-length distribution (similar shape, slightly higher peak); coefficient of restitution by surface (correct ordering reproduced); win-probability calibration (good, deviating in sparse bins). No Turing-type test — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- RallyNet: Dynamic Time Warping distance on landing and movement position sequences, CTC loss on shot-type sequences, a rule-based-agent normalised score averaged over metrics, rally-length distributions compared by KDE and Jensen-Shannon divergence, and win-rate difference between simulated and real match-ups (mean 3.79% over eight opponents versus 10.95% for hierarchical BC and 13.93% for BC) — [arXiv 2403.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12406v2)
- ShuttleNet: cross-entropy on shot type, MSE/MAE on landing coordinates, but landing error is scored on the best of K=10 samples, which uses the ground truth to select and so is optimistic — [arXiv 2112.01044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01044)
- Wang et al. table tennis: motion quality by discriminator score, skill accuracy and diversity score (ours 5.72 / 0.76 / 8.01 versus ASE 1.62 / 0.38 / 6.13); task by average consecutive hits (10.93) and landing error (0.26 m); strategy by win rate and average rounds; human-agent VR matches (agent win rate 0.64 initially, 0.78 after refinement) — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- DeepMind football: Elo against 13 held-out evaluator agents (64 matches per checkpoint), behaviour statistics borrowed from sports analytics (pass frequency and range, division of labour, off-ball scoring opportunity), counterfactual policy divergence (how much moving the ball/teammate/opponent changes the action distribution), probe tasks and representation probes. No quantitative comparison with human match data — [arXiv 2105.12196](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.12196)
- DeepMind robot table tennis: 29 human opponents of rated skill; won 13/29 matches (45%), 100% versus beginners, 55% versus intermediates, 0% versus advanced; 46% of games and 49% of points — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- Fernando et al. tennis: mean Euclidean error of predicted next-shot location per player (0.79-1.14 m) and AUC for winner/error/return — [arXiv 1901.05123](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.05123)
- RallyTemPose: per-player next-stroke accuracy varies by more than 20 points across players; authors suggest predictability could indicate how well a player disguises strokes — [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/html/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible evaluation suite for a pickleball twin, assembled from these papers: (1) held-out rallies per player with likelihood/zone accuracy for next-shot prediction; (2) distributional distance (Wasserstein) on shot speed, contact height, landing maps; (3) aggregate stats from simulated matches versus real (serve/return win %, third-shot drop versus drive rate, rally length, error rates); (4) win-probability calibration of simulated match-ups against real results; (5) player-identifiability: can a classifier tell which player a simulated rally imitates.
- Held-out data should be a random sample of rallies/matches so that reported fidelity generalises; ShuttleNet and RallyNet instead split chronologically within each match (first 80% train), which mixes the same match across train and test.

### Gaps
- No paper found validates a full-body physically simulated agent against real per-player match statistics; Vid2Player3D's quantitative style evaluation could not be read this pass.
- No standard benchmark exists for "plays like player X"; each paper defines its own metrics.

## 6. Abstraction choices for a first version

### Takeaway
The literature supports starting simple: paddle-only or kinematic players are enough for tactics, and every full-body system required a large extra layer (motion prior, physics correction, wrist/arm residual) just to hit the ball reliably. Motion priors are necessary whenever a torque-controlled body is used; task reward alone gives unnatural or non-converging results.

### Cited Findings
- Tracking-level and shot-level models with no bodies reproduce serve speeds, rally lengths, win percentages and counterfactuals (SportsNGEN players are 2D points) — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- A robot-arm paddle with only 8 controlled joints and 17 small skill networks reached amateur human level; its remaining weaknesses were physical reach and spin reading (return rate about 50% with some underspin, near zero at heavy underspin; trouble with very fast, very low and near-net balls) — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- Full humanoid without a prior: PPO yields "inhuman swinging motion" in tennis (SMPLOlympics); plain PPO and MotionVAE did not converge at all on humanoid robot tennis (LATENT) — [HumanoidOlympics site](https://humanoidolympics.github.io) (search summary); [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686)
- With priors, full-body tennis remains weak on generic baselines: best SMPLOlympics tennis baseline averages 2.5-4 hits with 1.9-5.3 m landing error after 1-3 GPU-days — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)
- Wang et al. table tennis in VR simulates the human's paddle physically while the human body is not simulated, i.e. paddle-only control for one side and a full humanoid on the other in the same physics scene — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- Vid2Player (2020) achieved controllable, player-specific tennis behaviour with purely kinematic video sprites and learned shot/position behaviour models, before the physical version existed — [arXiv 2008.04524](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.04524v2) (search summary)
- Ball-physics simplifications are common even in top work: Wang et al. model air resistance as velocity damping with no Magnus effect; Vid2Player3D precomputes ball trajectories in a 2D plane as a lookup table; PingpongDuo does not model spin — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1); [Supplement PDF](https://cs.stanford.edu/~haotianz/vid2player3d/data/tennis_skills_supp.pdf); [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20579)
- PingpongDuo found a composed design (separate footwork and strike skills) returned more balls than a single joint policy even though the joint policy tracked motion references more accurately — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20579)

### Inferences
- A sensible staging, each step reusing the previous one's outputs: (v0) shot-level model on the hit/bounce log; (v1) kinematic players, positions driven by a learned movement model, body shown by replaying or retrieving reconstructed motion clips, paddle and ball in real physics; (v2) paddle pose controlled directly by a policy with reach and swing-speed limits derived from the 3D body data; (v3) torque-controlled humanoids with a motion prior trained on the reconstructed motion. Only v3 needs the 10^9-sample budgets in section 1.
- Compute fit for a 64 GB Mac mini: v0-v2 are small supervised or light RL problems. v3 as published depends on NVIDIA GPU simulators (Isaac Gym/Lab, 8k-30k parallel environments) or multi-GPU MuJoCo JAX; no source reports these workloads on Apple silicon, so v3 would likely need CPU MuJoCo at much lower parallelism or rented GPUs. This is an inference; no benchmark was found.
- Pickleball has properties that favour the simpler abstractions: a slow, high-drag ball and short swings mean the paddle state at contact captures most of what matters for the ball, whereas tennis-style full-body power generation matters less. This is reasoning, not a cited result.

### Gaps
- No paper found directly compares paddle-only versus full-body agents on the same task for tactical fidelity.
- No published kinematic-plus-physics-ball hybrid for doubles.

## 7. Applications built or proposed, and commercial products

### Takeaway
Counterfactual replay, shot recommendation, opponent/style analysis, coaching feedback, synthetic data and game/VR opponents have all been demonstrated in research. Commercially, pickleball video analytics exists (PB Vision and integrations, now tied into DUPR ratings), but no product found sells a simulated, playable or counterfactual "digital twin" of a pickleball match.

### Cited Findings
- Counterfactual replay and best-shot recommendation: SportsNGEN branches real rallies at a shot and estimates win probability for each alternative; authors pitch it for coaching decisions and for broadcast enhancement — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- Tactic search: Match Point AI uses MCTS over shot directions against data-derived opponents — [arXiv 2408.05960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05960)
- Opponent scouting / style: RallyNet simulates a player's landing distributions across states and decodes shot intent (e.g. a clear setting up a smash); RallyTemPose latent player embeddings allow play-style comparison between players — [arXiv 2403.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12406v2); [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/html/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.html)
- Online opponent adaptation: DeepMind's robot keeps per-opponent skill preferences and targets the opponent's weaker side — [arXiv 2408.03906](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03906v1)
- Game AI / VR sparring partner: Wang et al. let a human play their simulated agent in VR, in competitive or cooperative mode — [arXiv 2407.16210](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16210v1)
- Synthetic data: RallyTemPose authors propose generating skeleton sequences of predicted strokes as synthetic data for a field short of annotated datasets — [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/html/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.html)
- Real robots: LATENT (Unitree G1 tennis rallies with humans; real success 78-91% over 20 rallies per setting), HITTER (up to 106 consecutive table tennis shots against a human), PingpongDuo (two G1s, more than 10 alternating strikes) — [arXiv 2603.12686](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12686); [arXiv 2508.21043](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21043v2) (search summary); [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20579)
- Commercial pickleball: PB Vision analyses recorded matches for shot speed, placement and consistency; DUPR announced a partnership in January 2026 (DUPR stays the rating system, PB Vision supplies analytics), extended in April 2026 with Save My Play for video-based, AI-assisted ratings; PodPlay and Sporfie integrate PB Vision at club level; App Store pricing ranges from a $9.99 60-minute pack to a $399 premium tier — [DUPR Jan 2026](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-partners-with-pb-vision-to-enable-ai-powered-ratings); [DUPR Apr 2026](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-expands-ai-camera-integration-with-save-my-play-to-power-seamless-video-based-ratings-through-pb-vision); [PodPlay](https://podplay.app/blog/podplay-x-pb-vision-raising-the-ai-video-analysis-bar-for-pickleball-clubs); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6467020610) (all search summary)
- A patent titled "Systems, methods, and computer-readable storage media storing instructions for simulating pickleball" exists — [ipiq.io listing](https://ipiq.io/patents/7212144) (search result title only; contents not read)

### Inferences
- The open commercial gap is generative/counterfactual: existing pickleball products describe what happened; none found simulate what would have happened or let a user play against a model of a named opponent.
- Player-likeness raises licensing questions in practice: Vid2Player3D withheld trained models because of footage licences, which is a caution for distributing models trained on broadcast video of named pros.

### Gaps
- Engineering blogs from sports-analytics vendors (Hawk-Eye, Stats Perform, SwingVision, PB Vision) with technical detail on simulation or forecasting were not found or not read; PB Vision's doubles-specific modelling, if any, is undocumented in the sources seen.
- The pickleball simulation patent's claims, assignee and status were not checked.

## 8. Pickleball and other doubles paddle sports

### Takeaway
Pickleball is nearly untouched by this research: no RL, self-play, imitation or shot-forecasting paper was found, only a point-level Markov chain and informal shot statistics. Doubles in general is a data and modelling gap across racket sports; the closest technical precedent is PingpongDuo's team-positioning planner.

### Cited Findings
- Searches for pickleball reinforcement learning, simulation agents and doubles agents returned no pickleball-specific paper as of November 2026 — [search results, e.g. nearest hits arXiv 2512.22186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22186v1) and [arXiv 2211.12234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.12234v1.pdf)
- Pickleball doubles scoring modelled as an absorbing Markov chain (4n^2+10 states) to test first-server advantage; works at rally level, not shot level — [arXiv 2303.02942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02942) (search summary)
- Informal shot-level pickleball analyses: Pickleball Effect counted about 250 rallies from APP gold-medal matches and reported 45% rally wins after third-shot drops versus 53% after drives; The Dink reported similar win rates for drives and drops across DUPR tiers from the author's own recordings; a student project built a classifier on professional doubles shot data from pklmart.com (shot type, location, rally ID). None is peer reviewed and methods are thin — [Pickleball Effect](https://pickleballeffect.com/strategy/doubles-pickleball-third-shot-strategy-when-to-drive-and-when-to-drop/); [The Dink](https://thedinkpickleball.com/do-deep-returns-win-more-rallies-what-about-drives-vs-drops); [Hyun and Cooper project](https://calebhyun.github.io/pickleball-point-analysis) (all search summary)
- Badminton stroke datasets (ShuttleSet, ShuttleSet22) are singles only; a 2025 paper on transferring singles-trained recognition to doubles states prior research focused on singles because of data availability and multi-person tracking, and frames itself as a foundation for doubles datasets — [arXiv 2306.15664](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.15664); [arXiv 2508.13507](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2508.13507v1) (search summary)
- PingpongDuo is described as the first humanoid table tennis doubles system; its core problems are that fast repositioning hurts strike accuracy and uncoordinated teammates collide in a confined workspace; solved with a joint target planner, a learned collision-risk filter and striker priority, using footwork from human doubles play. Real result: 2.15 m clear-and-recover in 2.4 s, more than 10 alternating strikes — [arXiv 2610.20579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20579)
- SMPLOlympics offers no doubles racket environment; its 2v2 team-sport baselines "fall short" — [arXiv 2407.00187](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00187)

### Inferences
- Pickleball doubles differs from table tennis doubles in the way that matters for learning: either partner may hit any ball, so "who takes it" is a learned decision, not a rule. No published method covers that decision; the nearest are PingpongDuo's joint positioning with striker priority and DeepMind football's emergent division of labour.
- The reader's dataset (3D bodies, ball, hits, bounces for four players per rally) appears to be richer than any public doubles racket-sport dataset found in this pass, so even descriptive doubles models (who takes middle balls, partner spacing, kitchen-line arrival times) would be new.
- Pickleball-specific rules that a simulation and any learned policy must encode (two-bounce rule, non-volley zone, side-out scoring, serving below the waist) are not addressed in any learning paper found.

### Gaps
- No public, shot-level, tracked pickleball dataset with documented size and licence was confirmed; pklmart.com is mentioned by a student project but was not examined.
- No padel, platform tennis or beach tennis learning-agent papers were searched for specifically; this pass cannot say whether they exist.

## 9. Predicting where a player will hit the ball, as a probability distribution (added scope)

### Takeaway
Published placement predictors exist for tennis and badminton and mostly consume exactly what the reader has: incoming ball, both players' positions, hitter identity and rally history. The distribution is usually a single bivariate Gaussian (ShuttleNet), a mixture of bivariate Gaussians (RallyNet), a discretised grid with sampling (SportsNGEN) or GAN samples (Fernando et al.), but evaluation is overwhelmingly point error (metres, MSE) rather than likelihood or calibration. Evidence that pre-contact pose beats position-only inputs is thin: the one clean ablation found (badminton, 2D pose, shot type not location) shows player ground position matters more than skeleton keypoints, with pose adding a few accuracy points.

### Cited Findings

**Tennis**
- Wei et al. (DICTA 2013), "Predicting shot locations in tennis using spatiotemporal data": Dynamic Bayesian Network on Hawk-Eye data from the 2012 Australian Open men's draw; inputs are incoming-shot features (speed, location, angle) and player feet locations; adapts online to each player — [Record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4044096?lng=en) (search summary)
- Fernando et al. (2019), memory-augmented semi-supervised GAN: inputs are the Hawk-Eye ball trajectory with incident speed and angle, receiver's feet trajectory, opponent trajectory and ID, current score, a noise vector, and each player's shot history via per-player memory. Output is an image-like response map of the next shot with its landing point, plus winner/error/return classification. Data: about 10,000 shots total, 8,780 for the top three players, chronological 70/25/5 split per player — [arXiv 1901.05123](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.05123)
- Fernando et al. results: mean landing error 0.93 m (Djokovic 0.79, Nadal 0.87, Federer 1.14) versus 1.89 m for Wei et al., 2.49 m and 4.64 m for older baselines; 1.00 m on four additional players. Ablation: global model without player adaptation 1.09 m; generator without adversarial training 2.63 m. Shot-outcome AUC 82.65 / 88.33 / 89.01 (winner / error / return). No likelihood or calibration reported, and no sampled distribution shown — [arXiv 1901.05123](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.05123)
- SportsNGEN represents the ball's next step as a categorical distribution over a 61x61x61 grid of offsets, sampled with nucleus sampling; shot placement distributions emerge from rolling the model forward many times (e.g. 100 simulations per option). Evaluated on downstream statistics and win-probability calibration, not on per-shot placement likelihood — [arXiv 2403.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12977v3)
- Wei, Lucey et al., "The Thin Edge of the Wedge" (MIT Sloan): predicts shot outcomes and point-winning probability using player style and context priors, rather than placement — [Sloan page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/the-thin-edge-of-the-wedge-accurately-predicting-shot-outcomes-in-tennis-using-style-and-context-priors) (search summary)
- Shimizu et al. (2019): first study predicting tennis shot direction as three classes (right cross, left cross, straight) from the player's continuous position and posture up to the moment of contact; created its own dataset — [cited in Nokihara et al., VISAPP 2023](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2023/117858/117858.pdf); [DOI](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3347318.3355523)
- Hazarika (2026 preprint): classifies tennis shot direction from pre-contact MediaPipe pose; 3D world coordinates consistently beat image landmarks, with the largest gain (+47 percentage points) for direction under cross-player evaluation. Compares pose representations, not pose versus no pose; not peer reviewed — [arXiv 2606.15992](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15992) (search summary)

**Badminton**
- ShuttleNet: inputs per stroke are shot type (10 classes), landing coordinates and hitter; output landing area is a bivariate Gaussian (mean, standard deviation, correlation; 5 outputs) trained by negative log-likelihood, shot type by cross-entropy. Data 75 matches / 4,325 rallies / 43,191 strokes / 31 players — [arXiv 2112.01044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01044)
- ShuttleNet evaluation: MSE/MAE of landing coordinates on the best of 10 samples and cross-entropy for type. With 8 observed strokes: CE 1.980, MSE 1.586, MAE 1.380 versus best baseline 2.384 / 1.643 / 1.402. Removing both players' style contexts raises CE to 2.417 (about 22% worse) but MSE only to 1.653; i.e. player identity helps shot-type prediction a lot and landing prediction a little — [arXiv 2112.01044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01044)
- RallyNet: landing position and post-shot movement position are modelled as mixtures of bivariate normal distributions, conditioned on score, shuttle position, incoming shot type, both players' positions and the opponent's movement vector; evaluated by DTW of rolled-out sequences rather than likelihood — [arXiv 2403.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12406v2)
- CoachAI Badminton Challenge 2023 scored landing predictions as coordinates with MSE/MAE; the winning team found landing-spot prediction hard to improve over ShuttleNet — [arXiv 2307.13715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13715) (search summary)
- RallyTemPose (Ibh et al., CVPR Workshops 2024): inputs are 2D skeleton keypoints, players' court (ground) positions and player embeddings for earlier strokes; predicts the next stroke type (not landing location). Data: ShuttleSet subset with 42 matches, 26 players, 3,000+ rallies, 34,000+ strokes; BadmintonDB with 9 matches, 811 rallies, 9,671 strokes (two players). Metrics: accuracy, top-2, top-3. ShuttleSet: 54.3% / 77.3% / 92.5% versus transformer baseline 49.8% / 73.9% / 87.2%; BadmintonDB 62.8% / 83.5% / 93.1% — [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/papers/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)
- RallyTemPose ablation (keypoints / ground position / player embedding on or off): accuracies across the eight variants range from 46.9% to 54.3%. The authors state "the most critical factor is the inclusion of the player ground position" (removing it costs 2.6 points), player-specific embeddings do "not significantly boost" accuracy, and they speculate 3D skeletons might help more — [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/papers/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)
- Nokihara et al. (VISAPP 2023): predicts future shuttle trajectory from match video; adding both players' positions to shuttle position beat shuttle-only input on average and final displacement error; 26 training and 3 test match videos — [VISAPP 2023 paper](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2023/117858/117858.pdf)
- ShuttleNet's own related-work framing, as summarised in RallyTemPose: earlier forecasting "used prior stroke types and shuttle placement", and skeleton data "is not commonly used as a modality for in-action sequence forecasting" — [CVPRW 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/papers/Ibh_A_Stroke_of_Genius_Predicting_the_Next_Move_in_Badminton_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)

**Table tennis**
- Wu et al. (2019) and Wu and Koike (2020) predicted the landing point of a table tennis serve from the server's motion before the hit, obtained by pose estimation — [cited in Nokihara et al., VISAPP 2023](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2023/117858/117858.pdf)
- RacketVision (2025) is a multi-sport benchmark (badminton, tennis, table tennis) that includes ball trajectory forecasting tasks; no pose-versus-no-pose comparison was seen in the summary — [arXiv 2511.17045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17045v2) (search summary)

**Pickleball**
- No published shot-placement or landing-location prediction model for pickleball was found — [nearest results: informal third-shot statistics](https://pickleballeffect.com/strategy/doubles-pickleball-third-shot-strategy-when-to-drive-and-when-to-drop/)

### Inferences
- Input ranking suggested by the ablations seen: rally/stroke history and both players' positions carry most of the signal; hitter identity gives a moderate gain for placement (1.09 m to 0.93 m in tennis; small MSE change in badminton) and a large gain for shot type; 2D pre-contact pose gives a small gain for shot type in badminton. No source measured the gain from pose for landing location specifically, nor from 3D pose or paddle orientation, which is what the reader has.
- There are two distinct prediction problems with different information: (a) tactical forecast before or as the incoming ball arrives (positions, history, identity; this is what ShuttleNet/RallyNet/Fernando do), and (b) anticipation in the last few hundred milliseconds before contact from the hitter's body and paddle (Shimizu, Wu and Koike, Hazarika). Pose should matter mostly for (b). A pickleball model can condition on the time-to-contact to cover both.
- Distribution representations in use, with trade-offs: single bivariate Gaussian (ShuttleNet; cannot express "cross-court or down the line" bimodality); Gaussian mixture / mixture density network (RallyNet; multi-modal, gives exact log-likelihood); discretised grid or zones with softmax (SportsNGEN; multi-modal, easy calibration and top-k metrics, resolution-limited); implicit generative samples (GAN; multi-modal but no likelihood). For a requirement that several targets be plausible, a mixture density head or a court-grid heatmap are the two options with published precedent in racket sports. No diffusion-based landing predictor was found.
- Evaluation practice in the field is weak for distributions: ShuttleNet's best-of-10-samples error and the mean-Euclidean errors elsewhere reward sharp guesses rather than honest distributions. Proper scoring (held-out negative log-likelihood), calibration/reliability curves, and top-k zone accuracy (as RallyTemPose does for stroke type) would be an improvement on published practice rather than a replication of it. SportsNGEN's win-probability calibration plot is the only calibration evidence found.
- Data scale needed, from the precedents: roughly 2-3.5k shots per player supported a player-specific tennis placement model at about 0.8-1.1 m mean error; 34-43k strokes across 26-31 players supported the badminton models. Pickleball courts are far smaller than tennis courts, so a metre-scale error would be much less useful there; zone or heatmap accuracy relative to court size is the fairer comparison. This is reasoning, not a cited figure.

### Gaps
- No direct published measurement of whether pre-contact body pose or racket/paddle pose improves landing-location prediction over a position-only model. Shimizu et al. and Wu and Koike were seen only through a secondary citation; their accuracy numbers and baselines were not read.
- No paper found reports log-likelihood or calibration for per-shot landing distributions in any racket sport.
- No doubles placement model found (which opponent is targeted, middle versus line), and nothing for pickleball.
- Diffusion or flow-based placement models for racket sports: none found; cannot rule out very recent preprints.
- Wei et al. 2013 full text and its accuracy were not read; the 1.89 m figure is as reported by Fernando et al.
